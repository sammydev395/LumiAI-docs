1. Overview

The Lumi Wand is a self-contained RGBW LED light fixture gripped and aimed by the HiWonder Ultra 6 DOF robotic arm's stock 2-finger gripper. The arm tracks faces, gestures, and targets — the wand directs light accordingly.

Driver electronics are off-the-shelf: 12× KESHY 3W 5-35V LED Driver 700 mA modules (26 × 14 mm measured 2026-10-24; XL4001E-class buck, factory-set at 700 mA, active-HIGH PWM) + 1× PCA9685 16-channel I2C PWM breakout, all housed inside a 2-piece clamshell PETG handle. The KESHY's 7-pin right-angle header pins are clipped to ≤ 2.0 mm stubs and the leads solder to the stubs (operator 2026-11-05: no connector at the driver; the as-delivered pins hit the rail at 6 of 12 seats).

The only LED driver in this design is the KESHY-family 3W 700 mA module (§6.2 Variant B), at all 12 seats. The VEEKET / PT4115 boards on the shelf are not used in any build.

Key specs:

  • 3× independently controllable RGBW 10W LEDs (12 PWM channels)
  • Arm tether (v9.11, §10.5.2): 6 live nets on ONE 6-pin JST-XH in the END PLATE — pin 1 = 13.8 V, 2 = GND-power, 3 = SDA, 4 = SCL, 5 = 3V3, 6 = GND-logic; no spare. At the bay end the power and signal legs remain two separate runs that meet only at the wand's 6-pin.
  • Total weight ~310-335 g, total length ~145 mm
  • Powered 13.8 V direct from the PSC-100-12 PSU (§5.1)

3.2 Gripper — HiWonder BigClaw (replaces stock LX-15D gripper)

The gripper is the HiWonder BigClaw + HTS-25L bus servo (86 mm jaw opening), in place of the stock 2-finger gripper (~55 mm jaw, ~125 g).

✅ The jaw does not constrain the wand's diameter

The jaw closes on the ear, not the barrel. The ear is 25.4 mm tangential (EAR_TANG) with a 4 mm-deep jaw slot in each ±X face. The wand's outer diameter is bounded by the shell, the heatsink and the fan, not by the gripper.

Closing a linkage gripper to 16 mm of an 86 mm jaw would put it near the end of its stroke, where the toggle angle is worst and servo angular error maps to its largest linear error. The one-inch ear stops the jaws 9.4 mm earlier, in a better part of the arc, and the web between the two slot floors is 17.4 mm, so the tab is also much stiffer. The cradles are unchanged.

That is what makes a larger-diameter, higher-power variant physically possible (§5.4).

Design decisions:

  • Threads at both ends, and both caps are two parts: TOP M70×3 (head), BOTTOM M72×3 (end cap / flashlight extension). A turning ring clamps a captive plate at each end, because a one-piece threaded cap winds up whatever it holds. Tool-less, and the flashlight extension's retaining ring uses the same bottom thread (Lumi_Wand_Flashlight_Variant.md §3.5a).
  • Fan inside the cylinder: Noctua NF-A4x10 PWM 4-pin 12 V (40 × 40 × 10) at Z = 1.6..11.6, held captive by 4 forks at the ±X clamshell seam (each ±X face split into 2, offset ±5 mm off the seam, tangs 8 mm wide). No fan screws; it installs laterally as the halves close. The ±X position leaves the +Y gap free for the internal JST-XH cradle (§9.3a). The fan corners (r = 28.28) clear the Ø64 bore with no wall pockets (FAN_CORNER_POCKET_ANGLES = ()).
  • Tether inlet in the END PLATE: one 6-pin JST-XH. The inlet is the +Y 6-pin female cradle the shell already carries at r = 30, open top and bottom, with the tether's male pushing up through an opening in the end plate: no PCB, no bosses, no screws (§9.3b). Pinout: §10.5.2. JST-XH pitch is 2.50 mm against a 0.1″ (2.54 mm) board, which compounds to 0.24 mm over a 6-pin row.
  • Wand-local TMP102 bonded to the heatsink underside, on the same I²C bus as the PCA9685. It drives the closed-loop fan ramp in both SKUs; a bay-side sensor would be a proxy at best.
  • Mode detection is implicit: the ESP32 probes for a master on the I²C bus at boot; none present → the ESP32 becomes master and drives the PCA9685. No mode-detect pin.

Thread spec — single-start, TOP M70×3 / BOTTOM M72×3:

  • Pitch 3 mm, ~3.3 turns engagement (10 mm THREAD_LEN per cap)
  • External thread on cylinder; internal thread on each cap. TOP (head-cap) = M70×3; BOTTOM (end-cap/extension) = M72×3 (THREAD_MAJOR = 70 and THREAD_MAJOR_BOT = 72 in lumi_wand_cyl_v1.py)
  • Square-profile thread (1.4 mm radial depth, NOT the ISO 1.84 mm) — easier to print at 0.12 mm layer height with thread axis vertical; no steep ISO flank overhangs
  • Internal-thread crest/root: TOP INT_THREAD_CREST = 68.2 / root 71.0; BOTTOM INT_THREAD_CREST_BOT = 70.2 / root 73.0 (= minor + 2×THREAD_CLEAR 0.5, root = crest + 2×THREAD_DEPTH 1.4). These still set both skirts (HEAD_SKIRT_OD / END_SKIRT_OD).
  • The three RINGS are cut OPENER than that (operator 2026-10-11: the rings must thread fully by hand). RING_INT_CREST 68.7 / RING_INT_ROOT 71.7 (head), RING_INT_CREST_BOT 70.7 / RING_INT_ROOT_BOT 73.7 (end + retaining), with RING_FLANK_CLEAR 0.6 axial. Radial engagement is 0.65. The cylinder's external threads are unchanged by this.
  • The BOTTOM thread form is shared by the end-cap and the flashlight battery extension's retaining ring, so they are mechanically interchangeable on the wand bottom
  • CAD implementation note: the external thread cuts on the cylinder are applied AFTER the XZ-plane half-split inside make_shell_halves(), NOT before. Applying the helical sweep to the full circumferential shell breaks the subsequent split boolean because the helix crosses the seam multiple times per turn. Splitting first, then cutting threads on each half-shell separately, keeps each boolean op operating on simpler topology. See cad/freecad/lumi_wand_cyl_v1.py _thread_pipe_sweep(), make_external_thread_grooves(), make_internal_thread_grooves(), _try_threads() (with volume-safety check + Compound→Solid normalization).

Fan power + control:

  • 13.8 V reaches the fan from the rail 30's FAN 2-pin berg at col 14 (strips 5-6 = GND-power / 13.8 V), fed by that rail's own PWR IN pigtail from the end plate (layout).
  • PWM input driven from PCA9685 channel 13 in BOTH modes (channels 0-11 are LEDs, 4 free) — the PCA9685 generates the PWM locally at the wand; the tether only carries I²C commands ("set ch 13 to N%"), not the PWM waveform itself. In arm-tether mode the Pi5 issues those commands; in flashlight mode the ESP32 issues them. Identical fan-control path in both SKUs.
  • Tach output is not connected. It is reserved for startup-failure detection: on the flashlight SKU it would be an ESP32 GPIO (firmware + one crimp); the tether SKU would need a 7th conductor the 6-pin inlet does not have.

Thermal sensing — wand-local TMP102:

  • SparkFun TMP102 Qwiic breakout (in stock) — I²C digital temp sensor, 0.5°C accuracy, runs from 1.4-3.6 V (use 3.3 V from the PCA9685 logic rail), default I²C address 0x48. The wand unit is modded to 0x49 (the wand PCA9685 is 0x41, the bay TMP102 0x48) — full procedure in Soldering Guide §7; the annotated image above is the pad map.
  • Mount: bonded to the heatsink underside with MG Chemicals 8329TCM thermal epoxy (same compound already used to bond the 3 RGBW LED stars on the top face). The underside is below the LED heat-generating surface and reads true heatsink temperature unaffected by direct radiation from the LED dies.
  • Wiring: SDA/SCL tap directly off the PCA9685's I²C lines (parallel slave on the same bus). VCC = 3.3 V from the PCA9685 logic rail tap point. GND = wand ground.
  • Control loop (both SKUs): master polls TMP102 at ~1 Hz; below 45°C → fan PWM 20% (acoustic floor); 45-65°C → linear ramp from 20% → 100%; above 65°C → fan PWM 100% + log warning; above 80°C → master cuts LED PWM to 0 (thermal protection) and keeps fan at 100% until temp drops below 70°C (LED_RESTORE_C in firmware/wand_extension/src/config.h; the firmware is the authority).
  • Why not the bay TMP102 as a proxy: the bay sensor measures bay state, not wand state — produces false negatives (LEDs hot but bay idle → fan stays low → wand overheats) and false positives (bay running heavy workload but wand off → fan ramps for nothing). Bay-side stays doing its bay job.

Printable parts from lumi_wand_cyl_v1.py (exported as WandCyl_*): half_plus / half_minus (shell halves: PCA wedges, L-tabs, ±X fan forks, +Y JST-XH cradle + gripper-jaw ear, external threads), head_ring + lens_plate (head), endcap_plate + endcap_ring (end cap), retaining_ring (flashlight extension: Ø85 × 14.5 mm, M72×3, 7.5 mm thread, Ø77.6 flange-lip hole, 225° collar notch; Lumi_Wand_Flashlight_Variant.md §3.5a).

5. Power System

5.1 Power Chain

Split rails. The wand LED rail is fed directly from the PSU 13.8 V rail through the bay dual-bus distribution block (Power_Bay_Design.md §11.3); the DROK buck feeds only the STM32 board, at 7.5 V (operator 2026-11-02; the STM32 input is also the arm's servo-bus voltage). Reasons: the STM32 board is the power hub for the Pi5 / NVMe / OAK-D / mic / PIRs / wand 3.3 V logic, so keeping the wand off its DROK removes that contention, and a dedicated 13.8 V feed gives the drivers more Vf headroom with no buck feeding a buck. The wand draws ~2.4 A at 13.8 V (§5.3). Tether wiring: §10.5.2.

🎯 Distribution block: YRYAR HL-PBB-2-6-MINI dual-bus module, ferrule-based, no modification, on every build (operator, 2026-11-24): Ultra, Nova, Halo HV, and Halo when its bay is rebuilt. Halo v1 as built keeps its modified APIELE until then. Wiring + port map: Power_Bay_Design.md §11.3; part spec: Electronics_Assembly_Guide.md §1.4.

Constant input power within 2 % across a 12 % voltage change — the constant-current buck signature: output is regulated at 0.7 A × Vf, so input power is fixed and input current scales inversely with input voltage. Back-solves to Vf ≈ 3.1–3.3 V at 80–85 % efficiency. Output current on the same channel measured 0.7 A, confirming the sense network.

Twelve channels at 13.8 V: 2.4 A / 33 W. Red channels have a lower Vf and draw less, so 2.4 A is a conservative ceiling.

⚠️ Never size anything from 12 × 700 mA. That is the LED-side current; a buck's input current is below its output current by roughly Vout/Vin ÷ η. Size fuses, the BMS and cables from the measured 2.4 A.

The 5 A bench PSU can drive all 12 channels. The flashlight BMS is 5 A (both units physically checked 2026-09-03); ~2.9 A pack draw gives ~1.7× margin. Still owed: the all-12 total measured at once, as confirmation.

5.4 60 W variant — concept direction (2026-09-06)

⚠️ This is recorded design direction, NOT a build spec. Nothing here is dimensioned, sourced or costed. It exists so the decisions taken on 2026-09-06 are not lost.

Shape of it: a higher-output wand at ~60 W, larger diameter, no flashlight extension on this SKU.

Why it exists: ✅ commercial viability. A brighter spotlight is the version with a real market — 33 W is a capable demo, 60 W is a product. That is the driver behind the variant, not curiosity about headroom.

Power budget — it fits, but it is not free. The wand goes 2.4 A → ~4.35 A at 13.8 V, taking the bay peak from 8.0 A (Assembly §11.3) to ~9.9 A. That is past the 100 W PSU's 7.25 A nameplate and past its bench limit (holds ~7.7 A, ~104 W; cuts out at 8 A), so the PSC-100-12 cannot carry this variant, even as a peak at simultaneous wand-full-white and arm-heavy-load. The HV version's PSC-150-12 (150 W total, shared with charging) carries it only while the battery isn't charging hard: check the peak against the load-plus-charge total before this variant ships on either PSU.

Thermal is the real constraint, not amps. Driver dissipation in the handle roughly doubles, ~4.2 W → **~7.6 W**, on top of double the LED heat. Diameter growth buys heatsink area and admits a much larger fan — an NF-A6x25 or NF-A8x25 is a step change in airflow over the NF-A4x10, not a nudge.

✅ DECIDED 2026-09-06 — six 10 W stars at the existing 700 mA

24 channels · 4 rails · 2× PCA9685 · 24 KESHY drivers · 6 star positions. Same emitter, same driver, same 700 mA as the 33 W wand.

The deciding argument is inventory and production, not engineering effort. A three-star / 1.5 A design needs a second emitter SKU and a second driver SKU used by one product only. Six-star keeps one emitter and one driver line item across all three products — standard wand, standalone, and Pro — which means one qualification, one bin, one thermal characterisation and volume pricing on a single part. For a small operation that dominates the per-unit build effort.

It also makes the Pro additive rather than a redesign: a 60 W wand is more of the same board the v9.11 layout already defines.

📌 The emitter still has to change — and one part serves both SKUs

Six-star keeps the architecture, but it does not close the CRI gate, which is the biggest commercial blocker. That needs a specified emitter with a datasheet, whatever the star count.

Whatever emitter is chosen: check Vf, not watts

Most "20 W RGBW" parts sold on Amazon / eBay / AliExpress reach their wattage by stacking dies in series — low current, high voltage. A typical CHANZON 20 W RGB is 300-350 mA per colour at Red 12-14 V and Green/Blue 18-21 V.

An 18-21 V load cannot be driven by a buck from a 13.8 V rail. Specifying an emitter on its wattage badge alone would dead-end the power architecture and force a boost driver or a higher rail. Single-junction parts like the XM-L Color stay at 2.2-3.7 V across their whole range and are safe here.

Rails: still three, at 30/150/270°, and the rail layout carries over — one net per strip, zero track cuts, zero link wires, same end-plate star feeding all three. Six emitters means eight drivers per rail instead of four, so the rail grows along its length (a second 4-pin berg pair, ~4 more columns of the 31 an ST1 strip offers), not in count.

✅ 15° lens cups are a stocked drop-in — but read the caveat before ordering

The same 20 mm XPE-family collimator is made in 15 / 30 / 45 / 60°, black or white holder, water-clear or beaded. Sources: AliExpress 10-pack, Walmart US 10-pack (faster than AliExpress into the USA), LEDsupply Cree XPE optics. Pennies each.

⚠️ But our emitter is not an XPE. These collimators are designed around a single ~3.45 × 3.45 mm XP-E die at the focal point. Ours is a 5 × 5 mm four-die cluster with R, G, B and W at different physical positions. Two consequences:

  1. The real beam will be wider than the nominal 15° — a larger source under-fills the collimator's design assumption.
  2. Colour separation. Dies at different positions map to different exit angles, so a tight optic produces coloured fringing — a rainbow edge on the beam. This is the classic failure of putting narrow optics on a multi-die RGBW array, and 15° will be markedly worse than the 30° we run today.

✅ Order the BEADED version, not water-clear. Beaded and frosted are not the same surface (Beaded vs frosted); beaded is the one we want. The beads re-mix the dies at the cost of a little beam widening — the right trade here. If fringing is still objectionable, the proper fix is a TIR optic designed for multi-die RGBW — e.g. LEDiL's RGBX single-lens family (recorded in Assembly Guide §1.4, 2026-10-02; Carclo has equivalents) — not a flashlight collimator.

📐 Beaded vs frosted — they are NOT the same surface (2026-09-07)

Earlier notes here used the two words interchangeably. They are different optics and only one of them is right for this emitter.

Beaded Frosted / matte
What it is A moulded array of tiny convex lenslets on the exit face — vendors describe it as "spot-like protrusions, like small water droplets" A randomly roughened surface, moulded or etched
Mechanism Refraction. Each bead is a miniature lens Scattering. Random microfacets
Spread added Controlled — set by bead radius and pitch Broad and less predictable
Transmission High; little back-scatter (clear versions are quoted at 95 %) Lower — more light lost to back-scatter and wide-angle stray
Beam edge Stays defined Washes into a soft halo

(A third surface shows up while shopping: striped / ripple — parallel grooves that spread in one axis only, giving an oval beam. Useful for a wall-wash, not for this.)

✅ Beaded, because our problem is angular and we want to spend as little beam width as possible fixing it. The four dies produce four beams ~11° apart; a diffuser blends them by widening. Beaded widens by a controlled amount, frosted over-widens and takes the peak candela with it.

⚠️ Neither one cancels the tilt — this sets a floor on how narrow a colour-mixed beam can be. To blend an 11° separation you have to add at least 11° of spread, so a nominal 15° optic lands nearer 19–20° once it is mixing properly, with correspondingly lower peak. A genuinely narrow RGBW beam is not available from this emitter at any price. Narrow beams are only honest in white-only operation.

📌 Vendor language is unreliable. Cheap listings use "beaded", "frosted" and "diffused" interchangeably. Judge by the product photo: visible discrete dimples = beaded; uniform milky = frosted.

⬜ NEW consequence of the 5.7° number — clock all three stars identically

A die offset 1.2 mm from the optical axis does not just fringe the mixed beam; in white-only use it tilts that star's beam by 5.7° off the mechanical axis, in the direction the W die happens to face. The three stars therefore each throw their white beam 5.7° off-axis in whatever direction that star was rotated to.

⬜ So star clocking is now an OPTICAL requirement, not just a mechanical one. Each 1° of star rotation is ≈ 0.26 mm of tangential shift. If this holds, all three stars must be clocked to the same absolute orientation so their three beams tilt the same way and superimpose, instead of splaying up to 11.4° apart. ⬜ Confirm on the bench in E2 before treating it as a build rule — light one star at a time, white only, at 3 m, and mark where each lands.

⚠️ Bench report 2026-09-07 — the clear lens pops OUT of its black holder when the side tab is pressed

These cups are a two-part snap assembly: the conical PMMA lens clips into the black holder on small internal barbs, and the side tab is the release feature — flexing it springs the holder wall outward and lets the lens go. That is by design (it is how you swap lenses in a holder), but it means the joint holding our optic together opens under side pressure.

This changes the gasket design — the preload must not fight the snap.

Bears on Effect
Wrong the black holder's top face pushes the holder down while the lens stays put — works the snap open
✅ Right the clear lens's own top rim, inboard of the holder pushes the lens into the holder — seats the snap deeper

✅ And the tower must not squeeze the release tabs. LENS_RING_ID is a slip fit on the holder OD, so any print that comes out tight, or any out-of-round tower, grips exactly the feature designed to release the lens. Relieve the tower ID at the tab positions — local pockets ~0.8 mm deep — so nothing bears on them.

📌 Or delete the holder entirely. It exists so hobbyists can glue a lens to an arbitrary board; we have a printed tower that can be the holder. Capturing the bare conical lens directly removes the snap joint, removes the Ø22 → Ø25.1 tower growth, and returns the envelope to roughly today's. ⬜ Needs the bare-lens flange dimensions measured off a real part before it can be drawn.

🔴 Commercially this matters more than the rattle. A lens that can leave its holder under side load can migrate in a dropped or vibrated wand, directly above a 10 W emitter. Whatever retention is chosen, the lens-to-holder joint needs to be positively secured — a dab of high-temp silicone or UV adhesive at assembly is the cheap answer if the holder stays.

✅ The resolution is not a diameter — it is deleting the black holder, already flagged above. 📌 And the part should probably be a flat die-cut silicone washer, not an O-ring. Two commercial gates that are not about lumens at all:

  • CRI. Generic RGBW white dies typically land CRI 70-80. Retail and hospitality specs usually want 90+; broadcast wants TLCI 90+. This is plausibly a harder blocker than brightness and it is not fixed by more watts. ⬜ Unmeasured — needs a real part with a datasheet.
  • Flicker. PWM runs at 1 kHz (config.h PWM_FREQ_HZ), and the KESHY is a hard enable input, so dimming is pure on/off chopping at 700 mA. Fine for eyes; 1 kHz can band visibly against a rolling shutter at short exposures, which matters the moment "photography & video fill light" is a listed use case. The PCA9685 tops out near 1.6 kHz, so a meaningful fix means a different dimming scheme, not a config change. ⬜ Untested.

6.2a PWM Polarity — BENCH RESOLUTION (2026-09-01)

⚠️ The KESHY / AliExpress 3W drivers are ACTIVE-HIGH. Both vendor listings claim "High level turn off the output / Low level Open the output" — that claim is wrong.

What was measured (single KESHY module, bench PSU, LiLyGO T-Display-S3 as signal source):

Test Result Reads as
PWM pin floating, module powered 5.28 V, LED ON internal pull-up; floating = full brightness
3.28 V applied to PWM LED stays ON above the switching threshold (measured 2026-09-03: full current by 1.67 V)
PWM tied to VIN− (0 V) LED OFF below the switching threshold (measured 2026-09-03: zero current at 0.828 V)

The input is a hard enable, not an analog dimmer: full current at 1.67 V, zero at 0.828 V, no analog region (threshold box below).

Consequence 1 — INVRT=0.

With INVRT=1 on an active-HIGH driver, a commanded duty of 0 % drives the PCA output HIGH = full brightness. So the firmware sets INVRT=0, actively cleared and verified at init (a stale INVRT=1 surviving a warm reset would otherwise put every channel full-on).

Consequence 2 — ⚠️ floating = ON is a hardware safety problem. Firmware cannot fix it.

Whenever the PCA9685 is unpowered, held in reset, or has OE high, all 12 PWM lines float to 5.28 V and every channel goes full-on — ~33 W of LED with no thermal loop running. In the tethered SKU the 3.3 V logic and the 13.8 V LED rail are separate supplies, so one being up without the other is a real state, not a hypothetical. The controller is not executing at the moment this matters, so the fix must be passive.

BUILD VALUE: 1 kΩ from PWM (pin 5) to VIN− (pin 4) at every driver (12×) — settled by measurement 2026-09-03; see the threshold box below.

✅ THRESHOLDS MEASURED 2026-09-03 — the driver is an ENABLE input, not an analog dimmer

✅ PWM dimming is unaffected. The PCA dims in the time domain, switching the pin fully on and off at 1 kHz. A hard-enable input behaves identically to an analog one under PWM.

Value: 1 kΩ, against the measured thresholds:

One resistor per channel, 12 total. 2 kΩ (2.88 V driven) and 4.7 kΩ (3.06 V) are equally valid — once the real threshold is known the exact value barely matters. Use whatever is in hand.

⚠️ Do not re-derive R_int from the 13 µA short-circuit reading. It was taken at the bottom of a cheap DMM's current range and disagrees with the direct 15 mV-at-2 kΩ result (which implies ~7.5 µA). Measure V_off directly for whatever resistor you fit instead of modelling the pull-up.

⚠️ Fit at the DRIVER end. Two reasons, and the second is decisive:

  • A PWM wire or the §10.5.4 PCA cable coming loose also fails dark, which PCA-end placement would not cover.
  • PCA-end mounting is physically impractical (operator call 2026-09-01) — the PCA9685 is suspended mid-cylinder on the internal rails, so its channel headers are not reachable for soldering once installed.

The 12 KESHYs in hand already carry it: a 1 kΩ 0805 across header pads 4 (VIN−) – 5 (PWM), fitted 2026-09-07. Meter PWM↔︎VIN− = 1 kΩ on every board after its pins are clipped and its leads are on (§10.5.9 build card). A board without one: fit a 1/4 W carbon-film axial (CFR-25), any value 1 kΩ–10 kΩ, across the IN−/PWM corner pads, lying flat (body 6.3 × 2.4 mm, lead Ø 0.55 mm). No 1 W / 2 W metal-oxide parts (11–15 mm bodies).

  • 350 °C — ordinary through-holes, the header/small-joint temperature, not the MCPCB regime.

✅ The fan channel (CH13) needs no pull-down — a Noctua running full speed on controller failure is already the safe state.

Consequence 3 — the pull-down forces totem-pole drive, so OUTDRV=1.

Open-drain (the PCA sinks for off and releases for on, the driver's own pull-up doing the rest) cannot co-exist with a fail-safe pull-down. Work the divider both ways:

  • LED must reach full on (>1.67 V measured) → R_pd ≥ ~0.4 × R_int
  • LED must be dark with nothing driving (<0.83 V measured) → R_pd ≤ ~0.18 × R_int

No value satisfies both. Only one of the two mechanisms can be had, and the pull-down is the one that fails safe — so OUTDRV=1 (totem-pole), the chip default, asserted and readback-verified alongside INVRT.

The 5.28 V pull-up is a non-issue for totem-pole: driving 3.3 V against 406 kΩ injects 4.9 µA back into the PCA output. That also means no level shifter and no 5 V logic rail — §6.3's "3.3 V mandatory" stands.

6.3.0 VCC vs V+ vs OE — the three pins this board's labelling hides (written 2026-09-01)

The two 6-pin headers on the left and right edges are the same nets, mirrored — GND · OE · SCL · SDA · VCC · V+ on both sides, duplicated so boards can be daisy-chained. They are not two different interfaces.

Pin What it actually is This build

This is the one way to destroy the board instantly: if a clone wired that terminal to the logic rail and you fed it 12.8 V assuming it was the servo rail, the chip is gone. Ten seconds of meter time removes the risk entirely.

Consequence for this build: the screw terminal stays empty. VCC arrives on the side header instead, alongside SDA/SCL/GND — four wires in one connector, which is why the header is the right landing here and the terminal is not.

OE — Output Enable, active LOW.

  • OE LOW (or grounded) → outputs enabled. ⚠️ Tie it to GND — do not assume the board pulls it down. This line used to claim an on-board pull-down, which was asserted without verification. (It was investigated as a suspect in the 2026-09-05 all-channels-full-on fault and cleared: grounding OE changed nothing. Root cause was stale firmware holding INVRT=1 — see Bench Phase 1.)
  • OE HIGH → outputs disabled.

What "disabled" means is set by OUTNE[1:0] in MODE2. At reset those are 00, which drives all outputs LOW — which on our active-HIGH drivers is all LEDs off. That makes OE a hardware blackout that works without I²C and without firmware, as long as the PCA is powered.

⚠️ OUTNE[1:0] must be 00. Firmware forces it (2026-09-05) and fails loud otherwise. With OUTNE=01 and OUTDRV=1, a high OE drives every output HIGH at any commanded duty — all 12 channels full-on — while INVRT and OUTDRV both still read back correct, so a readback check on those two alone will not catch it.

✅ Wiring OE to a GPIO stays optional — the dangerous case is already covered. At power-on every duty register is zero, so outputs sit LOW and the LEDs are off; and the genuinely hazardous state — PCA unpowered while the 13.8 V driver rail is live — is what the 4.7 kΩ pull-downs handle (§6.2a). So: leave OE grounded (or trust the on-board pull-down) and treat routing it to a spare GPIO as a nice-to-have second kill path, not a requirement. If it ever is driven, OUTNE must stay 00 — which is already the reset default.

⚠️ Do not confuse OE with the pull-downs. OE protects against software failure while the board is alive. The pull-downs protect against the board being dead or unpowered. They cover different failures and neither substitutes for the other.

⚡ Why there are two grounds — GND-power and GND-logic

SDA, SCL and 3V3 need a return of their own. Brought home on the power leg's 22 AWG GND-power, which also carries the 2.4 A LED switching current, the wand's I²C reference would sit 2.4 A × 42 mΩ = 102 mV above the Pi's, against ~990 mV of low-level margin, carrying the drivers' switching content.

A second ground wire on its own is worse than none. Landing a GND-logic wire at G469 pin 9 (Pi ground) while GND-power lands at the HL-PBB (PSU negative), with the two tied inside the wand, parallels the return between two bay nodes that are already tied. LED current then splits by conductance:

over 0.8 m LED return through the Pi's ground
GND-power 22 AWG 42 mΩ —
+ GND-logic 22–24 AWG ≤ 67 mΩ ≥ 0.85 A — 35 %
+ GND-logic 26 AWG 107 mΩ 0.64 A — 27 %

The design: the two grounds are separate nets inside the wand (separate strips on every rail, §10.5.3), so the LED current has exactly one path home. They meet at a single star point: in the bay (GND-power → HL-PBB B bus, GND-logic → G469 pin 9) or at the pack in the flashlight SKU.

⚠️ The one consequence: the PCA9685 drives the drivers' PWM inputs across the split, so they see up to −102 mV of offset at full draw. Inside the bench-measured thresholds (0.828 V off / 1.67 V full, driven level 2.62 V) and inside a typical −0.3 V input abs-max. Same for the fan PWM on CH13.

⚠️ Meter GND-power ↔︎ GND-logic OPEN on every rail before it goes in. A short there means a parallel return, with nothing visibly wrong.

Single V+ / single GND-power pin on the JST-XH. The measured peak is ~2.4 A (§5.3), under the JST-XH conservative 3 A/pin rule (the XH datasheet rates 5 A/pin with 22–16 AWG). The 3.3 V net is ~10 mA, so one contact is plenty.

❓ How do you SEPARATE this joint? (operator question, 2026-10-02)

"A jst-xh 6-pin connector will not work here since how will we reach in to press the clip to separate the wand extension part from the wand female side — or are jst-xh connectors just plain force fits?"

The second reading is the right one: JST XH is a FRICTION LOCK. There is no release lever to press. JST's own product page for the series lists Lock: Friction lock, which is their term for retention by interference between the housing and the header shroud — as opposed to a cantilever latch you depress, which is what SM, Micro-Fit and XT use. The joint therefore comes apart the way it goes together: axially, as the retaining ring is unscrewed, with nothing to reach in and release.

⚠️ Source conflict, stated rather than smoothed over. JST say "friction lock"; Wikipedia's connector table marks XH Lock: Yes. Those are probably the same feature described at different resolutions — the XH housing does have a ridge that clicks into the shroud — but one of them implies a lever and the other does not.

✅ Settle it in five seconds with the parts in hand: look at an XHP-6 for a cantilever tab standing proud of one face, and pull a mated pair apart. XH is the connector on every R/C LiPo balance lead, and those are pulled straight off. If a lever turns out to be there, the fallback is already in this project's vocabulary — see below.

✅ Fallback if it does latch: mate the XHP-6 onto a BARE 0.1″ header (a JST-XH female on a bare 0.1″ pin is friction only, no latch; the TMP102 cable relies on it). Replacing the wand's pin header with a plain 6-pin 0.1″ male makes the joint friction-only by construction. Cost: XH is 2.50 mm and 0.1″ is 2.54, so the pins splay by 0.20 mm cumulatively over six positions (§4.4).

⚠️ What DOES deserve attention is the withdrawal force, not a clip. Every separation pulls the connector apart through its two mountings: the XHP-6 is bonded to the extension's board, and the wand's pinned header is soldered into a board screwed to the wand's ears. The extension's two M2 self-taps are only 2.5 mm deep — they and the bond are the weakest links, and they see that load on every pack change. ⬜ Pull-test the first assembled pair, especially now that engagement is 4.0 mm of a 7.0 mm body by the operator's choice rather than a datasheet's.

⚠️ A friction fit gives no audible click, and this mate is blind. Seating cannot be confirmed by feel — confirm it by continuity across all six nets after the ring is tight, before power.

⚠️ The extension side needs a board too

It has the identical cradle (make_jst_male() builds the same 4-walled pocket with the same 17.7 mm opening as make_jst_female()), and its half of the connector cannot take a wire except through a board. Both halves are therefore a connector on a 3 × 12 stripboard, screwed into the cradle.

Glue — what it does and where it is. Only the EXTENSION half is glued, and it is not a choice: one end of an XH pair is a crimp shell with nothing to solder to. ✅ Operator, 2026-10-03: "We can use glue, not an issue" — and, the same day, "Wand side should have strip board mounted jst", which put the shell on the extension and the soldered pin header on the wand.

✅ The glue carries little load. Two M2 screws into the cradle ears hold the board, so the bond only has to resist axial pull-out of the housing itself, with the pocket capturing it on all four sides.

What the glue actually grabs — the operator's question, 2026-10-03: "Not sure how gluing will work, only way is that part of the connector is glued into cradle." ✅ Part of the connector is indeed the only thing bonded anywhere in the joint — and as of the same day it is bonded to a BOARD, not into a cradle, and on the extension half, not the wand.

The XHP-6 shell body bottoms on its stripboard and is epoxied into the spigot pocket — a bead on the two printed end walls, which is the four-sided joint the design had all along. The walls capture its lower 2.5 mm, so the bond is backed by a mechanical pocket rather than being the whole of the retention.

✅ It does not work alone, and it is not asked to. Three things hold that shell, and the glue is only one:

resists
the spigot pocket, four walls round its lower 2.5 mm rotation and every lateral direction
epoxy on the two end walls axial pull-out upward, when the joint is separated
the six crimped tails passing through the board and soldered underneath the same direction — six joints in parallel with the bond
the board itself, screwed up into the spigot with 2× M2 the floor the shell bottoms on

⚠️ The load direction is worth being clear about: mating pushes the shell down onto its board, which carries it mechanically. It is un-mating that lifts it, and that is the only case the epoxy sees — in parallel with six solder joints and the pocket walls.

⚠️ The WAND half has no bond at all. Its header is through-hole in a board held by two M2 screws, which is the same retention the rails use.

⬜ This is the specific thing to pull-test first — the extension-side bond, not the screws.

  • Adhesive: two-part epoxy, not CA. JST-XH bodies are PA66 nylon (glass-filled), a low-surface-energy substrate that cyanoacrylate bonds to poorly and unpredictably.
  • A bead on the two END walls is enough. Those are the faces that resist the push-mate force. Do not butter the whole pocket.
  • 🔴 Keep glue off the mating face and out of the contacts. Epoxy wicked into a contact is an unrecoverable, unmeasurable open — and this joint is blind, so you will not find it until the wand does not come up.
  • Let it cure fully before mating. A push-mate against a green bond will lift the housing straight back out of the cradle.

Assembly order — both sides, non-negotiable:

  1. Cut the board to 3 × 12 and chamfer its two outer corners, on the bench, outside any printed shell.
  2. Build the board: the pin header (wand — posts up through the OUTER row from underneath, soldered on the board's top face, body hanging below) or the six crimped SXH tails (extension — down through the OUTER row, soldered underneath); the pigtails into the MIDDLE row (⚠️ solder to the pad and pin tail — see the box in §10.5.4.4).
  3. Tie each bundle off to a spare hole at the free end of the board, by leg — power is pins 1, 2, 6; signal is 3, 4, 5. This is the strain relief and it is the only one.
  4. Continuity-check end to end AND column to column, then drop the finished assembly into its cradle and screw it down — both halves, DOWN into the ears from the board face. Gently on the extension: those pilots are only 2.5 mm deep.
  5. Extension only: epoxy the shell to its board's top face. Cure. Only then mate the joint.

Step 1 is not a preference — you cannot chamfer a board that is already inside a shell, and without the chamfers it will not enter the Ø64 bore at all.

Three things that define this harness

  2. The PWM run is ONE conductor, never PWM + GND. ⚠️ A GND conductor with each PWM wire would join GND-logic (the PCA's GND) to GND-power (the driver's or fan's VIN−) at every driver and at the fan: 13 parallel returns, the exact failure the split ground exists to prevent (§10.5.2). The PWM inputs already work across the split: −102 mV worst case, inside the measured 0.828 V off / 1.67 V full thresholds.
  3. The logic pigtail goes to ONE rail (30°), not three. The only logic loads are the PCA and the TMP102, and both land on rail 30, so pigtails to rails 150 and 270 would carry nothing. That saves 8 wires in the cylinder and 6 cradle-board holes. Rails 150 and 270 keep their logic strips unpopulated. All three boards are still the same copper.

  3. Wire the 12–13.8 V bus to all 3 module VIN+ inputs, GND to all GNDs.
  4. Wire PCA9685 PWM CH0/CH1/CH2 to module #1/#2/#3 DIM/PWM inputs respectively.
  5. Wire LED star: R+/R- to module #1, G+/G- to module #2, B+/B- to module #3 (skip W for POC).
  6. Power up. Set PCA9685 to 1 kHz frequency, all channels off. Verify no current draw on bench supply.
  7. PWM polarity check:
    • KESHY / AliExpress 3W (active-HIGH): with INVRT=0, CH0=4095 → R LED ON, CH0=0 → R LED OFF. Do not flip INVRT — that inverts the sense and boots every channel full-on.
    • Confirm correct polarity behavior matches §6.2 expectations.
  8. Sweep duty cycles 0 → 4095 in steps, verify smooth dimming on each color channel.
  9. Measure module output current at full duty: should be 600-700 mA per channel.
  10. PWM-flicker check (optional): point an OAK-D Lite at the LED at 30 fps and confirm no rolling banding.

Pass criteria: all 3 channels light up, dim smoothly, hold target current, polarity verified.

POC pass → de-risks the full 12-module build entirely. Move to Step 1 with confidence.

Step 1: Variant-specific current setup (12 modules)

KESHY (production) — skip; ships factory-set at 700 mA. No current setup exists on any in-use driver.

Step 2: Module Bench Verification (12 modules)

  1. With a 12–13.8 V current-limited bench supply + 1× test LED + ammeter in series (10 A DC range), verify each of 12 modules outputs 600-700 mA at 100% PWM. Not 5 V — see the §7.3 headroom note; at 5 V a good module reads low and looks like a reject.
  2. Drive DIM with 3.3V from a PCA9685 channel at 50% PWM, confirm the LED is at half-brightness.
  3. Mark any out-of-spec modules and substitute from spares.

Step 3: LED Cluster (thermal-adhesive bond, gauged through the lens plate)

LED stars are bonded to the heatsink with a thermally conductive adhesive — no mechanical fastening. The bond is permanent and gives a better thermal interface than thermal paste alone. The heatsink stays unmodified at the LED positions, preserving the full thermal contact area under each LED die.

Step 4: Handle Assembly

One driver type, one shell pocket. There are NO STUDS, NO PER-PCB SCREWS and NO MID-DISC anywhere in this design. The 12 driver boards bond with 3M 4910 VHB tape; the PCA9685 is captured by 4 radial fork wedges at its top and bottom edges; the fan is held by side forks with no screws at all.

  1. 3D-print the 4 parts in their assigned materials per 3D_Printing_Guide.md row 2/3/3a:

    • 2× cylinder shell halves (PLA-HT, Bambu Lab) from lumi_wand_cyl_half_plus.stl and lumi_wand_cyl_half_minus.stl. PCA-support tabs and L-tabs are fused in by the macro; no separate parts.
    • 1× end plate + end ring (PLA-HT) from lumi_wand_cyl_endcap_plate.stl + lumi_wand_cyl_endcap_ring.stl.
    • 1× head ring + lens plate (NylonX, dried per §3.1 of the print guide) from lumi_wand_cyl_head_ring.stl + lumi_wand_cyl_lens_plate.stl.

  2. Cut 12× ~14 × 21 mm 3M 4910 VHB pads (one per driver pocket). Peel one liner and press tape to the 36 × 20 mm flat pocket floor (pocket is sunk 2.5 mm into the wall — board sits flush with the cylinder ID when bonded).

  3. Install drivers, 6 per half (12 total). For each pocket: peel the second VHB liner, lower the driver board (KESHY 3W — either fits) into the pocket with the inboard edge facing the cylinder centre, press 5 sec. Component side faces inward for wiring access. For KESHY: straighten the 7-pin right-angle header to vertical before bonding so pins point UP into the central cavity.

  4. Insert the PCA9685 vertically into the open cylinder interior of one half-shell (PCB plane parallel to the XZ split plane). The 4 radial support tabs (2 in each half at the PCA top and bottom edges) will pinch the PCB in X/Y when the halves come together. Top edge of the PCB sits against the heatsink underside once the heatsink is installed; bottom edge rests against the end-cap bottom plate. No adhesive.

  5. Fit the three rails and wire the drivers to them — full procedure in §10.5.8. Each rail mounts on its 2 posts at 30° / 150° / 270° with 2× M2×4 self-tap, and each driver's 2-pin power plug goes onto one column of the nearest rail's driver berg.

  6. Wire PCA9685 → driver DIM pins with 12× 3-pin servo-style pigtails (PWM + GND only; V+ unused). Route radially from the PCA9685 output headers to each driver's DIM/GND pin pair. With no mid-disc obstructing the centre any more, these short pigtails fan out cleanly.

  7. Wire driver LED-OUT → LED stars with the 24-wire bundle (12 pairs, 26 AWG silicone). Route up through the open cylinder interior (no mid-disc plane to cross), past the central PCA9685, into the 2 mm heatsink annulus, and out to the assigned LED pad on each star.

  8. Bring the tether in through the END PLATE. Its 6-pin JST-XH male push-fits UP through the plate's opening at +Y, r ≈ 30, into the cradle the wand already carries (13.8 V, GND-power, SDA, SCL, 3V3, GND-logic — §10.5.2). From the cradle, three pigtails run as a star, one straight up to each rail.

  9. Mount the fan — a 40 × 40 × 10 mm 4-pin PWM unit at Z = 1.6 .. 11.6 inside the cylinder, held captive by side forks at the ±X seam. No screws. Power comes from rail 30's FAN berg (col 14) at 13.8 V; PWM comes from PCA9685 ch13; TACH is clipped and insulated.

  10. Close the clamshell. Bring both shell halves together around the populated interior. The PCA fork wedges, the fan forks, L-tabs and gripper flats all key the halves' alignment. At the bottom, seat the END PLATE on its 3 keys at 30/150/270° and spin the END RING on (M72×3, hand-tight, tool-less). At the top, spin the HEAD RING on (M70×3) over the heatsink that carries the 3 LED stars from Step 3, then the LENS PLATE.

Fastener count: 6 — the M2 self-taps that hold the three rails to their posts, and nothing else. Caps thread, the fan and the PCA are fork-held, the heatsink sits on its L-tab shelves, and both ends of the wand are tool-less. Full list: §9.4.

Field replacement of a single driver: unbolt both caps, separate the halves, peel the failed board off its VHB pad (slip a thin spudger under the inboard short edge — less SMD density there), apply a fresh VHB pad, press in replacement. ~3 min per swap, no other boards disturbed.

Step 5: Final Assembly

  1. Drop the heatsink onto its 3 L-tab shelves at 30°/150°/270°; the milled slot in the puck rim keys onto the tab at 30°, which clocks it. No screws — the L-tab vertical legs constrain it radially and the head ring traps it axially.
  2. Place the lens cups onto the LED stars (one per LED) — see §9.1 for the two optic families.
  3. Spin the head ring on (M70×3) and fit the lens plate — the plate is keyed so it does not turn with the ring.
  4. Bench-test: power up at 13.8 V, i2cdetect -y 1 → 0x41 and 0x49, exercise all 12 channels and the ch13 fan.

Step 6: Gripper Integration

  1. Route the tether along the arm links with adhesive cable clips — two cables, run apart: the 3-cond power leg and the 3-cond signal leg.
  2. Slack loops at each joint.
  3. Test grip + full motion envelope. Verify no cable snagging, no slip, stable I2C.

17. Risks

  1. KESHY PWM pin floats HIGH = full brightness. The driver is active-HIGH and its PWM pin sits at 5.28 V on an internal pull-up, so any time the PCA9685 is unpowered, in reset, or has OE high, all 12 channels go full-on (~33 W with no thermal loop running). Firmware cannot fix this — it is not running at the moment it matters. Mitigations: (a) INVRT=0, cleared and verified at init, fail loud; (b) a hard pull-down resistor from each PWM line to IN− so the default state is dark — see §6.2a for sizing.
  2. Module batch variability. AliExpress KESHY modules show small batch-to-batch differences (inductor brand, cap rating). Mitigation: ordered 14 for 12-module build + 2 spares; eyeball first 3 under magnification before bulk processing.
  3. Clipping the header pins. Clip to ≤ 2.0 mm with flush cutters while holding the board, so the cut cannot lever the pads; the 0805 on pads 4–5 is not at risk because the cut is away from the board. Beep stub-to-stub afterwards and meter PWM↔︎VIN− = 1 kΩ (§10.5.9 build card).
  4. Vent slot debris ingress. Angled-down vents block falling dust but not directed airflow. Mitigation: vacuum cleanup during workshop testing; ceiling-mount production install is fine.
  5. PCA9685 at 3.3V driving DIM. Verified spec; 3.3V > 2.4V Vih min. Empirical confirmation on first bring-up.
  6. Cable connector durability. The arm tether terminates in one 6-pin JST-XH at the end plate. XH is cheap and not ideal for repeated arm flex. Consider Molex Micro-Fit / JST-SH if connector fatigue shows up. ⚡ The risk CONCENTRATED rather than halved: one connector now carries all six nets, so a single failed contact can drop the I²C reference (GND-logic, pin 6) as readily as the LED feed. It is also the SAME connector the wand↔︎extension joint uses, so a change here is a change in both SKUs.
  7. 24-wire bundle in the heatsink annulus. All 24 wires (12 LED+ / 12 LED−) route in the 5 mm radial gap between the Ø54 heatsink and the Ø64 cylinder ID — fanning around the ~200 mm circumference, ~2 % fill. Lots of room; the 5 mm annulus is the design invariant. History: v9 spec was 2 mm; bumped 2026-05-28 to 3 mm by shrinking heatsink Ø50→Ø48; bumped 2026-05-29 to 5 mm by the Ø60→Ø64 cyl OD enlargement; v9.8 (2026-09-17) grew both together (Ø48→Ø54 heatsink + Ø58→Ø64 cyl ID) to hold the 5 mm annulus after the CYL_OD 64→70 bump. No through-heatsink wire holes (was v7).
  8. Cable routing past arm joints. 60-80 cm tether needs slack loops at each joint. Bench-test full sweep before flight.
  9. EMI from driver-module buck switching (XL4001E-class KESHY). Modules are in the handle (not the base), so the SW node is short — only ~4 cm to the LED. The major EMI concern of the deprecated base-PCB design is gone.
  10. BigClaw + HTS-25L on the bus. Switching to a bus-servo gripper means the HTS-25L joins the same UART chain as the arm joints — assign a unique servo ID before plugging in (factory default is usually ID=1, may collide with shoulder). Mitigation: flash a unique ID via HiWonder's servo configuration tool (or our own ros2_control bus driver's set-id command) on a bench rig BEFORE connecting to the arm chain. Bus collisions can otherwise scramble multiple joints simultaneously. (Note: HTS-25L is the same servo family as the elbow joint, so existing arm-spare stock is the source — no separate purchase.)
  11. BigClaw jaw pad attachment. The BigClaw ships with bare aluminum jaw faces. The PETG handle has flat faces but no grip pads. Mitigation: cut 2× 86 × 10 × 3 mm strips of silicone or TPU foam tape and adhere to the inner jaw faces before first grab. Without pads, the wand can rock/slip in the jaw under arm dynamics.

20.1 Address mod — relocate ADDR from GND to V+ → 0x49

See Electronics_Soldering_Guide.md §7.1 for the full procedure (trace cut + V+ bridge technique, continuity verification before/after each step).

Quick reference: 2-step mod (not 1) — cut the ADD0 trace (ADDR→GND) AND solder-bridge ADDR centre pad → V+ rail-tie pad. TMP102's ADDR pin has no internal pull-up; cutting alone leaves it floating with undefined behavior. Bench-verify with i2cdetect -y 1 showing 0x49 AND bay TMP102 still at 0x48 before bonding to heatsink.

Safety: before bridging ADDR to V+, confirm the bay TMP102 is also wired and at 0x48 — so when you bench-verify, you can distinguish "wand TMP102 mod failed" (only 0x48 shows) from "wand TMP102 mod worked, bay TMP102 disconnected" (only 0x49 shows). Both should appear together once everything is right.

Quick reference: same technique as §6.1, with a single 4-pin male on the module's 0.1″ row. No ALERT line is used for the wand TMP102 — ch 13 PWM is the actuator, not an interrupt.

20.3 Thermal bond to heatsink underside

Use MG Chemicals 8329TCM 2-part thermal-conductive epoxy (~1 W/m·K). See §15.1 for the general bonding procedure; specifics for this bond:

  1. Surface prep: clean the heatsink underside (the flat surface at the underside of the CNC aluminum puck) with IPA. Lightly abrade with 400-grit paper if the surface is mirror-polished; epoxy bonds better to a slightly rough surface.
  2. TMP102 prep: identify the IC body (the small SMD chip on the front side of the board). The die is in the IC body. Bond the BACK of the board to the heatsink so the IC body faces away from the heatsink — the thermal path is heatsink → PCB → through-PCB-thermal-vias → IC body. (The SparkFun board has thermal vias under the IC for exactly this purpose.)
  3. Mix MG 8329TCM per datasheet (1 : 1 by volume; mix 60 s; pot life 45 min — see §15.1).
  4. Apply a thin layer (~0.5 mm) to the heatsink underside in a footprint matching the TMP102 board.
  5. Press the TMP102 BACK SIDE onto the epoxy. Some squeeze-out is fine. Wipe excess with IPA.
  6. Cure: 24 h at room temperature for full strength. Don't power up during cure (heat would set the epoxy unevenly).

20.4 4-pin cable, TMP102 → the TMP berg (col 13)

⚡ The GND conductor lands on GND-logic (strip 4), never GND-power (strip 5). They are adjacent strips; meter it.

21. PCA9685 (Wand) — Verify Defaults + Cable (bench)

Goal: put the SunFounder PCA9685 breakout at I²C address 0x41 — ONE pad mod: close A0 — and wire it into the wand. Full board reference + pad-mod recipe live in Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §6.3.1 + §6.3.2.

Procedure:

  1. Address pads — close A0, leave the rest OPEN: the 6 pads (A5 / A4 / A3 / A2 / A1 / A0+RW) are silk-marked "(Open=0/Closed=1)"; factory = all OPEN = 0x40. Solder-bridge pad A0 only → 0x41 (same bridge technique as the INA226 address pads, Soldering Guide §5.2). A5–A1 bridged → desolder. Verify with i2cdetect: 0x41 present, 0x40 absent (no HAT on the bench bus).

  2. Inspect pull-ups: with a multimeter, continuity-check SDA pad → VCC pad. Should read ~10 kΩ (Adafruit-pattern onboard pull-ups). Leave intact — the wand-end pull-ups are the second pair after TMP102's 4.7 kΩ at the bay end, per the EXTERNAL §2 pull-up reconciliation.

  3. Lead — soldered at the PCA, 4-pin Dupont at the rail (§10.5.4.3, §10.5.9 PCA). Trim the TOP-end 6-pin header, bridge OE→GND at the pads, and lap-solder four 26 AWG leads to GND, VCC, SDA, SCL:

    Conductor PCA pad Lands on the PCA 4-pin berg, col 11, rail 30°
    1 GND (+ OE) strip 4 — ⚡ GND-logic, not GND-power
    2 VCC strip 3 (3V3)
    3 SDA strip 1
    4 SCL strip 2

    ⚠️ Two silk orders are on record for this board (VCC/GND/SDA/SCL/OE/V+ and GND/OE/SCL/SDA/VCC/V+), read from different faces. Solder to the net, after metering your own board.

24. Wand Fan — Noctua NF-A4x10 PWM (40 × 40 × 10 mm) connector re-crimp (bench)

Goal: the stock Noctua 4-pin connector gets cut and re-terminated: fan power (2-pin Dupont) onto rail 30's FAN berg at col 14, fan PWM (1-pin Dupont) onto PCA9685 ch13's PWM pin. TACH is disconnected and insulated.

Per Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §10.5.5.1 and §6.4 (fan choice — ChaoPu ACP4010-12-PWM primary source / Noctua NF-A4x10 PWM premium alternate; wiring is identical for both).

Procedure:

  1. Cut the stock 4-pin connector off the Noctua, leaving ~5 cm of wire from the fan body.
  2. Separate the 4 wires.
  3. Fan power — 2-pin Dupont female for rail 30's FAN berg (col 14): yellow (V+) → strip 6 (13.8 V), black (GND) → strip 5 (GND-power).
  4. Fan PWM — 1-pin Dupont female, blue (PWM) ONLY, onto the PWM pin of PCA9685 ch13's 3-pin output row (§10.5.9 FAN-W). No GND conductor with it: the fan's ground is its power ground.
  5. Green (TACH): clip the wire flush with the fan body, then add a small piece of heat-shrink over the stub for insulation. Reserved for startup-failure detection in a future rev.

The fan-PWM end is a 1-pin Dupont on ch13's PWM pin; the fan-power end is a 2-pin Dupont female on rail 30's FAN berg at col 14 (strips 5-6).

Why TACH stays disconnected: closing the loop on TACH would let the fan node detect blocked-rotor or bearing failures — useful for production reliability, but not needed for the current dev unit. Add when the production reliability story is being formalized.

25. Verify Wand LED Rail Behavior at 13.8 V — bench

Before installing the wand into the arm, bench-test at 13.8 V:

  • Set PCA9685 ch 0-11 to 100 % duty (all 12 channels full white).
  • Multimeter in-line on the wand tether V+: current should read ~2.4 A at 13.8 V. (Integration §5.3, measured 2026-09-01)
  • LED brightness is set by each driver's sense network, not by Vin, so it should match the driver's 700 mA bench reading.
  • Driver-module temperature: Individual driver bodies should stabilize at ≤ 55 °C in ambient after ~5 min at 100 % duty. If any driver exceeds 65 °C, back off to 80 % duty until the cause is investigated.

If the bench-test passes, the wand is cleared for install into the arm.
