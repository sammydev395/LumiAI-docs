Stripboard cutting — hobby SCROLL SAW In hand — the operator's hobby scroll saw. Blade class: fine skip-tooth. The sanctioned way to cut any stripboard/perfboard TO SIZE (operator safety call 2026-10-06): both hands guide the work, the blade cannot skate, and the cut line is away from the fingers. Cut just outside a hole row, then file back to the holes. Know-how: FR4 is glass-epoxy and abrasive — it eats fine blades, expect to replace them (brown phenolic like the ST1 cuts easily by comparison); tape over the cut line cuts chipping and thin-board chatter; wear the dust mask — a scroll saw makes far more airborne dust than scoring did, and the garage fan recirculates it. Track mods are a different job and already solved — spot-face cutter or 3 mm drill twirled by hand; the v9.11 rails need no track cuts at all. Push-scoring a board to size with the X-Acto is banned; the scalpel stays for SMD trace cuts on flat boards only.

All bench tools are in inventory — the LumiAI electronics build is fully equipped at the bench.

Hot-air rework station: DECIDED NOT TO PURCHASE (2026-05-31). The total SMD lift/swap work across the whole build is:

  • INA226 R4/R5 lift × 2 resistors × 1 unit = 2 SMD ops (the "01C" 10 kΩ pull-up disable, §5.3)
  • TMP102 ADD0 cut + V+ bridge × 1 unit = 1 cut + 1 small solder bridge (§7.1) — not an SMD lift, doesn't need hot air.

Total: 2 SMD ops, never to be repeated at this scale. Iron+tweezers (Electronics_Soldering_Guide.md §4.2 lift / §4.3 place) handles both. A hot-air station ($100+) is not justified for a one-time work envelope. LED drivers run at their factory current set point (no sense-R swap). Iron-only is the only path, and that decision is final.

3. JST-XH 2.54 mm Crimping (the fleet-standard connector)

Goal: every in-house cable terminates in JST-XH at at least one end. The PEBA crimper handles all sizes from 28–22 AWG; the same dies cover all the housings we use (2 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 6-pin).

Procedure (one crimp):

  1. Strip wire ~3 mm. Don't twist the strands — solid-tin already.
  2. Place the crimp barrel in the PEBA jaw matching the wire AWG (smallest barrel for 28 AWG, largest for 22 AWG).
  3. Insert wire so the bare-conductor crimp wings sit at the wire-end and the insulation-grip wings sit on the insulated section. There must be ~0.5 mm of bare conductor visible past the crimp wings — if zero, the conductor isn't seated; if more than 1 mm, pull back.
  4. Squeeze the crimper fully closed. The detent releases.
  5. Tug-test: pull the wire firmly. The crimp must not slip. Reject if it does.
  6. Insert into housing until you feel the latch click (audible/tactile). Verify by tugging the wire — the latch should hold the crimp in place.

Gotchas:

  • A loose crimp passes the tug test once but fails after thermal cycling. If in doubt, re-crimp.
  • Housing latches: female crimps have a flat lance that the housing window catches; orient correctly or the crimp won't latch. Look at a good housing under a loupe before crimping a batch.
  • Mating direction: BnB-XH-A = top-mount male (PCB through-hole), SnB-XH-A = side-mount male, XHP-n = female housing with crimps. The wand uses both B/S (PCB-mount male sockets on the rails and on the joint boards) and XHP (female on cables). Live sizes: B3B / XHP-3 on every tether and inter-rail leg, B6B-XH-A / XHP-6 at the +Y joint, B2B/B4B on the fan and TMP102 taps.

Tip: crimp all the cables for one assembly step at once (a batch of 12 driver-power cables, say) rather than mixing crimping with soldering — the bench setup is different.

Rationale for cool-biased 2× RGBW + 1× RGBWW mix:

  • LumiAI MVP positioning is utility-first (garage, kitchen, factory task lighting per LumiAI Robotic Arm System.md §165/§171) — cool/daylight CCT is the dominant use case.
  • Single warm-white middle star gives software enough headroom to bias toward warm-ambient (restaurant/music-sync mode) without losing the cool peak brightness for task lighting.
  • Symmetric layout (cool-warm-cool, bottom-to-top) looks visually balanced when the wand rotates or tilts — no "warm side / cool side" bias.
  • Software effectively gets a 5-channel mixer (R / G / B / Cool-W / Warm-W) by treating the 3 white channels as 2× cool + 1× warm.

12.3 LED MCPCB end — solder

See Electronics_Soldering_Guide.md §10 for the full procedure (MCPCB high-thermal-mass solder technique).

Quick reference: aluminum MCPCB substrate sucks heat — chisel tip at 380–400 °C, preheat board to ~100 °C if available, tin pad first then place tinned wire, reflow both ~2 s. Watch for cold joints (gray, dull, frosty) — they're weak; reflow with more heat + flux. 8 pads per star (4 channels × 2 wires) × 3 stars = 24 wires total.

12.4 Heatsink bonding — MG 8329TCM thermal epoxy

After all 24 wires are soldered to the 3× LED stars but BEFORE the stars are bonded:

  1. Bench test: power up each star via its driver (bench supply current-limited to 700 mA per channel) → confirm all 3× LED stars × 4 channels light correctly. If any channel doesn't light, debug here (cold solder joint? swap wires? driver swap?). Far easier to fix before the stars are epoxied down.
  2. Surface prep: clean the heatsink LED-bonding face (the top face of the CNC aluminum puck) with IPA. Lightly abrade with 400-grit paper if mirror-polished.
  3. MCPCB prep: clean the back of each MCPCB with IPA.
  4. Mix MG 8329TCM per datasheet (1:1 by volume; mix 60 s; pot life 45 min — see §15.1. One batch covers all 3 stars and the §7.3 TMP102 bond; do them together).
  5. Apply a thin layer (~0.3–0.5 mm) to the heatsink at the LED-star footprint positions per Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §10.5.5.2 (LED1 at 90° + offset, LED2 at 210° + offset, LED3 at 330° + offset, all on a Ø30 BCD).
  6. Press each MCPCB onto its position. Some squeeze-out is fine. Wipe excess with IPA. Don't twist after placement — slide if needed for alignment, then settle.
  7. Cure: 24 h at room temperature for full strength. Don't power up during cure — heat from running LEDs would set the epoxy unevenly.

After cure: light cable management to bundle the 24 wires up through the cyl-to-heatsink 5 mm annulus per §10.5.5.2.

14a.2 The +Y joint connector — both halves

The wand↔︎extension joint is a 6-pin JST-XH, and the two halves are not built the same way. Both use the same board — 3 rows × 12 columns of STRIPBOARD, 6.85 × 30.48 mm (inner edge trimmed), strips across the rows so every column is one isolated net, screwed down with 2× M2 self-tap into the cradle's 3 mm ears, 22.86 mm apart. The extension's screws get only 2.0 mm of thread (in a 2.5 mm-tall spigot) against the wand's 5.0. Everything else differs: the wand half is a pin header soldered to the board, body hanging down into the pocket — no glue; the extension half is an XHP-6 crimp shell bottoming on its board and bonded into the proud spigot pocket that captures its lower 2.5 mm, its six tails passing down through the board. ✅ Operator, 2026-10-03: "Wand side should have strip board mounted jst." Full procedure: Soldering Guide §9.5.

The SCREWS are the depth gauge. There is no seating flange: the board is 30.48 mm long, sits on top of a 28.4 mm cradle on both halves, and two M2 screws set its depth.

⚠️ Break the two outer corners off both boards before fitting. A straight knife cut on each end — about 1 × 2 mm off each outer corner. The wand's bore is Ø64 and the board is flat; without the chamfers the corners stand 3 mm into the wall and the board will not go in.

Bench order, both sides — this order is forced, not preferred:

Step Why
1 Cut the board to 3 × 12 (scroll saw, just outside a hole row, file back — §1.1 tool row; no push-scoring) and chamfer its two outer corners It will not enter the wand's bore otherwise, and you cannot file it in situ

3 Solder the MIDDLE row (the outgoing pigtails) first on the wand half Both land on the board's top face there, and once the header body is seated the iron cannot reach the middle row cleanly past it. On the extension both land underneath, so the order is free

🔴 Neither board is optional. The wand's half is a PCB header whose contacts are 3.4 mm solid solder posts with no crimp barrel, so a wire cannot be attached to it except through a board; the extension's shell has no posts, so its tails have to land somewhere.

🔴 The extension-side shell cannot be soldered to a board. An XHP-6 is a crimp shell with no posts. It bottoms on the board, is bonded into the spigot pocket above it, and its six tails pass down through the board; the board is a wire-to-wire junction on that half. The wand half has no bond at all — its header is plain through-hole.

⚠️ This mate is blind and is not bench-testable — it first happens at week-4 integration, inside a closed shell. Continuity-check both halves end-to-end before either one goes into a cradle.

⬜ Pull-test the first assembled pair. Engagement is 4.0 mm of a 7.0 mm body — the operator's number, not a datasheet's; the XH's internal contact-engagement depth is not in hand. Withdrawal is resisted by the extension-side bond (§15.4) and the M2 screws on both halves.

15. Bonding Reference

Used in: TMP102 → heatsink (§7.3), LED MCPCB → heatsink (§12.4), VHB-mount LED drivers (wand doc §10.5), the EXTENSION-side JST-XH shell → its spigot pocket (§14a.2, §15.4) — the only connector bond.

15.1 Thermal epoxy — MG Chemicals 8329TCM

  • Conductivity: 1.36 W/m·K (low compared to thermal grease at ~5–10, but epoxy gives mechanical retention which grease can't — and the heatsink-to-air step at ~5 °C/W dominates total junction-to-ambient anyway, so the bond costs only ~2 °C at the junction).
  • Mix ratio: 1 : 1 by volume (A : B).
  • Mix time: 60 s. Don't whip air bubbles — mix slowly.
  • Pot life: 45 min after mixing — enough for a single-batch bond of 3 LED stars + the TMP102; see Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §13 Step 3).
  • Working time: 45 min of genuine positioning time. Place deliberately; don't rush the alignment.
  • Cure: 7 h minimum to handle / 24 h at room temp (22 °C) for full strength. 1 h at 65 °C for accelerated cure — but don't use a heat gun (uneven). A printer-bed oven works.
  • Don't power up during cure — uneven LED heat would set the epoxy unevenly + degrade thermal conductivity.

Bond surface prep (universal):

  1. Clean both surfaces with IPA.
  2. Light abrasion (400-grit paper) if mirror-polished.
  3. Dry fully before mixing epoxy.

Don't over-epoxy. A thin layer (0.3–0.5 mm) is enough. Excess epoxy is mostly a thermal barrier (the polymer matrix is not conductive — the conductivity comes from the suspended particles, which need to bridge gap continuously). More is NOT better.

15.2 VHB foam tape — 3M VHB 5952 (or equivalent)

  • Thickness: 0.8–1 mm (0.04″).
  • Bond strength: builds over 72 h at room temp; 50 % within 1 h.
  • Surface prep: IPA clean both surfaces. Pressure-press for 10 s (firm thumb pressure).
  • Use cases: LED-driver mounting, both variants (the modules have no mounting holes, so VHB is the only mount option; wand doc §9.6). PCA9685 fallback mount if M3 standoffs are unavailable.
  • Don't VHB-bond components that will see > 80 °C continuous — the foam softens. The PT4115 modules dissipate < 0.5 W each and stay well below this.

15.4 The extension-side connector shell into its spigot pocket — JST-XH at the +Y joint

What glues a connector housing into a printed pocket. Used in: §14a.2, the extension half of the wand↔︎extension joint.

⚠️ This section applies to the EXTENSION half only. One end of an XH pair is a crimp shell with no solder posts, so it has nothing to solder to and must be bonded. That end is the extension's (operator, 2026-10-03: "Wand side should have strip board mounted jst"); the wand's half is a pin header that solders to its stripboard and is never glued. ✅ Operator, 2026-10-03: "We can use glue, not an issue."

Two M2 self-taps hold the board, so the epoxy only resists axial pull-out of the housing.

Substrate: glass-filled PA66 nylon (the JST-XH body) against PETG or ABS (the printed cradle).

  • 🔴 Do NOT use CA / superglue. PA66 is a low-surface-energy plastic; cyanoacrylate gives a bond that looks fine on the bench and lets go under the push-mate load — and this joint is blind, so you learn about it at final assembly. CA also wicks, which is exactly the failure mode you cannot afford here (see below).
  • ✅ Use a two-part epoxy. The MG Chemicals 8329TCM already in inventory for §15.1 works — the thermal filler is irrelevant here but harmless, and its 45 min pot life is generous for a fiddly placement. A plain 5-minute structural epoxy is also fine; the load is small.
    • ⚠️ Before using 8329TCM near contacts, read the tube: it is a ceramic-filled, not silver-filled, epoxy and is not sold as an electrically conductive adhesive — but do not lean on that. Keep it out of the contacts regardless (next bullet).
  • The pocket is the retention; the glue only resists axial pull-out. The cradle already captures the body on all four sides — three printed 2.5 mm walls plus the shell wall as the fused fourth side. You are not structurally bonding anything, you are stopping it backing out.

Method:

  1. IPA-clean the cradle walls and the two end faces of the housing. Let it flash off.
  2. A thin bead on the two END walls only. Those are the faces that take the push-mate force. Do not butter the whole pocket — squeeze-out has to go somewhere and the only places left are the contacts and the mating face.
  3. Bond the shell into the spigot pocket, a bead on the two end walls, then screw its board up into the spigot from underneath (§14a.2) — the two M2 screws are the depth stop. There is no bearing flange; the board is 30.48 mm over a 28.4 mm cradle and sits on top of it.
  4. Cure fully before mating: 7 h to handle, 24 h at 22 °C for full strength (8329TCM figures, §15.1). Mating against a green bond lifts the housing out of the cradle.

🔴 Keep epoxy off the mating face and out of the contacts. Epoxy in a contact is an open circuit that is both unrecoverable and unmeasurable from outside — and on a blind joint you will not see it before it fails. If you get squeeze-out onto the mating face, wipe it with IPA immediately, while it is still wet.

⚠️ Not a field-serviceable joint. Once cured, removing a housing means destroying either the housing or the printed cradle. If the glued cradles prove unreliable in service, the documented fallback is a proto-board-soldered connector with the cradles reworked for a horizontal PCB mount — Flashlight Variant §3.9a.

  1. CV → 7.50 V (LV; never above 8.4 V) or 12.0 V (Halo HV). Seal the CV pot with varnish.

16f.5 Checks before power, and first power-up

F. Before any power (system off; Power_Bay §16.1 plus this build):

  1. Mains:
    • tug-test every mains termination;
    • C14 E ↔︎ PSU case metal < 0.1 Ω;
    • L ↔︎ N ↔︎ E open with the rocker OFF;

  2. No shorts:
    • HL-PBB A bus ↔︎ B bus: open;

    • INA226 shunts ~2 mΩ;

  1. AC only: DC rocker OFF, battery unmated, F1 fuse out, Deans unmated. PSU CH1 13.8 V.

  6. Pack onto its new pad / tray. Check the PSU-to-pack gap.
  7. Lower in the new unit (fence + bars + carrier) straight down onto the 6 posts, over the PSU and the pack. Feed the pack's lead up beside F1 as it goes down.
  8. The landings that could only be made on the robot:
    • land the pre-crimped mains L / N / ⏚ forks + ring at the PSU and CH1 at +V / −V (screw down only);
    • connect BATT #8 to the PSU's CH2 lead, and mate BATT #6 to the pack's lead (after the polarity check, step 9);
    • fit F1's fuse.
  9. Meter before power:
    • earth: C14 E ↔︎ PSU case < 0.1 Ω;
    • tug-test the mains;
    • BATT polarity end-to-end, then mate the pack.
  10. Through the opening and the deck back on:
    • BUCK-OUT up the opening to Halo's STM32-leg Deans (left unmated);
    • the new tether up the opening and along the arm's route, tied where the old one was, XHP-6 into the wand;
    • the new FFC's top end into the elec-bay breakout in place of the old one (blue stripe up);
    • the Mus-14 → HAT cable. Then lower the upper deck back onto the posts. Deans left unmated.
  11. Power up per §16f.5 (no IP6518 step):
    • CH1 13.8 → DC ON → bus 13.8, Mus-14 5 V, fans;
    • buck 7.50 V at the Deans → mate the Deans → STM32 → Pi;
    • battery: INA-BATT positive.
  12. Pi-side checks:
    • G469 3V3 / 5V;
    • i2cdetect 0x44 / 0x45 / 0x47 / 0x48 + 0x49 with the wand;
    • fans' PWM / tach;
    • each PIR toggles;
    • INA3 positive with the arm powered;
    • re-check HOME. Then BringUp Phases 3–6.
