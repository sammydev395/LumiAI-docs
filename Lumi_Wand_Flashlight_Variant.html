1. Overview

The Lumi Wand v9-FL is a standalone battery-powered flashlight version of the arm-mounted Lumi Wand. It shares the entire v9 upper assembly (cylinder body + head ring + lens plate + LEDs + heatsink + Noctua fan + PCA9685 + 12 LED drivers + the three ST1 power rails) with the arm-tether SKU — only the bottom modular piece differs.

The wand body is variant-agnostic — it terminates at the M88×3 external thread on its bottom OD (the Ø86 wand) and a 6-pin JST-XH female cradle at +Y, r ≈ 30, open top and bottom so a male plug push-fits UP into it through the end plate. It doesn't care whether the bay tether or the battery extension is mated. In arm-tether mode the M88×3 thread takes the end ring, which clamps the end plate over the cradle, and the bay tether's 6-pin plugs up through the plate's opening. In flashlight mode the retaining ring (M88×3 internal) threads onto the wand bottom and clamps the extension's Ø97.6 top flange (see §3.5a), while power + I²C cross the joint on the internal +Y board-to-board 6-pin (§3.9a) — the same six nets and the same connector, which is exactly why one cradle serves both SKUs.

Wand↔︎extension TOP joint: the M64×3/M66×3 TOP thread on the extension is gone (v9.5). The extension top is a Ø92 neck with a Ø86 socket (the Ø76 body flares into it at 50°) that takes the wand's end skirt, topped by a Ø97.6 × 3 flange clamped by a separate printed retaining ring that threads onto the wand bottom (M88×3, the Ø86 wand). The bottom (battery-end) joint is still a thread, M72×3, and takes the extension's own tail cap — end plate + end ring, the wand end cap's Ø70 design (2026-11-05).

Product name: Lumi Wand Anywhere — "Studio-grade color, anywhere. The light that goes where you do." (operator 2026-10-21).

1.1 Tripod / stand mount (2026-10-21, CAD dd1d6ab)

The wand + extension mounts on any standard camera tripod, light stand or 1/4"-20 bracket with the LCD facing UP.

  • Where: a solid block inside the −Y (rocker) extension half, between the cartridge stop ring and the rocker switch. The 1/4"-20 thread opens on a 16 × 14 mm flat facet on the −Y face, opposite the LCD. Both LCD variants (LilyGO / Waveshare) share the same −Y half.
  • Insert: HELIFOUNER 1/4"-20 screw-in insert nut, 10 mm (in stock, B08CHJ87VB), in a Ø8.7 through-pilot. After printing, drive it from outside with a 6 mm hex key until its flange seats on the facet. The flange stands 1.5 mm proud and is the bearing face for the tripod plate.
  • Balance: the assembled wand + extension + 4-cell cartridge is about 1.0 kg (CAD estimate: cell and electronics masses assumed). The CG sits at ext Z ≈ 129, about 13 mm head-side of the screw (bore centre ext Z 115.97) and ~2 mm off its vertical line, about 0.13 N·m on the tripod head. The block rides with the rocker: it ends 0.23 under the rocker's inside frame, so the screw can't sit closer to the CG; any normal ball/pan head holds it.
  • The 1/4"-20 insert in the ear is superseded for tripod use (operator 2026-10-21). The CG sits far from the ear, so a stand would topple, and the ear is on the LCD side, so the LCD would face down.
  • Build detail: 3D Printing Guide, extension notes; wall map: §3 −Y (270°) row.

Why this matters: same factory line, same upper-assembly inventory, two SKUs. The flashlight is purely an additive bottom-module — no compromise to the arm-tether design.

2. Locked Architecture Decisions (2026-05-27)

# Decision Rationale
1 Stepped silhouette: Ø70 head-cap → Ø64 wand → Ø70 extension → Ø72 tail cap Three step transitions create a "tactical-flashlight" grip pattern. The Ø70 extension belly accommodates 4× 26700 LiFePO4 cells (2×2, outer-edge reach 62.8 mm, needs Ø64 ID minimum). The extension TOP is a Ø74.1 flange (no thread — clamped by the retaining ring, §3.5a); the extension BOTTOM necks down to a Ø66 OD threaded peg (M66×3 external) that mates the tail cap. (v9.5: the bottom thread + end-cap were raised M64×3 → M66×3 so the retaining ring can wrap the Ø74.1 top flange and still thread on the wand bottom; the end-cap OD grows Ø70 → Ø72 to carry it.) 21700 LiFePO4 doesn't exist commercially (21700 is Li-ion-only).
1a ✅ Unified end cap — STILL UNIFIED, and now a PLATE + RING pair (v9.11, 2026-10-01). The wand end cap split in two; the flashlight tail uses the same two parts, with the 6-pin opening blanked on this SKU. Operator: "extension can use the same new endcap design, we'll cover the connector hole with something. I like the plate and ring design much better, much easier to screw on and off." One design, two roles, and a collar that turns without turning its contents.
2 TWO-STAGE BOARD PLAN (locked 2026-08-28). Bench / breadboard bring-up = LiLyGO T-Display-S3 NON-TOUCH (the touch LiLyGO is FAULTY — dead panel, runs hot — retired as a spare). Shell integration = Waveshare ESP32-S3-LCD-1.9 (With Touch), in hand in India — it is the board that goes into the printed extension (LCD_TYPE="waveshare", SCREW mount, §3.2.2). ⚠️ Gated on the firmware port — §4.2.1 and India plan §5.0a. Original decision: LilyGO T-Display-S3 Touch Edition or Waveshare ESP32-S3-LCD-1.9 (With Touch) as MCU + display — BOTH are supported in CAD (the CAD designs a +Y mount for each, selected by the LCD_TYPE switch: LilyGO = glue mount, Waveshare = screw mount). Waveshare ordered + received 2026-06-20. Functionally equivalent panels (both 1.9" 170×320 IPS + CST816 touch + ESP32-S3). The Waveshare (ESP32-S3R8, ST7789V2, USB-C, Wi-Fi 6 + BLE 5) adds a QMI8658 6-axis IMU, TF card slot, Pico-header compatibility. Pinouts differ (no STEMMA QT on Waveshare's I²C — need datasheet check); §4.2 pin assignments + §3.2.1 spec card are the LilyGO baseline and require re-mapping for the Waveshare. See §5.0 row 1 + §5.0.1 LCD parameters table. Pick either board at print time via LCD_TYPE.
3 LCD mounts side-wall flush, NOT tail-flush The user runs the LED wand from the side-mounted touch screen — flashlight is gripped with the screen facing forward and the rocker switch falling under the thumb on the back.
4 Side-wall LCD mount — display-specific (v9.6) LilyGO = GLUE mount: a 63 × 25 mm PCB through-slot in the +Y wall, flanked by a flat facet glue-seat (~4 mm deep, 3.5 mm wide each long side → slot 25 + 2×3.5 = 32 = glass width); the glass cover's 3.5 mm overhang glues to the flat seat (NO raised box, NO push-out window). Waveshare = SCREW mount: a 52.6 × 29 mm PCB through-slot with the glass overhanging it, panel held by 4× M2 brass standoffs into two internal straps (the raised bezel frame is gone). See §3.2.
5 4S LiFePO4 pack (4× 26700) 12.8 V nominal directly drives the Noctua fan + 12× KESHY 3W 700 mA drivers (PT4115/VEEKET NOT USED — see Integration §6.2) — both in spec. 26700 chosen over 18650 (and over the non-existent 21700 LiFePO4) for 2.2× capacity (4000 mAh vs 1800 mAh — 51 Wh vs 23 Wh) → 2.5 hr runtime at 25% duty vs 68 min on 18650. VariCore 26700 — $17.87 for 4 cells, 5000 cycles claimed. Drives the extension OD up to Ø70 (decision #1).
6 5 V rail buck Canonical 5 V rail = 12V/24V→5V 10 A buck (§5.0 rows 5/6, bench-verified); fixed-12V 8 A units are NOT USED (optional pre-regulator/spares). See §4.1.
7 NO mid-pack tap for ESP32 power Asymmetric cell discharge destroys series LiFePO4 packs in ~50 cycles + bypasses BMS overcurrent protection. Always buck off full pack.
8 Illuminated SPST rocker switch — delivered part (2026-08-21): R&D 3-pin KCD1, LED-illuminated (lights at 12 V) NOT a momentary push button — push variants were too axially deep for this form factor. Same rocker family the user uses 2× of on the LumiAI power bay (super compact, fully researched). Specs lock 2026-05-28; the delivered R&D 3-pin KCD1 is THE build part (KCD1-104/106 remain the compatible-family cutout reference — §3.3).
9 Rocker switch on side OPPOSITE the LCD facet Thumb-accessible on the back of the grip when user holds the flashlight with LCD facing them. Standard tactical-light placement.
10 ESP32 boot-time mode detection via I²C bus probe No dedicated MODE_DET pin. ESP32 probes for an existing I²C master at boot → none present → becomes the master and drives the PCA9685. Superseded: implemented firmware boots as I²C master unconditionally (§4.3). Keeps the JST-XH at 7-pin.
11 Thermal sensing via SparkFun TMP102 bonded to the heatsink underside I²C slave (0x49 — operator-modded; ships 0x48, mod per Soldering Guide §7) on the same SDA/SCL bus as the PCA9685 (0x40). ESP32 polls it at ~1 Hz, drives Noctua PWM via PCA9685 ch 13. Identical control loop to the arm-tether SKU's Pi5 → TMP102 → PCA9685 path. User has the TMP102 in stock. See Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §4.4 for the full thermal-loop spec.

3. Mechanical Design

3.1 Battery Extension Cylinder

⚠️ v9.8 (2026-09-17) — EVERY DIAMETER HERE GREW BY 6 mm

Four Ø26 cells did not physically fit the Ø64 bore. A 2×2 pack needs Ø62.77 and Ø64 left 0.62 mm per side — no room for holder walls or the Keystone contact plates. The extension is now Ø76 OD / Ø70 ID, and the wand body went with it (Ø64 → Ø70) since everything is being re-printed anyway.

The wand bump also closed a separate blocker for free: a 3-pin JST-XH could not pass through the wand sidewall between two driver boards. ℹ️ v9.11 made that blocker moot by deleting the sidewall connectors entirely — the wand's only inlet is the 6-pin in the end plate (§9.3b). The Ø70 bump stands on its own merits; it is no longer load-bearing for the connector.

The retaining-ring chain is now derived from the body OD rather than hardcoded, and fit_checks_wand_flashlight.py asserts both macros agree. All 16 layer-1 checks pass.

What it costs

  • Runtime: ~84 → ~67 min full white (46 → 37 Wh usable), ~3.1 → ~2.5 h typical. A 20 % cut.
  • Contact resistance. 4S is 8 contacts in series. At the measured 2.6 A pack draw: 0.10 V / 0.27 W at 5 mΩ per contact, 0.42 V / 1.1 W at 20 mΩ — and contact resistance rises with oxidation and vibration over the product's life. Specify plated contacts and measure a real holder before committing.
  • Intermittency. A knock can momentarily open a spring contact → ESP32 brownout. Fit a bulk capacitor across the pack input if this goes ahead.
  • 🔴 Safety — the one that actually matters commercially. User-replaceable cells mean a user can fit 26650 Li-ion (3.7 V) in place of LiFePO4 (3.2 V). 4S then sits at 14.8 V nominal / 16.8 V charged into a design and a LiFePO4 BMS built for 12.8 V / 14.6 V, with cell cutoffs that are wrong for that chemistry. 26650 Li-ion is widely sold and someone will do it. A sealed pack makes this impossible; a holder invites it. Mitigate with mechanical keying, unmissable labelling, and a BMS spec'd to fail safe — or accept it, as plenty of products shipping user-swappable 18650s do.

✅ What holds the cells — the CELL SPACER, decided and built 2026-09-20

The finding above exposed that nothing in the cartridge located a cell laterally. The old design used the 2×2 holder rings; the cartridge deleted them and inherited "located by the bore", which the v9.8 bump then loosened 6×. Three things had to be true of the fix, and two of them killed the obvious answers:

1. No wall can go between two cells. Adjacent cell centres are 26 mm apart and the cells are Ø26 — they touch. The gap is 0.00 mm. No per-cell collar of any thickness fits, at any print setting, ever. Material can only go outside the 2×2 envelope.

2. No central collar is needed either. The first sketch had one around the tube. Four cells on a touching 26 mm pitch are mutually jammed: no single cell can move inward without pushing two neighbours outward, and the group cannot shrink. The only free motion is the whole pack sliding in the bore — which is what an outer wall stops. The collar was redundant, and it turned out the raw boolean puts material at r = 3.27, inside the Ø8 tube.

3. It is a separate part — but SCREWED to its cap, which turned out to be essential. A 6 mm wall standing off a cap's inboard face would sit on the bed, and the caps print flat on one face (since 2026-10-07 outboard face down, pockets up — flat Ø66 × 3.3 discs, no boss). 🔎 Both caps in plan — every contact, link and tap in place

The section views above show how one contact mounts; this shows where all eight go and what joins them. Two things it settles that a section cannot:

⚠️ The two views are mirrored. The base cap is drawn from BELOW, because that is the face its links are soldered on — so its X axis is flipped. C1 is upper-LEFT on the top cap and upper-RIGHT on the base cap. Read the cell labels, never the corner. Getting it backwards swaps link A with link C and lands the balance taps on the wrong cells — a BMS fault rather than a dead short, so it will not announce itself.

⚠️ Every contact on one cap is the same part, and the polarity marks do not choose it. Keystone's names — 5222 "negative spring", 5221 "positive button" — are AA-alkaline conventions for a raised + nub. Our 26700s are flat-top at both ends, so either contact meets either face. The split here is purely mechanical: all four springs on one cap because the spring absorbs cell-length tolerance, all four buttons on the other because the button is the rigid datum. The top cap therefore carries a "negative spring" on C1, which is the pack positive terminal — and that is correct.

📌 Tap each balance point at the MIDPOINT of its link, not at a plate — one joint instead of two, and it can never be mistaken for a pack terminal.

⚠️ Keep the base cap's links OUT of the hex nut's sweep (2026-10-07). The M8 nut turns on the outboard face over the centre (13 AF, corners at r 7.5). Rule: nothing at r < 9.5 — Electronics' measured 13 mm-socket sweep (2026-10-09) — links A and C, taps B1/B3, service loops, RTV tacks and any wire slack (Soldering §16.5). Since the wire slots moved to the pocket centres (928dfd4, 2026-10-09) a link run between two adjacent slots lies at r ≥ 13 (slot centres at (±13, ±13)), so it clears the rule without an arc; take the tap at the middle of the link.

✅ Pocket clocking per position — no longer needed (2026-10-09). The eyelet still sits 1.4 mm from one edge and 1.2 mm from the other; check a real part for fit, nothing more.

✅ Both caps are now the SAME geometry — the sprung plate went INBOARD (2026-10-06)

Both caps carry their plate in a 0.9 mm pocket on the INBOARD face, held by two M1.2 × 2.5 screws, with the lead coming up a 5.0 × 4.0 wire slot through the pocket centre (row 4 above). The sprung cap is the same part, flipped into the assembly. (Two STLs are still exported, under their old names.)

Why: the spring pushes the plate and the cell apart. On the outboard face that reaction pulled the plate off the cap, so its two screws carried the whole spring load in pull-out — and their pilots sat 0.05 mm from the Ø9 spring bore, so an M1.2 thread broke into it. Inboard, the spring presses the plate into its pocket, exactly as the cell presses the 5221 button into the rigid cap, and the screws only locate. The Ø9 bore is gone.

Clearances (checked in the fit gate): the M1.2 heads now sit beside the spring's base coil. The operator measured the 5222's widest coil at Ø7.25, against a head inner edge at r 4.05, which leaves 0.43 mm. The heads stand ~0.7 proud, inside the spring's working height.

Cost: the sprung cap's 2.31 mm of plate-to-face material now sits inside the cartridge, which goes 81.11 → 83.42 mm. The battery zone goes 82 → 84.5, so the extension grows by the same 2.5 mm. The spacer goes 6.0 → 8.5 tall so it still grips the cell tops (3.69 mm), which now end 4.81 below the sprung cap instead of 2.80.

✅ 18650 contacts DO suit a 26700 — confirmed

What changes with cell size in Keystone's catalogue is the compartment length "L" — and L is our printed part. (For comparison, the C/26650 pair 5240/5241 costs 13.0 mm against the 18650 pair's 6.6 mm, because it is built for a deeper commercial case. ⚠️ The real cost of springs is resistance, not length

Published measurements of 18650-holder spring contacts run 30–100 mΩ each. Four in series at our 2.6 A pack current is 0.3–1.0 V and 0.8–2.7 W — up to ~8 % of a 12.8 V pack, dissipated as heat inside the tube. A soldered nickel-strip pack has none of this. This is the actual price of user-replaceable cells, and it is worth more attention than the 6 mm of length.

The standard fix is a spring bypass: a loose strand of fine tinned copper soldered from the spring's base coil to its tip coil, deliberately longer than the spring so it never fights the travel. It solders because the spring is nickel plated — the same reason nickel strip wets in a second on this bench while the cell caps refused to wet at all.

⬜ Measure a real 5222 four-wire at 2.6 A before committing. It is one number and it decides whether the bypass is optional or mandatory.

📌 The links are now wire or strip, not copper traces

With printed holders rather than PCB end caps, the three series links and the balance taps are wire soldered to the back of each contact plate, brought up a wire slot through the pocket centre from the outboard face, where the links run (928dfd4, 2026-10-09 — cap features, row 4, Soldering §16.5). ✅ This is the easy soldering — small, low-mass parts that wet in a second, and nothing like the cell-cap joint that refused to take at all. It is also all done on the bench with no cell present.

The tube must be PLASTIC, not aluminium, and that matters more without a cage: it is the only thing standing in the middle of four cans at four different potentials, so a conductive spine is one nicked wrap away from shorting cells. The fit gate proves the straight run is clear of every cartridge part.

End caps: with no cage the caps do everything. The links follow the locked orientation in §3.6.1.3: top cap carries pack+ (C1), link B (C2−↔︎C3+) with tap B2, and pack− (C4); bottom cap carries link A (C1−↔︎C2+) with tap B1 and link C (C3−↔︎C4+) with tap B3. Add a flat or key on one cap so the cartridge cannot rotate in the bore and twist its own tail.

⚠️ What IS new with swappable cells: abrasion

A soldered pack is wrapped once and never moves. A user-replaceable cell slides in and out against its neighbours and the bore, and that wears the wrap. Cheap answers, none of which cost diameter: shallow locating pockets in the end caps, a smooth bore, a re-wrap if nicked line in the manual, and — if wanted — a 0.2 mm PET sleeve per cell, which takes the reach to 63.7 mm and still clears Ø64. (0.3 mm does not: 64.2 mm.) ⚠️ 2026-10-09: that sleeve figure was checked against the old Ø64 bore only. The cartridge's cell spacers now hold the cells in Ø26.5 pockets on a fixed 26 mm pitch (0.25 mm radial), and a sleeved cell is Ø26.4 — the four would want a 26.4 pitch, so sleeves do not fit the current spacers without re-sizing them. Not evaluated in CAD.

STILL OPEN — flat-top cells cannot be mechanically keyed against reversal

The VariCore 26700 is flat-head, so a user can seat one backwards and it will still make contact. Button-top cells plus a recessed positive pad is the classic mechanical fix; the alternative is reverse-polarity protection in the circuit. ✅ 2026-09-06 — the mechanism to copy: sprung end plates on standoffs

A ~$13 AliExpress "solder-free 7-cell 18650 power bank" kit shows the pattern we want: two end plates carrying spring contacts, joined by standoff rods, cells held captive in compression between them. No holder box, no soldering, cells drop in and out.

That compression arrangement is mechanically better than a side-clip holder for our use — a knock loads the spring rather than levering the cell sideways out of a clip, so it is more shock-tolerant, which matters for a handheld.

But buy the pattern, not the product. That kit is unusable here for two independent reasons:

  1. Geometry. It is Ø60 with a ~56 mm usable bore, built around 7× Ø18 cells. Our 2×2 of Ø26 needs a 62.8 mm bore. It physically cannot take our cells.
  2. Electronics. It is an 18650 Li-ion power-bank board — USB-A / USB-C / boost converter / fuel display. We need 4S LiFePO4 straight out at 12.8 V through a LiFePO4 BMS. Wrong chemistry management end to end.

And 18650 is not a way out of the cell-size problem. LiFePO4 18650 is only ~1.5 Ah:

3.6.1 Pack assembly + BMS install — the actual bench procedure

3.6.1.0 Read first — the two things that actually hurt

  • A bare 4S LiFePO4 pack is ~51 Wh with no protection until the BMS is in and activated. LiFePO4 is the safe chemistry — it won't run away like Li-ion — but a dead short across a 3 C-rated 4 Ah cell still delivers enough current to weld, glow a tool red, and burn you. Remove watch and rings. Work on wood or a silicone mat, never bare metal. Keep one pack lead insulated at all times (tape the end) until it is landing in its terminal.
  • Sampling-lead order is not a style preference. A 4S BMS front-end samples cell taps in sequence; connecting them out of order, or plugging the balance connector while a tap is missing, puts the full pack differential across one cell's input and kills the board. B− first, then B1 → B2 → B3 → B4 ascending, and the connector goes in LAST.

3.6.1.1 Incoming cell check + matching

Method-independent — this section survived the cartridge move intact. Steps 1–4 are about selecting and matching cells, which you do whatever connects them.

  1. DMM every cell open-circuit. Healthy LiFePO4 rests 3.2–3.3 V; a freshly charged cell reads up to ~3.4 V. Reject anything below 2.5 V — it has been over-discharged and will drag the pack.
  2. Match them. All four should sit within ~50 mV of each other before assembly. The Lisolec's balancer is 36 mA — it will equalise a 50 mV spread overnight, but it will take days on a 300 mV spread and the pack will behave badly until it does. If they're spread, charge or bleed the outliers individually first.
  3. Record the four voltages next to the cell numbers. You will compare against these after the first charge.
  4. If they're spread, fix it before assembly. The bench PSU doubles as a single-cell charger: 3.60 V CV (not 3.65 — leave yourself margin), current limit 1 A, one cell at a time, never unattended. Bleed a high cell through a power resistor. This is the one pack task that can cost days, so do the measuring first, ahead of any other pack work — it parallelises with parts shipping.

Practise the joint on a spare plate, never on a build cell

An iron never touches a cell. The pack is the solder-free contact cartridge — cells drop into printed end caps against Keystone 5221/5222 contacts. Solder will not take on a cell cap at all (thermal mass, not chemistry: Soldering Guide §16.3a); that failure is why the cartridge exists.

The only soldered joints in the pack are the three series links and the balance taps. Each lead comes up a 5.0 × 4.0 wire slot through the pocket centre and is soldered to the back of the plate, plate lifted clear, cells out — cap features, row 4 and Soldering Guide §16.5.

Practise that joint first (18 AWG silicone onto the back of a spare plate): a 0.51 mm nickel-plated steel plate sinks more heat than bare strip, so dial the tip in on a spare rather than on the plate you are going to fit.

Two bottom links, one top link. Every link is a short hop between two adjacent contact plates at the same end — nothing runs along the length of a can.

✅ Both main leads come out the TOP: pack+ = C1(+) and pack− = C4(−). That is the parity result of alternating an even number of cells, and it is why this arrangement is worth the trouble — there is no full-length return run back to the BMS.

In the soldered-strip era that meant a 71 mm strip down the side of a can; with the cartridge it is worse, because there is no longer a route along a can at all. Longer, more resistive, harder to insulate, and it defeats the point of putting the links on the cap outboard faces.

3.6.1.4 BMS wiring — order of operations

The Lisolec is a common-port board: charge and discharge share P−, and there is no separate C− (the C− shown in the vendor diagram belongs to the separate-port variant — ignore it). Critically, this class of BMS switches the NEGATIVE leg — B+ is a straight pass-through to the load. That is why the topology in §4.1 puts the rocker in the positive leg and takes system ground from P−:

  pack+ (B+) ──────────────► rocker LINE ──► rocker LOAD ──► LED rail / fan / 5 V buck
  pack− (B−) ──► [BMS] ──► P− ─────────────────────────────► system GND
                                        └─► rocker lamp return

Land the rocker's lamp pin on P−, not on B− — on the protected side, so the lamp dies with the load when the BMS trips rather than staying lit off an unprotected node.

📖 Wire gauges, where each lead lands, and the soldering technique for both: Electronics_Soldering_Guide.md §16.5. Short version: 18 AWG for pack+ and the BMS B− return, 26 AWG for all five sense taps; leads land on the back of the contact plates through the wire slots (see the box below and §16.5).

⚠️ The SEQUENCE below is live and unchanged; the LANDINGS are not. Soldering Guide §16.5 is explicit about this: the order — B− first, B1→B4 ascending, balance connector LAST — carries over intact (gauges: see §16.5 — 26 AWG taps; leads landing on a plate are 18 AWG silicone since 2026-10-09). What changed is where each lead lands: the back of a contact plate, up the wire slot through the pocket centre from the cap's outboard face (928dfd4, 2026-10-09), never nickel strip on a cell.

Sequence:

  1. Build the string first, BMS not yet attached. Solder the three series links plate to plate (each end up its wire slot, onto the back of the plate) and fit the pack+ / pack− leads — all of it on the bench, plates in hand, before any cell goes in. Then assemble the cartridge. Both main leads taped off.
  2. Verify the string. DMM across pack+/pack− → 12.8–13.2 V open-circuit. If it reads ~9.6 V one cell is reversed; if ~6.4 V, two are. Stop and fix — do not proceed to the BMS.
  3. pack− (18 AWG) from the C4 plate to the cartridge XT30 FEMALE; the extension's XT30 MALE − lead lands on the BMS B− pad (§3.6.1.4a). This is the main current-carrying return, not a sense wire — it gets the 18 AWG treatment (Soldering Guide §16.5).
  4. Solder the sampling leads in ascending order: B1 → B2 → B3 → B4, into the XH-5 female in pin order 1 B− · 2 B1 · 3 B2 · 4 B3 · 5 B4. B− and B4 sense solder to their 18 AWG pack lead ~5 mm behind the cartridge XT30 female cup (§3.6.1.4a); B1–B3 each onto its link at the link's midpoint (taps are taken mid-link, §3.4 cap plan); B4 onto the pack+ plate. B1 = link A (Cell1−/Cell2+), B2 = link B, B3 = link C, B4 = pack+. ⚠️ Order is not a style preference — see §3.6.1.0.
  5. Measure at the balance connector BEFORE plugging it in. Probe pin-to-pin: B−→B1, B1→B2, B2→B3, B3→B4 must each read 2.5–3.65 V (the LiFePO4 window — not the 2.8–4.2 V Li-ion window; the Lisolec ships LiFePO4 firmware). B−→B4 must equal the pack total from step 2. Any tap reading ~0 V or ~6 V means a mis-order — fix it here, because plugging it in wrong is how the BMS dies.
  6. Only now plug the balance connector in.
  7. Verify the output. P+ (= B+) to P− should read pack voltage. If it reads 0 V the BMS is asleep, not broken — common-port boards commonly ship latched off and wake on the first charge pulse. Briefly present the PD charger on the charge path and re-measure.

⚠️ 3.8.3.1 The charge run's VBUS/GND were undersized — corrected 2026-08-28

This section originally specified 28 AWG for all six conductors on both runs. That is fine for the dev jack and wrong for the charge jack, because the pack's entire charge current flows through that jack's VBUS and GND.

  • Charging is a dumb CV arrangement: the PD trigger presents 15 V onto the pack via BMS P+/P−, and current is limited by the PD source's own current limit, tapering as the pack rises to the BMS's 14.6 V cutoff. There is no charge controller in the loop to hold current down.
  • The stated ~1.5–2 h for a 4 Ah pack implies a bulk rate around 3 A; the BMS's charge ceiling is 5 A. Size for 5 A.
  • 28 AWG is rated ~1.4 A chassis (0.23 A for power transmission). At 3–5 A it is an overload, in a sealed cavity, next to a lithium pack.
  • Use 22 AWG (~7 A) for the charge jack's VBUS and GND. 22 AWG silicone is in stock. Signal conductors stay 28 AWG. Ampacity ladder for reference: 26 AWG ≈ 2.2 A · 24 AWG ≈ 3.5 A · 22 AWG ≈ 7 A.

Two things still to verify on the bench:

  1. The 6-pin jack's own VBUS rating. Six-pin USB-C variants are commonly rated 3 A, some 5 A — the Teansic kit doesn't publish it. If it's a 3 A part, that becomes the real ceiling, not the wire.
  2. Pick a modest PD brick. A 15 V / 3 A (45 W) source keeps the whole chain inside comfortable margin and still hits the documented charge time. The trigger's "100 W" capability (6.6 A at 15 V) is headroom you have no reason to use here.

Two rules that are easy to get wrong (both enforced in Soldering Guide §13.3):

  • No 5.1 kΩ CC pull-downs on these jacks. They are passive bulkheads — the PD trigger and the ESP32 board each terminate CC themselves. A second sink termination breaks PD negotiation.
  • Shield → that jack's GND. Both ends are the same system; a floating shell has no benefit.
  • 🔴 Do NOT join the two jacks' GNDs (or shields) on the panel board (2026-10-24). The charge jack's GND is BMS P− (through the PD trigger, E10); the dev jack's GND is the LCD board = P−logic (E7). Bridging them makes a second P−logic ↔︎ BMS P− path in parallel with E14, the extension's one star point (§4.1.1). Keep two separate copper islands, and meter them OPEN with nothing plugged in before the panel is wired to anything.

The USB-C jacks here are panel bulkheads through a printed wall, and the technique — a scrap-PCB bulkhead captured in a printed pocket — is the one the wand uses for its 6-pin cradle (Integration §9.3b).

⚡ P− IS SPLIT — the wand's star ground does not survive a single bus (2026-09-23)

The defect. The wand's rail splits its ground: GND-power returns the 12 drivers and the fan, GND-logic returns the PCA9685 and the TMP102 (Integration §5.4.1 — ST1 board spec and sourcing). Both arrive here on the joint connector — and until 2026-09-23 both landed on the same P− bus, alongside E7 (the LCD board's GND). That rejoins them a few millimetres from the pack and undoes the split entirely: the ESP32 — which is the I²C master in this SKU — would reference its SDA/SCL to a bus carrying the full 2.4 A of LED return.

The wand-side split would have been decoration. The bay SKU gets its star point in the power bay; the flashlight SKU has no bay, so this board is the star point, and it has to actually be one.

🔑 The strip map does two jobs at once

1. The M2 holes have to be in dead copper. Drilling Ø2.2 severs the strip it is in, and the screw head then sits on that strip's copper. The wand rails learned this the hard way — an earlier revision put their holes on an invented 4th strip and was withdrawn (Integration §5.4.1 — ST1 board spec and sourcing). 2. Those same dead strips are the crossing corridors. R1 bridges dead strip 5; R2 and C1 bridge dead strip 2. No bare component lead ever crosses a live bus. The only thing that does is the insulated jumper joining the two tap halves.

A no-jumper map exists — P− · dead · free · tap · dead · V+ puts both components' spans over dead or unused strips — but it lands both buses on the outermost strips, 1.27 mm from a board edge, where a pull on the 18 AWG feed could peel the track. The jumper is the cheaper risk.

📖 What E13 actually is — and why you have not seen it before

It is not new, and it is not a design change here. E13 entered the docs on 2026-08-28, in two commits the same day: 5efbf08 added trap 3 to the §4.2.1 ESP32 pin map, and 0cbd2b1 added the E13 row to the harness table.

The trap: the ESP32 board's BAT_ADC on GPIO 4 is wired to its onboard 1S LiPo connector and tops out near 4.2 V. Our pack is 12.8–14.6 V, and we power the board from the 5 V buck rather than that connector — so GPIO 4 reads essentially nothing. The fix is an external 100 kΩ / 20 kΩ divider off the rocker-switched rail (so it draws nothing when the wand is off) into GPIO 3 (ADC1_CH2): 14.6 V reads 2.43 V at the pin, inside the ~2.5 V linear range at 11 dB attenuation, drawing 122 µA while on, with 100 nF pin-to-ground. Calibrate against a DMM — the ESP32-S3 ADC is not accurate out of the box.

Why it stayed invisible: it was specified electrically, in a pin-map trap list, and never given a physical location. It has been one row in a wiring table for three weeks. This section is the first time it has been placed on a board — which is a placement decision, not a new component.

Firmware support landed 2026-09-21: PIN_VBAT_ADC 3 in config.h, read via analogReadMilliVolts() (eFuse-calibrated) in battery.h, shown on the LCD status line and the web UI (packV/packLow in /api/state). VBAT_CAL is the DMM trim constant — default 1.000 until calibrated. Pin at 0 V (rocker off, or no divider — the bay SKU) reads as absent and displays nothing. Low warn at 12.0 V (~3.0 V/cell).

How you land on the LCD board — both boards ship with headers fitted: use them

Revised 2026-08-28 (twice in one day — see the note at the end), and widened 2026-09-20: the in-hand Waveshare has male headers already soldered on both rows, plus its 4 corner M2 brass standoffs — and the LiLyGO now on order is headered too (16 mm glass face to pin tips, §3.2.1). That settles the interface for either board: plug into them. Do not desolder them.

Every board that now goes into a shell arrives headered, so the desoldering argument below applies to whichever one you fit. The bare India bench LiLyGO is the sole exception, and it is a breadboard board that never enters a shell — Soldering Guide §15.

Why keep them, even though a soldered harness would be the textbook choice for a sealed handheld: removing two ~20-pin rows is **~40 desoldering operations on a board with a bonded display** — the exact heat exposure that already cost this project one LCD board, on the only shell board on site. That risk dominates any marginal retention benefit. The headers stay.

The one real cost is retention — the wand gets drop-tested, and a plain dupont shell can walk off a pin. Solve it, don't ignore it:

Approach Notes
Multi-way housing, never single-way shells One 4-way body cannot creep off pin-by-pin the way four 1-way shells can. Group by adjacency: on the Waveshare, VSYS 39 / GND 38 / (3V3_EN 37, skip) / 3V3 36 are four consecutive positions on the left row, and GPIO 15 / 16 are adjacent on the right. ⚠️ The LiLyGO groups differently — re-derive it, do not carry the Waveshare's bundles over. Its rows are 3V · 1 · 2 · 3 · 10 · 11 · 12 · 13 · NC · NC · GND · 5V (left) and GND · GND · 43 · 44 · 18 · 17 · 21 · 16 · NC · GND · GND · 3V (right) (§3.2). That happens to fall out cleaner than the Waveshare: power is a 2-way at the far end of the left row (GND 11, 5V 12 — adjacent), and I²C is a 2-way on the right row (SCL 18 at 5, SDA 17 at 6 — adjacent). The wand bus's 3V3 is at right 12, next to GND at right 11, so it wants its own 2-way rather than being stretched into the I²C body. GPIO 3 (the E13 divider tap, §3.8.4) is at left 4 — pair it with left 3 or 5 rather than using a single-way shell.
JST-XH female onto the 0.1″ pins — it latches The fleet-standard trick already sanctioned for the TMP102 (§10.5.5.1): JST-XH is 2.50 mm vs the header's 2.54 mm, ~0.04 mm/pin of splay, fine at ≤4–6 pins. Keep each connector small and it latches properly.
RTV over the seated housing A bead of Permatex 81158 (in stock) across the housing/pin junction. Non-conductive, and removable later with heat and a pick. Belt and braces on top of either option above.
Anchor the bundle Zip-tie or RTV the harness to an internal strap a few cm back so a tug on the loom never reaches the connector.

Crimping: the PEBA kit covers 2.54 mm dupont contacts and JST-XH (Assembly Guide §1.1), so both routes are in hand. 24 AWG signals except the USB-C jack leads (26), 22 AWG for the 5 V/GND pair.

Mechanically the fitted headers are fine. Pins protrude off the back (component side) into the cavity, alongside — not through — the Waveshare's 4× M2 studs, which sit on the measured 27.0 × 32.6 mm pattern outboard of its header rows (X ±13.5 vs ±9.5); its pins pass through slots in the straps the studs screw to. Radially: the pin tips reach r 18.2 (Waveshare) / r 20 (LiLyGO), about 18–20 mm in from the OD, and the only thing opposite is the rocker at 270° (blade tips r 11.7), leaving about 30 mm of clear bore between them.

Bonus: with headers on both boards, the bench LiLyGO and the shell Waveshare behave identically, so the §5.0a port can be bench-proven on the breadboard with plain jumpers before the board ever goes in the shell.

Record of the reversal, so the reasoning survives: an earlier revision of this section said "do not fit headers on the shell board — direct-solder", argued from a bare board. That argument still holds if you are ordering fresh: for a sealed, drop-tested handheld, a soldered harness beats a connector. It stopped being the right call the moment the board arrived already headered, because the cost of getting to that ideal is 40 desoldering cycles next to a bonded display. Ideal build ≠ right build when the delta is paid in risk to an irreplaceable part.

⚠️ Where the buck's 5 V lands on the LCD board

Feed VSYS (header pin 39), not VBUS and not 3V3.

  • Not 3V3 — that is the on-board regulator's output. Back-feeding it bypasses the regulator and can exceed the ESP32's absolute maximum.
  • Not VBUS — that is the USB 5 V rail, tied to the dev jack. Driving it back-feeds the USB connector and collides the moment a laptop is plugged into the dev port.
  • VSYS is the system input on this Pico-pin-compatible header (VBUS 40 / VSYS 39 / GND 38 / 3V3_EN 37 / 3V3 36 / VREF 35 — the Pico arrangement exactly), and 5 V sits inside its range.

⚠️ Verify before committing. This board also carries a 1S LiPo charger and JST connector, and VSYS may interact with that charger's output. Check the Waveshare schematic for how VSYS is OR'd, and if you want belt-and-braces isolation from the USB path put a Schottky in series with E6. If VSYS turns out unsuitable, the guaranteed-safe fallback is to feed 5 V into the lower dev jack's VBUS — powering the board exactly as a USB host would — accepting that you must not then plug a laptop into that jack while the pack is live.

Order of assembly, and why

  1. Pack + BMS (§3.6.1) — nothing else can be tested without a supply.
  2. Rocker in circuit (E1–E3) — a mechanical cutoff from the very first power-up, so every later step has an off switch. Confirm the lamp lights at 12.8 V.
  3. Buck (E4–E5), output open. Verify 5.00 V ± 0.1 unloaded, then loaded. This is the gate — a buck presenting 12.8 V kills the ESP32 board instantly, and it is the only unrecoverable mistake on this bench.
  4. Then, and only then, E6–E7 to the LCD board.
  5. PD trigger: set DIP 1-0-0 = 15 V (§3.8.1) and verify 15 V out against a PD source BEFORE E10 lands on P+/P−. The DIP is set once at assembly and faces into the cavity thereafter.
  6. Panel jacks (E11–E12) last — they are the most fragile joints and want the least handling.

Terminations

  • Screw terminals (PD trigger VOUT, buck if it ships with them): bare stranded or an XHF ferrule. Never tinned — solder cold-flows under clamp pressure and the joint backs out (Assembly Guide §12.2, cold-flow rule 2026-08-14).
  • Module mounting: buck on the raised VHB pad at 315°, PD trigger on the raised pad at 225°, BMS at 0°, rocker at 270°, LCD at 90°, USB panel at 180° (§3.7).
  • Distribution node → the ground-bus board, §3.8.4. The P− node is the one that matters: six loads plus its feed = seven conductors, all terminating on one small solder pad on the Lisolec module. Both nodes now land on one 15.24 × 25.4 mm ST1 stripboard blank with two buses.

Three traps this map exposes

  1. 🔴 The LCD is SPI, not parallel-8. LCD_CLK/DIN/DC/CS is a 4-wire SPI bus. The LiLyGO's Bus_Parallel8 LovyanGFX config will not drive this panel — lgfx_waveshare_s3.h must use lgfx::Bus_SPI with the pins above, panel ST7789-family, 170 × 320. This was the open question in the port plan; it's now answered.
  2. 🔴 GPIO 14 collides with our Button 2. Firmware currently has PIN_BTN_USER 14 (the channel-sweep self-test, the Phase-5 bring-up instrument). On the Waveshare GPIO 14 is LCD_BL — wiring a button there fights the backlight. Reassign to GPIO 0 (BOOT), which is the only other button on the board, or drive the sweep from the web UI (/sweep already exists) and drop the physical button on this SKU.
  3. 🔴 BAT_ADC on GPIO 4 cannot read the 4S pack. The board has a battery divider, but it is wired to the on-board JST 1S LiPo connector + charge management — it tops out around 4.2 V and our pack is 12.8–14.6 V. We also power the board from the 5 V buck, not that connector, so GPIO 4 will read ~nothing. Add an external divider across the rocker-switched 12 V rail (so it draws zero when the wand is off) into a free ADC1 pin — GPIO 3. Starting values: 100 kΩ / 20 kΩ → 14.6 V reads 2.43 V at the pin (inside the ~2.5 V linear range at 11 dB attenuation), drawing 122 µA while on. Add 100 nF pin-to-GND for noise. Calibrate against a DMM at a known pack voltage — ESP32-S3 ADC is not accurate out of the box. Firmware side implemented 2026-09-21 — PIN_VBAT_ADC 3 + battery.h; set VBAT_CAL from the DMM reading. One bench note: GPIO 3 is an S3 strapping pin (JTAG_SEL, inert unless its eFuse is burned) — sanity-check one boot with the rocker ON when the divider is first wired.

4.3 ESP32 Boot-Time Mode Detection

Historical rationale (planning, 2026-05) — implemented firmware boots as I²C master unconditionally (main.cpp); there is no boot-time probe. The pseudocode is kept to record why no MODE_DET pin exists on the connector.

on boot:
  scan I²C bus for PCA9685 at 0x40
  if device responds (we are master, no contention):
    enter FLASHLIGHT_MODE
    init LCD UI
    init touch
    listen for user input
  else:
    (shouldn't happen in v9-FL — there is no other master)
    fall through to FLASHLIGHT_MODE

This avoids needing a dedicated MODE_DET pin on the wand's tether connector — v9.11 has one 6-pin JST-XH in the end plate, all six nets spoken for, so there is no spare pin to add one to. Historical: 7-pin (V+/GND/SDA/SCL/3.3V + 2 SPARE — Integration §10.5.3).
