Core Narrative (One Machine, Many Jobs)

Position LumiAI as a single ceiling-mounted machine that does the work of several devices at once — the same arm, camera, and sensor suite handle multiple jobs simultaneously, and the platform is extensible to more:

  • Light — aims a precise, tracked beam exactly where it's needed, hands-free, from spotlight to RGBW scene.
  • Guard — watches for fire, intruders, CO, and falls around the clock, corroborating alarms and flagging what people miss.
  • Sense — tracks people, tables, and occupancy in real time with on-device vision that understands the whole room, not just a point.
  • Voice & Gesture — talk to it, point, or just walk in; the iFlytek HiWonder far-field sound array (mic array + speaker) takes natural spoken commands and talks back, alongside on-device face and gesture recognition.

How It Works (Sense → See → Aim → Light)

A passive sensor wakes the system, the camera understands the scene, the arm aims, and the wand lights — in well under a second, with zero buttons.

  1. Sense presence — a 4-sensor PIR ring gives ~360° coverage in total darkness, waking the arm the instant anyone enters (or on the spoken wake word, at micro-amp standby).
  2. See the scene — the OAK-D S2 stereo-depth AI camera recognizes faces, gestures, and motion on-device (OpenCV + PyTorch on a Raspberry Pi 5).
  3. Aim precisely — six degrees of freedom let the arm track a moving target smoothly: a person, a performer, or your hands on the workbench.
  4. Light it up — the Lumi Wand delivers a clean spotlight today, with 12-channel addressable RGBW scenes, beam shaping, and music sync next.

Three Parts, One Brain

An arm to move, a wand to light, and a sensor suite to perceive — engineered in-house on a Raspberry Pi 5 platform with battery backup.

  • The Arm — LumiAI Arm: a modified 6-axis chassis rebuilt in the US with a custom camera mount, machined base plate, and an integrated LiFePO4 UPS. Ceiling- or wall-mounted for full-room reach. (Compute: RPi 5 + 1 TB NVMe · Backup: LiFePO4 UPS)
  • The Wand — Lumi Wand: the business end — a 12-driver addressable RGBW light head with active thermal control, swappable between spotlight, floodlight, and effect attachments. (Control: I²C · PCA9685 · Variants: Arm / Flashlight)
  • The Sensors — Sensor Suite: a perception ring around the base — passive PIR presence that fires in total darkness, plus power, thermal, and air-quality telemetry that corroborate your alarms and feed the AI. (Presence: 4× PIR in the dark · Hazard: CO + smoke + PM · Voice: iFlytek HiWonder sound array)

🚀 Product Overview

Concept

A 6-DOF robotic arm platform integrating an AI-driven optical camera and a versatile gripper-mounted lighting system. Designed to be modular and environment-adaptive, the product enhances lighting control in garages, kitchens, restaurants, and entertainment venues by intelligently following human gestures, faces, and movement. The system is typically ceiling-mounted (inverted) or side wall-mounted, providing optimal coverage and tracking capabilities from elevated positions.

Key Features

  • AI Vision System:
    • Arm-mounted OAK-D S2 stereo-depth AI camera (standardized across all units) for on-device face, gesture, and depth-aware tracking.
    • PIR sensor for initial presence detection, enabling smart energy usage.
  • Voice System:
    • iFlytek HiWonder sound array (far-field mic array + speaker) for natural spoken commands, wake-word standby, and voice feedback.
  • Lighting System:
    • Relay-controlled spotlight (first release MVP).
    • Lumi Wand: 12-channel addressable RGBW light module (3× independently controllable 10 W RGBW LEDs) gripped and aimed by the arm.
    • Modular & standalone-capable: the same Lumi Wand can be sold separately as a battery-powered handheld light via the LumiAI Battery Extension (see Standalone Product section).
    • Future versions: RGB light effects, dynamic beam shaping, and synchronized music-light interactions.
    • Flexible gripper mount allows swap-out between spotlight, floodlight, or decorative lighting attachments.
  • Robotic Arm:
    • Imported Hiwonder ArmPi Ultra (6-DOF) chassis from China.
    • Modified in-house (US) with custom 3D-printed and CNC-machined parts for camera integration, UPS housing, and sensor boards.
    • Mounting Options: Typically installed as ceiling-mounted (inverted) or side wall-mounted for optimal coverage and tracking range.
  • Hardware Platform:
    • Raspberry Pi 5, upgraded with 1 TB NVMe + 512 GB SD card.
    • Integrated UPS module for uninterrupted operation.
    • Relay and PIR boards securely mounted on aluminum base plate.
  • Software Stack:
    • Restic for automated local + cloud backups.
    • AI models for gesture and face recognition (OpenCV + PyTorch/ONNX on RPi5).
    • Future software roadmap: MQTT/REST API integration for home automation & restaurant lighting systems.

Differentiation

  • Most robotic lighting systems are fixed-mount and pre-programmed (DJ lights, moving head lights).
  • This product adds true autonomy + human interaction, making it ideal for smart homes, garages, kitchens, gyms, and restaurants/bars seeking interactive ambiance.
  • Scalable from utility (garage task light) to experience (dance floor entertainment) to AI coaching (gym/fitness).
  • Modular product family: one shared wand engine sells two ways — embedded in the robotic arm system and as a standalone battery-powered handheld light — broadening the addressable market without new core tooling.

📱 Phone-Camera Mode — your phone as the robot's camera ⭐ (flagship; in development)

Clip your phone into the BigClaw phone cradle (see Mounts & Payloads above) and the arm + the LumiAI app work it as a robot camera operator. One app — the companion app, not a second one — coordinates the move and the capture together, which a stock third-party camera app can't:

  • Shoot-on-settle — the arm moves, settles, then the app fires. No start-of-clip blur or wobble.
  • Recording tied to the move — recording starts/stops with the framing move, so clips need no trimming.
  • Frames from the phone's own feed (not the tracker's offset view), with exposure + focus locked through the move.
  • Portrait ↔︎ landscape via the arm's wrist (a 90° roll mid-shot) — or bring your own mount on the cradle post's 1/4"-20 tip.
  • Hands-free direction — voice via the phone's own mic (through our app), or the iFLYTEK far-field array where fitted ("take a picture", "start recording", "follow me"); or a gesture (from the phone's camera, or the OAK-D S2 where fitted — incl. a "photo" gesture with a countdown).
  • Arm pose stamped per frame for post stabilization / stitching.
  • Where it records — base Creator records inside the LumiAI app (that's what lets the phone-camera tracking work), then shares out through the normal share sheet; auto-tracking in third-party apps is not a base claim. With the optional OAK-D S2 the Pi tracks independently, so the creator can record in any app (TikTok / IG / native camera) and still be followed — claim this only for the OAK option, labelled as such. Live streaming isn't claimed either way (direct RTMP out is a possible later add).
  • Powers your phone while it shoots — USB-C fast charging (PD / PPS — Super Fast Charging on Galaxy) from the arm keeps the phone topped up through long sessions. Modern phones are all USB-C.

Pitch: "Your phone becomes the robot's camera." Control is hands-free — no shutter button, no touching the phone — built into the one planned companion app (not a separate one). (On the entry Creator edition the phone is also the eyes and the mic — see Editions below.)

⚠️ In development — not shipping. The phone cradle is in its first test print (fit unproven); the app is a planned flagship direction, not a released feature. No capture-spec claims. Designed for a bare Samsung S22 and iPhone 12 Pro Max (others TBD). Held from behind, the arm can appear in the rear ultrawide's view.

🔦 Lumi Wand Anywhere — Standalone (Battery-Powered)

The Lumi Wand is a standalone product in its own right — not only an arm accessory — a battery-powered, app- and touch-controlled RGBW light that goes anywhere: no arm, no tether, no mains, no install. The LumiAI Battery Extension (a modular bottom section) screws onto the exact same wand upper assembly used in the arm system. Sold standalone as the Lumi Wand Anywhere. Studio-grade color, anywhere. The light that goes where you do.

How It Works — One Wand, Two Products

The wand body is variant-agnostic: it terminates in a standard threaded joint and one 6-pin connector in its end plate, and does not care what is on the other side of it — the arm tether and the battery extension present the same six nets on the same connector.

  • Arm-Tethered SKU → wand body + end plate and retaining ring, powered/controlled through the robotic arm's power bay via the end-plate connector.
  • Standalone SKU → wand body + Battery Extension, fully self-contained and portable.

This means one factory line and one shared inventory of wand upper assemblies feeds both products. The standalone version is a purely additive bottom module — it adds a revenue stream with no redesign of the core wand.

What's in the Battery Extension

  • 12-Channel Addressable RGBW Light: 3× independently controllable 10 W RGBW LEDs — the same premium light engine as the arm-mounted wand.
  • Touchscreen Control: Integrated 1.9" color touch LCD (LilyGO T-Display-S3 / ESP32-S3) for on-device color, brightness, and effect selection — no phone or app required to operate.
  • Wireless Connectivity: Built-in Wi-Fi + Bluetooth 5.0 for app control, firmware updates, and future smart-lighting integrations.
  • LiFePO4 Battery + USB-C PD: 4× 26700 LiFePO4 cells (~51 Wh), recharged from any standard USB-C PD charger — no proprietary dock. (⚠️ Runtime, light output, and IP rating are TBD — bench measurement pending; do not quote until measured.)
  • Mounts Anywhere: a 1/4"-20 threaded insert on the body (−Y face, opposite the LCD) screws onto any camera tripod, light stand, or bracket with the LCD facing up — or just hold it. ~1 kg, balanced for any ball/pan head. (See Flashlight Variant §1.1.)
  • Rugged, One-Handed Form Factor: illuminated rocker switch and a stepped, grippable silhouette built for field use.

🔆 Third SKU — LumiAI Wand Pro (60 W Spotlight)

⚠️ In design, not shipping. Direction set 2026-09-06. Engineering detail, photometric numbers and open gates live in Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §5.4. Nothing in this section should be quoted to a customer until the two gates below are closed.

A higher-output wand aimed at commercial installations where the standard 33 W unit runs out of reach: larger diameter, roughly double the light engine, no battery extension on this SKU (it is a powered fixture, not a portable one).

Why a Third SKU

The 33 W wand is a capable demo and a genuinely good accent light at short range. It runs out at distance. Commercial ceilings, retail feature displays and larger rooms put the subject 3-5 m away, and illuminance falls with the square of distance — so the same fixture that delivers a strong 1,364 lux at 2 m gives only 218 lux at 5 m, below office ambient. The Pro exists to hold a usable level out to where commercial installations actually mount.

What Changes

  • Double the light engine — 6 stars / 24 channels, at the same proven 700 mA per die as every other SKU. One emitter part number and one driver part number across the whole product line, which is the point: the Pro is additive to the existing build, not a parallel one. (Decided 2026-09-06 on inventory and production grounds; a 3-star higher-current alternative was evaluated and rejected because it added a second emitter and driver SKU used by one product only.)
  • Emitter: to be decided by measurement. The incumbent generic star stays for now; a branded alternative (Cree XM-L Color RGBW) will be tested against it side by side on the USA bench before either is committed. Whichever wins is used at the same current in all SKUs.
  • Bigger fan and heatsink — the real constraint at 60 W is heat, not current, and it now concentrates into three packages rather than spreading over six.
  • Larger diameter — freed up by the v9.6 gripper-jaw ear, which grips a 16 mm feature rather than the barrel, so the body is no longer sized by the gripper.
  • No battery extension — mains/bay powered only.

⛔ Two Gates Before This Is Sellable

  1. Colour rendering. The current generic RGBW emitter has no datasheet on file and its white die is estimated at CRI 70-80 — it has never been measured, because there is no spectrometer on the bench. Retail and hospitality specifications routinely require 90+. This is not solved by more watts. 🟡 A side-by-side test against a branded emitter is planned for the USA bench; the part-to-part consistency of the generic bin is expected to matter more than its average. Probably the single biggest commercial blocker, ahead of brightness.
  2. Flicker under camera. Dimming is 1 kHz PWM, which is invisible to the eye but can band against a rolling shutter at short exposures. Any customer filming under these lights — and "photography & video fill" is already a listed use case — will see it.

📌 Try the Optic First

Illuminance scales with centre-beam candela, and candela scales inversely with beam solid angle. Swapping the 30° lens cup for a 15° roughly quadruples centre-beam intensity — worth 2× the usable distance, against 1.41× for doubling the power. A 15° optic on the existing 33 W wand reaches further than a 60 W wand at 30°, for the price of a lens.

They multiply rather than compete — 60 W with a 15° optic is the strongest configuration — but the lens swap is a same-day test against a months-long build, and it should be run before the Pro's specification is frozen.
