Wand Bench Test — 3-Phase Incremental Rig (India build, 2026-08)

Purpose: stand up the ENTIRE wand electrically on the bench — in three phases that progressively replace breadboard jumpers with the real rails, JST-XH cables, and connectors — so that everything that will live inside the clamshell has already passed on its final wiring before assembly. Nothing goes into the shells unproven.

Master: LiLyGO T-Display-S3 NON-TOUCH running firmware/wand_extension/ — SDA = GPIO 17 / SCL = GPIO 18 (src/config.h; the pre-2026-08-27 18/17 assignment was a swap, fixed at first hardware bring-up). There is no CST816 on this unit — the I²C bus is exclusively ours. (The touch LiLyGO is FAULTY and retired; the Waveshare board takes over at shell integration — build plan §5.0a — but every phase below runs on the bench LiLyGO.) Supplies: bench PSU 0–30 V/5 A, and from Phase 2 on, the extension pack (4S LiFePO4 + 10 A BMS, build plan §2.6).

Standing gates that govern every phase (build plan §1):

  • INVRT=0 (cleared) + OUTDRV=1 (totem-pole, required by the pull-downs — Integration §6.2a; firmware config.h) verified by firmware at boot (fail-loud) — never bypass. ⚠️ Reversed 2026-09-01: KESHY is active-HIGH (Integration §6.2a).
  • ⚠️ UNBONDED-STAR THERMAL LIMIT: until the 8329TCM epoxy bond, a star merely resting on the heatsink has a poor heat path — ≤30 s per channel at 700 mA, ≤10 s all-channels. Mitigation (use it): the M3 washer clamp (below) gives clamped metal contact — with it, per-channel runs can extend to a few minutes; keep all-channels-full to ≤60 s and watch the TMP102.
  • M3 WASHER CLAMP (as-built heatsink v9.8.2, exports/cnc/lumi_wand_heatsink.nc): the puck has 3× M3 clearance through-holes at r=8 mm / 30°-150°-270°, each 3 mm outside the two adjacent star edges. Bench clamp: M3×12–16 screw down each hole, largest flat washer from the kit (Ø12+ for ~3 mm overlap onto BOTH neighboring stars) with a nylon washer as the contact layer (star pads are on the periphery — nylon can't short them), M3 nut underneath (raise the puck on two strips so nuts clear). Clock each star's pads away from its two contact points. Snug only, alternating between the 3 screws — overtightening bows the star and lifts its center. The same 3 screws serve as the epoxy-cure fixture later.
  • PCA9685 V+ terminal is NEVER powered (signal-only design, Integration §6.3).
  • Single-point ground: PSU GND, LiLyGO GND, and (Phase 2+) pack GND tie at ONE node.
  • The epoxy bond happens ONLY after Phase 3 passes (Assembly Guide §12.4).

Phase 0 — the bench-test star (do this first; everything else depends on it)

Stars arrive with bare pads — no pigtails. Until one star has wires on it there is no load to test a driver with, so this is the true first task of the build, ahead of the §2.1 driver sweep.

  1. Take one non-build star out of the spares — not one of the three that go on the heatsink.
  2. Solder pigtails at 380–400 °C (Soldering Guide §10). One channel is enough for the driver sweep; all four makes it a general-purpose bench load. Use longer leads than the build stars (~25–30 cm) so it reaches around the bench.
    • Terminate the far end in a 2-pin FEMALE connector (dupont or JST-XH) rather than bare wire. This star gets plugged and unplugged 15 times during the §2.1 driver sweep — a plug turns that into a second per module instead of re-clipping leads each time, and it removes the slip-and-short risk the bare-pad warning below is about. Female, not male: the driver presents the pins — the KESHY's LED output is on its 7-pin header and there is no screw terminal. ⚠️ This sweep runs BEFORE the header pins are clipped to their 2.0 mm build stubs; after clipping, nothing plugs onto them (Assembly Guide §12.2).
  3. Label it "BENCH TEST — not for build" with tape or a marker and keep it in its own bag. It gets abused across 15 driver checks; it should never end up in the wand.
  4. Do not alligator-clip bare pads instead. The pads are small, contact is unreliable mid-reading, and a slipped clip shorts the die.

This star is the load for build plan §2.1 and for any later "does this driver still work" question. No heatsink required for those checks — each module runs only a few seconds at a time; kill the PSU between modules rather than leaving the star lit while you write down readings.

What the pull-downs are for on this rig. Every driver's PWM pin floats to 5.28 V on an internal pull-up, and the driver is active-HIGH — so an unconnected PWM pin means that channel is at full brightness. Without the four resistors, any channel whose jumper is not yet in place lights up the moment the PSU comes on. Full detail: Integration §6.2a.

🔴 ROOT CAUSE ON FILE — all-channels-full-on, 2026-09-05

The bench was running pre-fix firmware. The INVRT=0 correction was committed hours earlier and never flashed; the board still held the old build, whose PCA9685_REQUIRE_INVRT 1 set INVRT on purpose and refused to boot without it. On an active-HIGH driver that makes a commanded duty of 0 drive every output HIGH — including CH15, which the firmware never writes. Reflashing fixed it.

Symptoms, and why each one pointed at the chip rather than the wiring:

  • Channels came on only once SDA/SCL were connected — i.e. the moment the firmware could configure the PCA.
  • CH15 read 3.23 V. Firmware never touches that channel, so the state was chip-wide, not a per-channel write.
  • OE tied to GND changed nothing — outputs enabled and still driven high.

OUTNE was NOT the cause, despite an in-flight theory that said so. The dump read MODE2=0x04, the chip's reset default, so OUTNE was 00 throughout — and OUTNE only has any effect when OE is HIGH, which the GND jumper had already ruled out. Firmware now forces OUTNE=00 anyway as defensive practice; that change is correct but it fixed nothing here.

The lesson worth keeping: a committed fix is not a flashed fix. Before debugging LED behaviour, confirm the build on the board is the build in the repo. The boot dump exists for exactly that — [pca] MODE1=.. MODE2=.. (INVRT=.. OUTDRV=.. OUTNE=..).

Healthy boot looks like: MODE1=0x20 MODE2=0x04 (INVRT=0 OUTDRV=1 OUTNE=0) · LED0 ON=0000 OFF=1000 (full-off bit=1) · all channels dark with the PSU up. ✅ Confirmed 2026-09-05.

Step 5a — the BTN2 channel sweep, in full

What it is: a built-in self-test in the wand firmware. It walks all 12 PCA9685 LED channels one at a time, ramping each 0 → 100 % → 0, so you see every channel come up and go down in isolation without touching a phone or a slider. It is the cheapest way to catch a dead channel, a swapped pair or a driver that never enables.

The button: front button 2 on the LiLyGO T-Display-S3 — GPIO 14, INPUT_PULLUP, active-LOW (config.h PIN_BTN_USER). ⚠️ It fires on RELEASE, not press — the handler debounces 30 ms, then waits for the button to come back HIGH before starting (main.cpp). A press-and-hold does nothing until you let go.

Also reachable over the air, if the button is awkward with the rig wired up: POST /api/preset with name=sweep to the LumiWand AP. Same routine.

What it does, exactly: for ch 0 → 11, ramp 0→100 then 100→0 in 5 % steps at 30 ms, then force that channel off. The LCD shows Sweep ch N/11 throughout.

Per channel 42 steps × 30 ms ≈ 1.3 s
All 12 channels ≈ 15 s

Procedure

  1. Rig powered and passing steps 1-5 (bus clean, channels dimming, current in range).
  2. Press and release button 2. The LCD switches to a navy sweep screen.
  3. Watch the four wired channels ramp up and down in turn, then let it run to the end.
  4. LCD returns to the normal status line. Channels all off.

What you should see on the breadboard rig — read this before you call it a failure:

Sweep step Channel As-wired colour Expect
ch 0 0 GREEN ramps up and down
ch 1 1 WHITE ramps up and down
ch 2 2 RED ramps up and down
ch 3 3 BLUE ramps up and down
ch 4-11 — nothing wired ~10 s of no light while the LCD keeps counting

⚠️ The last ~10 seconds look like a hang. They are not. Only 4 drivers are on the breadboard; channels 4-11 are sweeping into open air. The colour order is G, W, R, B because the rig is wired to CH_OFF_G=0 / CH_OFF_W=1 / CH_OFF_R=2 / CH_OFF_B=3 — not the canonical R,G,B,W.

PASS = all four wired channels ramp smoothly up and back down, one at a time with no other channel lighting, in the G-W-R-B order above, and the LCD returns to the status line by itself.

FAIL modes worth knowing: two channels lighting together → a crossed PWM lead at the PCA or a swapped driver input. A channel that stays on after its ramp → suspect that channel's pull-down. A channel that never lights → that driver, its PWM jumper, or its LED pair.

Method — validate the instrument first, then five things that decide whether the results mean anything

0. Validate the meter first — it's a ~$38 consumer photometer, not a lab spectroradiometer. Before trusting any generic-emitter number, point it at a source you know is high-CRI (a quality photo light / a known 95+ bulb) and confirm it reads high — an unvalidated reading is not evidence. Treat absolute CRI/R9 as ±a few points; everything in E1/E2 is a relative A/B on the same meter (generic vs Cree, centre vs edge, PWM vs analog), which is what it is reliable for. Don't touch the white sensor window, and keep distance/geometry identical between samples.

  1. Fixed geometry. Tape-mark the fixture and the meter. Dark room, same ambient for every reading. Any change between runs invalidates the comparison, and it is the easiest thing to get wrong.
  2. Measure at thermal equilibrium. Record at t = 0 and t = 30 min. LEDs droop 10-20 % as the junction heats — comparing a cold star against a hot one measures warm-up, not the emitter.
  3. Same drive current, verified. 700 mA for both. DMM in series — do not assume the driver.
  4. Swap only the star. Same heatsink, same bond, same driver, same optic. If the thermal path differs, the experiment is measuring the bond.
  5. Three samples of each part. ⚠️ This is the one most likely to decide the outcome. Generic emitters vary far more part-to-part than branded ones, and you cannot ship consistent fixtures from an inconsistent bin. One sample tells you nothing about what arrives in a reel.

Decision criteria — write these down before measuring, not after:

  • Generic Ra ≥ 85 and R9 ≥ 20 → generic is viable for retail/hospitality. Ship it, save ~100/unitontheProand 50 on the standard wand.
  • Generic Ra < 80 or R9 ≤ 0 → Cree is required for any commercial SKU. (R9 at or below zero is common on cheap RGBW white dies and is an immediate disqualifier for merchandise lighting.)
  • Sample-to-sample spread > ~500 K CCT or > ~15 % lux → generic is not production-viable regardless of its average, because consistent fixtures cannot be built from an inconsistent bin. This is the most likely failure and the strongest reason to run three of each.

E2 — optic

Same emitter throughout, swap only the cup: 15° beaded · 30° (current) · 45° beaded.

  1. Lux at 2 m and 3 m, centre, white only then all-on.
  2. Beam angle by measurement — sweep off-axis and find where lux falls to 50 % of centre. Compare against the nominal angle on the packet; expect the real beam to be wider, because a 5 × 5 mm four-die source under-fills an optic designed for a 3.45 mm die.
  3. Photograph the beam on a white wall at ~2 m, all four dies on — look for coloured fringing at the beam edge (the failure mode). The photo shows it; now also quantify it with the Light Master: read CCT + DUV at beam centre vs. beam edge. A large centre-to-edge delta is colour-over-angle, in numbers. This is exactly what a multi-die TIR optic (LEDiL RGBX / Carclo) is designed to collapse — measure the RGBX the same centre-vs-edge way when it is in hand, and it should flatten that delta vs. the flashlight collimators.
  4. Compare measured centre-beam candela against the predictions in §5.4.2.

Decide from measurements, not from the nominal angle. Predicted: 30° → 15° should be worth ~2× usable distance against ~1.41× for doubling the power. If fringing makes 15° unusable, the fallback is a TIR optic designed for multi-die RGBW (Ledil / Carclo), not a flashlight collimator.
