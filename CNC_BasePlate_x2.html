Plate 2: check the hole set before the drill pass

The drill is a Ø3.175 twist drill. A drill that lands partly on an existing hole walks into the wall and breaks. It is safe only when it lands fully inside an existing hole (centre within 0.4 mm of that hole's centre for a Ø4.0, 0.65 mm for a Ø4.5), or on solid sheet.

What to do. Lay the 1:1 template on the sheet and look at each of the 14 target holes. The template is in the machine frame (mount face up, origin = stock centre, X right, Y up): print it at 100 % on A3 with no fit-to-page, check the 100 mm bar with a ruler, put the sheet's min-X / min-Y corner on the corner mark, and read the holes through the paper. Each target has a green ring (r 0.25) and a dashed ring (r 5.0). Alternative with calipers, for a 200 × 300 sheet measured from the min-X and min-Y edges (use W/2 and H/2 if the real sheet differs):

Classify every target:

What is on the sheet Rule Action
An existing hole centred within 0.25 mm of the target Right Put its ID in the skip list.
No existing hole centre within 5.0 mm of the target Missing Drill it (leave it out of the skip list).
An existing hole centre between 0.25 and 5.0 mm from the target Conflict Do not drill it: a pilot there lands partly on the old hole. The operator decides (accept the old hole where it is, or another method). Tell CNC.

Why 5.0 mm. The finished new hole is hand-drilled to Ø4.5 (Ø4.0 for PSU) and an old hole may be Ø4.5, so the web between them is d − (D_new + D_old)/2, and a 0.5 mm web needs d ≥ 5.0 mm. Any old hole whose centre is more than 5.0 mm from every target is a harmless unused hole.

On a sheet laid out like the original, nothing falls inside the 5.0 mm rings: the closest target pairs are STRAP.L.hi to POST.5 at 11.56 mm, PSU.1 to STRAP.R.lo at 12.87, STRAP.R.hi to POST.6 at 15.68, PSU.2 to POST.7 at 22.52. If the sheet still has the old 8-hole carriage set, the closest to a target are POST.1 to CARRIAGE.L.2 at 10.04, POST.2 to CARRIAGE.L.1 at 12.10 and PSU.2 to CARRIAGE.L.3 at 16.54, all outside 5.0. The Ultra's own pattern is unknown, so the check above is what decides it. The generator's 0.5 mm web guard only compares each hole with the outline; it cannot see what is on the sheet.

Dreanique DSA1F-D3.1-L08 (3 ordered 2026-11-03 on AliExpress, $5.26 each, en route; unproven)

Single-flute solid carbide, 1/8" shank. Not an O-flute. From the listing drawing (operator screenshot): cut length 8 mm, overall length 38.1 mm, shank 3.175 mm, cutting diameter about 3.1 mm (the "D3.1" in the part number). Flute geometry (spiral up/down) and centre-cutting are not stated: read them off the bit before the first cut. Until centre-cutting is confirmed, do not use the NC's straight vertical plunge on this bit; use a ramped or pre-drilled entry (needs an NC variant, not generated yet).

  • Size: the NC compensates for 3.175 mm, so a 3.1 mm cutter cuts the outline about 0.04 mm per side (0.075 mm total) large and the engrave lines about 0.075 mm narrower. Measure the real cutting diameter with calipers; the fence fit should tolerate it, check on the scrap run.

  • Collet: 1/8" OZ8, the same as T1. The T1 to T2 swap is a bit swap, not a collet change, so the NC's "1/8" to 1/4"" wording does not apply to this bit. Re-touch Z.

  • Stick-out: seat the shank deep; expose the cut depth (3.2 mm) plus clamp/screw-head clearance, about 12 mm, not the whole shank.

  • Baseline = the NC as written (SHARPRO values): S18000, lateral F600 (0.033 mm per rev), vertical plunge F100, OP3 passes at 0.6 mm steps to Z-3.20, engrave 0.375 + 0.375. Same cutter diameter and flute count, so chip load per flute is unchanged.

  • First run, scrap 1060 3 mm only: lateral override to about 70 % (F400, 0.022 mm per rev), plunge at F60. Cutting fluid or air blast. Stop and look at the flute after the engrave and again after one full contour. Go to 100 % only if the flute is clean and the chips come out as chips, not strings.

  • Not before: it is not on a plate until it has passed the scrap run. The SHARPRO stays the plate tool until then.

What holds each plate

No tape or VHB. Clamps hold the sheet for the drill pass, then screws hold it from the pause on.

  • Drill pass (before the M0): edge toe clamps. The sheet is 200 wide and the cutter edge reaches |X| = 80.75, so there are about 19 mm of free sheet on each long side. Put the clamp toes no further in than |X| = 86 (5 mm clear of the cutter), three per long side at Y ≈ −110, 0 and +110. There is no room at the 300-long ends (8.5 mm free). Use low-profile clamps and jog the tool in the spindle over them to check the collet nut clears their tops.
  • At the M0 (after OP1): screws through four holes into fresh spoilboard: POST.5, POST.6, POST.7 and POST.8, which span the plate length. Machine coordinates: POST.5 X +68.886 Y +104.018, POST.6 X −69.254 Y +103.848, POST.7 X +43.206 Y −121.262, POST.8 X −43.504 Y −120.992. The NC's tool-change banner lists them. The clamps stay on, so the waste frame stays held after the contour frees it, and the screws carry the finished part.
  • Plate 1: the four holes are the Ø3.175 pilots OP1 just drilled; M3 × 15 SHCS self-tapping into the spoilboard.
  • Plate 2: screw only through holes verified on the sheet. Each of POST.5 to POST.8 is either drilled by this run or listed in the skip list because the operator checked it against the template; the NC banner tags each one "drilled by OP1" or "existing, verified". Never put a screw through an existing hole that was not checked. A verified old hole may already be Ø4.5, which leaves a thin bearing ring under an M3 head: use an M3 screw with an OD 7 washer, or an M4 × 16 SHCS.
