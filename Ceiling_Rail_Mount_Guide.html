LumiAI — Ceiling Rail Mount Guide

Purpose: Mount the LumiAI base plate to the ceiling on linear rails so the robot can be slid down for service and locked in place during operation. Date: 2026-05-06 (compact M4 stop revision — MGW15-inspired)

1. Why rails

The robot base needs to be:

  • Securely fixed to the ceiling during operation (resists 6-DOF arm dynamics).
  • Removable for service / cleaning / repair without unbolting from above the ceiling — a ladder + 4 lag bolts is a 30-minute job; a rail slide-out is a 30-second job.
  • Cleanly aligned (parallel rails, no rocking).

The original CNC-machined channel design (custom slots in 20×20 mm aluminum bar) approximated a dovetail with M6 locking screws and required ~2.5 hours of CNC + drilling + tapping per pair of channels. Off-the-shelf MGN15H linear rails do the same job for ~$60 in parts, no machining.

3. Sizing rationale

Why MGN15H (not MGN12)

For a ceiling-inverted 6-DOF arm, the binding load isn't pure static (4 kg arm vs 312 kg static rating = 78× margin) — it's moment load from the arm swinging.

Rail Moment load (My / Mz) Margin at full arm extension
MGN12H ~7-9 N·m ~30×
MGN15H ~11.7 N·m ~50×

MGN15H is only marginally more expensive and the extra moment headroom matters when the arm is throwing the LumiAI Wand around at speed.

MGN15C vs MGN15H — full spec chart

Within the MGN15 family there are two carriage block lengths: C (short) and H (long). Pick H — it has materially higher load and moment ratings for a small price difference.

Spec MGN15C (short) MGN15H (long)
Block length 45.5 mm 58.8 mm
Block width 32 mm 32 mm
Block height 12 mm 12 mm
Dynamic load rating (C) 1,170 N 1,610 N (~362 lbf)
Static load rating (C₀) 2,140 N 3,060 N (~688 lbf)
Moment load My 6.95 N·m 11.7 N·m
Moment load Mz 6.95 N·m 11.7 N·m
Weight (block only) ~18 g ~28 g

Figures are from HIWIN's published MGN-series datasheet. Generic Chinese rail blocks list comparable specs on paper — actual precision and load capacity can vary; only HIWIN-genuine units hold tight tolerances. For a 4 kg arm at workshop tolerances this isn't a concern.

Why H over C for this application:

  • ~29% higher dynamic rating (1,610 N vs 1,170 N)
  • ~43% higher static rating (3,060 N vs 2,140 N)
  • ~68% higher moment capacity (11.7 N·m vs 6.95 N·m) — the binding constraint for a 6-DOF arm
  • Same width/height — drop-in physical replacement, no design changes

The long block adds 13 mm of length per carriage but is the right call for load-bearing axes.

Why two rails (not one)

A single rail lets the base plate pivot around the rail axis under arm yaw. Two parallel rails kill that rotation completely. The cost difference is one extra rail (~$25); the structural difference is significant.

Why 200 mm length

Base plate footprint: ~150 × 275 mm. 200 mm rail gives ~25 mm slide travel — enough to disengage the carriages cleanly during service, not so long that mounting becomes ungainly. 300 mm is overkill and harder to mount.

4. Mounting hole modification (only if needed)

Inspect the rail's mounting holes on receipt before drilling anything. MGN15H rails have varying hole sizes by manufacturer:

  • Hiwin / TEN-HIGH genuine spec: Ø3.5 mm through-hole + Ø6 mm × 4.5 mm counterbore (designed for M3 SHCS into a tapped base). M3 lag bolts into ceiling joists are undersized for this — drill out to Ø5 with a step drill so M5 lag bolts fit.
  • Generic Chinese (AliExpress 2-pack option, $26): appears to ship with Ø6 mm clearance through-holes already — M5 lag bolts drop straight in with 1 mm clearance, no drilling needed. M6 lag bolts also fit. Confirm with calipers on receipt.

If drill-out is required (Hiwin spec):

  • Aluminum extrusion drills like butter; ~2 minutes per rail.
  • Only the 4 holes per rail you'll actually use as anchors need enlarging (every ~50 mm of rail). The rest can stay at original size.
  • Use a step drill to take it from M3 (Ø3.5) → M5 (Ø5) in one shot. Don't try to drill straight to 5 mm with a twist drill — it will walk.

If drill-out is NOT required (cheaper Chinese rails with Ø6 holes): skip this section entirely. Proceed to §5.

5. Installation procedure

5.1 Mark and pre-drill ceiling

Use the 3D-printed parallelism jig (cad/freecad/lumi_mount_ubracket.py, resized for MGN15H — see §7 below) to mark the rail positions on the ceiling at the correct spacing.

  • Rail spacing (centerline-to-centerline) = 103.145 mm. This is the as-built value — the two carriages are placed by the user in Plasticity as the solids Mounting Carriage Left / Mounting Carriage Right in cad/reference/step/Base_plate_mounting_holes.step, and cad/freecad/lumi_base_plate.py drills the plate's Ø3.4 clearance holes coaxial with each carriage's own 4 M3 holes. Those carriage solids are the single source of truth — no hand spacing, no fence-outline formula. The macro still boolean-checks every carriage hole's M3-head footprint against the fence solid and fails loudly if one would clip the fence wall.

    Diagonal layout (2026-05-17, "more stable" revision): the carriages are NOT side-by-side at one Y. They are offset diagonally to widen the support base and improve pitch/roll moment resistance:

    Carriage Centre (FILE X, Y)
    LEFT (183.87, −69.5) — front half
    RIGHT (287.01, +71.5) — back half

    Rail c/c (the X gap between the two rails) = 287.01 − 183.87 = 103.145 mm. Each carriage still travels along its own rail in Y; the plate slides as a unit. The diagonal placement also sidesteps the plate's hourglass waist (it pinches to ~111.5 mm only at Y≈0 — neither carriage sits there).

  • Locate the ceiling joists with a stud finder. Position rails so the M5 lag bolts land in joist material — drywall alone will not hold a 4 kg moving load.

5.2 Bolt rails to ceiling

  • M5 × 50 mm lag bolts with fender washers, 4 per rail, into joists.
  • Tighten progressively — don't fully torque one bolt before starting the next. Pulling one corner tight first will pull the rail out of straight.
  • Verify rails are parallel by sliding the carriages along their full length. They should move freely with no binding. If a carriage binds at any point, loosen the rail mounts on that side and retorque progressively.

5.3 Mount carriages to LumiAI base plate

The base plate carries a dedicated, parametric 8-hole carriage pattern (4 per carriage), generated by cad/freecad/lumi_base_plate.py — it is the single source of truth, no "verify/re-drill the OP1 holes" guesswork.

  • Hole spec: Ø3.4 mm M3 clearance through-holes (full through the 3 mm aluminium plate). The MGN15H block ships with its own 4× M3 tapped holes in the steel; you never tap a thin plate when the mate is tapped steel. M3 socket-head cap screws pass from the component side, through the plate, into the carriage's M3 taps.

  • Per-carriage pattern (Hiwin MGN15H datasheet): 4× M3 on a 25 mm × 25 mm rectangle — 25 mm transverse (B, across the 32 mm carriage width = the rail-to-rail X direction) × 25 mm longitudinal (C, along the rail = plate Y, the H-length block).

  • As-built positions (FILE frame, mm) — diagonal layout, from the Mounting Carriage solids:

    Carriage Centre (X, Y) 4 holes (X × Y)
    LEFT (183.87, −69.5) {171.37, 196.37} × {−82, −57}
    RIGHT (287.01, +71.5) {274.51, 299.51} × {59, 84}

    Carriages are diagonally offset (LEFT front, RIGHT back — see §5.1) for a wider, more stable support base; rail c/c = 103.145 mm. The plate's Ø3.4 holes are drilled coaxial with the carriage solids' own M3 holes (verified 0.0000 mm).

  • The 4 M3 screw heads land on the component side. If a head falls under the PSU, the PSU simply seats ~screw-head-height higher — accepted, no interference issue.

  • M3 socket-head bolts (NOT M5 — MGN15H is an M3-mount carriage), Loctite 243 on the threads — vibration from arm dynamics will eventually walk an unsecured bolt out, and once a single carriage bolt loosens the load redistributes badly.

5.4 Slide robot up to operating position

Two-person job (robot + arm + battery + electronics ≈ 4 kg). One person supports the robot; the other slides carriages onto the rails from the open end and pushes the robot to the operating position.

5.5 Install rail stops (3D-printed PA-CF, replaces detent-pin lock)

The carriages are locked in place by 4 printed PA-CF slides (2 per rail) that grip the rail body via a brass-insert + M5 set-screw friction lock. No drilling of the carriage or the rail. The slides bracket each carriage above and below, constraining its Z position to the parked window.

Why this approach (vs. the original detent-pin lock)

The original §5.5 design called for drilling Ø5 mm holes through the carriage mounting flange and rail flange to accept a quick-release detent pin. That worked but had three drawbacks:

  • The carriage flange is small — drilling near the steel bearing body risks breaching the ball channel and scrapping the carriage.
  • The pin location is FIXED at the parked Z position only. If the parked Z changes during install (e.g., joist position different than expected), you'd have to drill again.
  • With pre-mounted carriages on the LumiAI base plate, the carriage mounting flange is permanently sandwiched against the base plate — no top-side access for a vertical pin once the assembly is up.

The stop-lock approach solves all three:

  • Stops clamp the rail at any Z position the user picks during install — set-screw friction lock.
  • No carriage drilling — stops ride on the rail BODY, not the carriage.
  • M4 set screw is on the SIDE of the stop (perpendicular to the rail) — accessible from a ladder with a single Allen key, regardless of base plate orientation.

Hardware per stop

  • 1× printed PA-CF stop (cad/freecad/lumi_wand_rail_stop.py) — 32 × 16 × 10 mm (cross-section matches the MGN15H carriage profile per Hiwin spec: W=32, H=16), channel slip-fit on the MGN15H rail body (Wr=15, Hr=10). Design inspired by Hiwin's commercial MGW15 steel stopper (M4 thread approach) but with the M4 hole on the SIDE wall as a STEPPED THROUGH HOLE (Ø5.4 × 6 mm insert seat + Ø4.5 × 2.25 mm clearance to channel) so the set screw tip can actually emerge and clamp the rail.
  • 1× M4 brass heat-set insert (Ø5.6 mm OD × 6 mm long, in side wall — drops into the Ø5.4 mm seat and stops at the step)
  • 1× M4 × 10 mm cup-point (or knurled-tip) set screw

4 stops + 4 inserts + 4 set screws total per LumiAI install (2 per rail × 2 rails).

Heat-set the brass inserts (one-time, on the bench)

  1. Print the 4 stops per 3D_Printing_Guide.md §5c.
  2. Use a soldering iron at ~230°C with a flat tip. Place a brass insert tip-down in the Ø5.4 mm hole on the stop's +X side wall. Press gently with the iron tip until the insert sinks flush with the surface (PA-CF flows around the knurls, ~3-5 sec). Remove iron, let cool 30 sec before handling.
  3. Repeat for all 4 stops.
  4. Test thread an M4 set screw into each insert — should turn smoothly with finger pressure for the first few turns.

Install on the rails

  1. Confirm the carriages are mounted to the base plate as described in §5.3.
  2. Slide each carriage onto its rail from the open end (per §5.4) — push to the parked position.
  3. TOP stop (per rail): slip the printed stop onto the rail above the carriage (open end) — push it down the rail until it's directly above the carriage, then snug the carriage upward against it. Tighten the M4 set screw via a 2 mm Allen key — the cup-point clamps against the rail's side face by friction. Stop is now locked at this Z.
  4. BOTTOM stop (per rail): slip the printed stop onto the rail below the carriage (open end) — push it up until snug against the carriage's bottom face. Tighten the M4 set screw.
  5. Repeat steps 3-4 for the second rail.
  6. The carriages are now positively constrained between the top and bottom stops on each rail. With both stops snugged tight against the carriage faces, the carriage does not move at all during arm operation.

Tip: orient each stop so the M4 set screw faces OUTWARD (away from the other rail) — this gives the cleanest ladder access for tightening / loosening.

6. Service slide-out

  1. From the ladder, loosen the BOTTOM stop's M4 set screw on each rail (single 2 mm Allen key — same tool for both stops). 2 set screws total to free the carriages.
  2. Slide each BOTTOM stop downward off the rail end (no longer captured by friction once loosened).
  3. One person supports the robot.
  4. Slide the robot's carriages downward off the rail ends — the TOP stops stay in place on the rails (no need to remove them for service).
  5. Lower the robot to a bench or table.
  6. To reinstall: reverse — slide carriages back up onto the rails, push up to the parked position (against the still-installed TOP stops), slide BOTTOM stops back on, snug them against the carriage bottoms, retighten the M4 set screws.

End-to-end: ~45 seconds with two people. Single 2 mm Allen key for everything; no other tools.

Why the TOP stops stay on the rails during service: the carriage exits the rail downward (gravity-assisted). The TOP stops only block UP-direction travel — they don't impede the service slide-out. Leaving them in place means the parked Z position is preserved across service cycles, no re-positioning needed on reinstall.

8. Risks and follow-ups

  • Stop creep / set-screw loosening over time: if the M4 set screw works loose under arm vibration, the stop could drift along the rail. Mitigation: apply blue Loctite 243 (medium-strength threadlocker) to the set screw threads at install. Re-tighten every ~6 months as preventive maintenance, or whenever the assembly is opened for service. The cup-point biting into the rail's anodizing also helps anti-rotation once seated.
  • Stop channel wear: PA-CF on aluminum has very low wear under static load (the stop is locked, not sliding repeatedly). PETG-CF has somewhat higher wear; if using PETG-CF, plan to replace stops every 5+ years of frequent service cycles. PA-CF expected to last the life of the LumiAI install.
  • Drywall-only mounts: absolutely will fail. If your ceiling is finished drywall over open joists, the lag bolts MUST hit joist material. If joists don't align with rail positions, add a sister joist or a 3 mm aluminum backer strap that spans 2+ joists.
  • Alternative locks if stop friction proves marginal: drill a Ø3 mm cross-pin hole through the stop AND a matching shallow dimple in the rail's side face at the parked position — the M4 set screw with knurled tip + cross-pin gives positive engagement. Only needed if friction-only proves unreliable in soak testing; install with Loctite 243 first.
  • Aesthetics: if commercial install requires hidden hardware, design a 3D-printed fascia cover that snap-fits over the rail face leaving only the carriage exposed.
