  • Glue is the release layer that protects the plate, not just adhesion. Owner reports: bare PETG on PEI is what wears the coating out (several dozen prints to failure). The 2-week-old X1C textured plate lost its PETG grip in patches on 2026-10-18 after bare-PETG use. Well-kept Bambu PEI plates last 1,000+ hours with glue.
  • No IPA / alcohol on PEI. A Bambu support reply (quoted by owners) says alcohol makes the problem worse. Glue stick is water-soluble, so IPA only smears it around (the patchy glue seen 2026-10-18). Soap and hot water removes glue and oil; rinse, dry with a clean paper towel, don't touch the print area.

🖨️ WHICH PRINTER, WHICH MATERIAL, WHICH COLOUR

The two-printer split. ASA and NylonX run on the H2S. The X1C takes the PETG+ carriers, brackets and cartridge, the PLA sensor carriers and the red cradle test set. ASA prints much better than PLA or PETG, so it takes the wand, the extensions, the fence and the covers. The table below is what the plate books carry.

  • ASA: wand, wand extensions, end plates, end rings, retaining ring (the wand/extension joiner), fence halves, electronics-bay covers, servo base covers.
  • PETG+: straps and rail stops (they take impact; PETG+ is tougher), the power-bay carrier, the cartridge and the camera adapter.
  • Sensor carriers (ZE15-CO, SPS30 plate and strap): PLA, X1C slot 1.
  • Head lens plate: NylonX until the spool runs out, then PAHT-CF. TPU where needed.
  • ⚠️ ASA warps more than PETG+, not less (higher shrinkage), so long flat parts such as the 228 mm carrier stay PETG+.
  • A part moving to ASA needs: a brim, a re-run of its strength check against ASA, the spool dried at 65 °C, and the chamber closed.

🧪 First ASA job on the H2S: the THREAD COUPON, before any shell half (PolyLite profile, door closed — the door-open rule covers PLA and PETG+, not ASA; see §3.1). Do not spend a 5-hour half finding out what five minutes of coupon shows.

Judging the coupon (method from the LumiAI 3D Printing session, 2026-10-14): hand-thread the mating ring rather than calipering crests — every thread pair carries 0.5 mm radial clearance, so crest measurements mislead. On the exported file, check BOTH the bore-radius spread across the thread band (~1.4 mm cut / 0 uncut) AND that root-radius verts span thousands of distinct Z values — the histogram alone once passed a threadless cartridge tube.

⛔ TPU on the H2S bypasses the AMS and the filament buffer — and 95A and 85A take DIFFERENT paths. Source: Bambu's "Preparation for TPU Printing for H2 Series" (edited 2026-08-10), which is what the printer's own "Detail" link opens — "When H2S and H2C print specific types of TPU, the feeding method is basically the same as that of H2D." https://wiki.bambulab.com/en/h2/h2d-tpu-printing-guide

⚠️ Everything below is the procedure WITHOUT the Feed Assist Module. Step 1 is the same for both durometers. Then the paths diverge — 95A routes a tube, 85A has none at all.

  1. Unplug the internal PTFE tube at BOTH ends — the toolhead end FIRST, then the end seated in the spring-loaded filament buffer on the back wall. Bambu's page lists both: "Disconnect the PTFE tube of the right hotend", then the one "on the filament buffer".

TPU 95A / 90A — the tube goes out through the "TPU" hole

  1. Push the buffer end OUT through the "TPU" hole, from inside to outside.

  2. Set the length from the toolhead's FAR position. Move the toolhead to the front, by the screen — the point farthest from the hole — and leave enough tube inside to reach it with slack. The remainder runs out the back towards the PrintDry.

    ⚠️ If that leaves too little tube to reach the PrintDry without sharp bends, use ONE longer tube instead — 4 mm OD / 2 mm ID PTFE, PrintDry straight through to the toolhead. The operator expects to need this, and no such tube is on the spares shelf, so treat it as a likely purchase alongside the nozzle.

  3. Thread the filament BEFORE heating. With the toolhead end still unplugged, push the filament through the tube until it comes out at the toolhead end.

TPU 85A — no tube, fed from ABOVE

  1. Take the internal tube right OUT — 85A "cannot be fed through a PTFE tube and must be directly fed into the extruder". ⛔ It is removed, not parked in the hole; steps 2–3 above are 95A's only.

  2. Feed from the top. Bambu's preferred route for 90A/85A is the top feeding rack: open the top cover and run the filament down from the PrintDry placed ABOVE the printer — "the shortest feeding path and the least resistance ... highly flexible filaments such as 90A and 85A". Failing that, keep the dryer close and in line with the extruder. The filament runs unguided either way.

    ⚠️ Bambu publish an "H2D Flexible Filament Top-feed Rack" model for this. It is named for the H2D and nobody has checked that it fits the H2S — the TPU High-Flow Kit turned out H2D-only too. The top-feed principle needs no rack: it is the dryer placed high and the cover open.

Then both again:

  1. Set the hotend to 250 °C. ⚠️ This is the LOADING temperature and it applies to both durometers. The 0.6 book's 225 °C is 85A's printing temperature — a different number for a different job.
  2. Feed it into the extruder by hand, with single slow taps of the extruder ↓ arrow. ⛔ Never rapid taps. Wait until it extrudes steadily.
  3. 95A only — NOW plug the tube into the toolhead, then start the job. Bambu is explicit that "85A does not require this step of inserting back the PTFE tube" — for 85A there is no tube to plug in.

Teardown. 95A: pull the tube back in through the hole, then plug it into the buffer and the toolhead. 85A: refit the internal tube at both ends and close the top cover. (The refit is not in Bambu's steps — it follows from having removed the tube, and without it the AMS path stays broken for the next non-TPU print.)

Bed: 95A — Bambu liquid glue or a glue stick on Smooth or Textured PEI. 85A — no glue.

🔁 The 0.4 stays the H2S default; the 0.6 goes in for 85A jobs only (operator, 2026-11-01). Per swap:

  1. Fit the hardened 0.6 before an 85A plate.
  2. Set the nozzle size on the printer's screen to match what you just fitted. The printer does not sense it — that value is a declaration, and the job is run against it.
  3. Run the 85A plate.
  4. Put the 0.4 back, and set the screen to 0.4 again.

Because the 0.4 remains the default, every other H2S plate stays as sliced — the 95A plates, the PETG+ wand parts, the ASA shells and the NylonX lens plates need no re-slice.

✅ Check before EVERY H2S job: the nozzle in the machine, the size on the screen, and the book's nozzle profile all three agree. Two out of three is the failure mode, and step 4 is the one that gets forgotten — the 0.6 left in after plate 30 is a 0.4 job printed through the wrong nozzle, with nothing on screen to say so.

🛑 85A needs a bigger nozzle than this printer carries. Bambu's page lists 0.6, 0.8 and the TPU high-flow as the supported options, but the TPU High-Flow Kit is sold for H2D / H2D Pro only — so on the H2S the real choice is a 0.6 or 0.8 Standard Flow hardened hotend. A standard 0.4 is not supported, and nor are 0.2 nozzles or ordinary high-flow nozzles. This is why the cradle's 85A job needs the 0.6 — see §3.5.

⚠️ Cold pulls — Bambu's rule is 3–5 after a CF/GF job, before TPU. On this H2S that is not currently due: the hotend has run "tons of PLA+ and PETG+" since the NylonX lens plate (operator, 2026-11-01), so it has been flushed many times over. Do the 3–5 pulls if the TPU clogs or under-extrudes, or if you have just printed CF/GF.

⚠ Unloading NylonX → switching back to PLA — per printer

It said the auto Load/Unload routines drop to PLA's ~220 °C and ram a solid nylon plug, so you must drive 280 °C by hand. That is true of the X1C and NOT of the H2S — see below. The manual procedure below is the X1C procedure.

H2S — use the printer's own filament-change routine ✅

The H2S handles the purge itself, and it does the right thing. Operator, 2026-10-27: it "takes nozzle to 270 or so and asks user to pull back external filament, then when u load say blue pla it keeps nozzle at 270 while purging old filament til the blue pla shows thru — its a long old filament purge."

~270 °C sits inside NylonX's ~260–290 °C melt range, and the machine holds it through both the unload and the purge of the new filament — which is exactly what the manual X1C procedure exists to force by hand.

  • Use the built-in change routine. Do not drive the temperature manually.
  • Your check is the purge itself: it must run CLEAN — no black streaks, no CF flecks — before you accept the swap. The purge is long; let it finish.
  • A cold pull is OPTIONAL here, and only if specks persist after a clean- looking purge.

X1C — the manual 280 °C hand-feed below is REQUIRED

Do not skip this on the X1C. Skipping it cost a full extruder teardown. NylonX-CF melts at ~260–290 °C; PLA loads/prints at ~220 °C. Any nylon left in the hotend stays solid at PLA temperature — so when you load PLA next, it rams a hard nylon plug, can't advance, and the auto-cutter snaps the (brittle) filament and leaves a stub. The residue cooks into a blob in the melt zone. Confirmed failure mode (X1C, 2026-06-24): an ~8 mm black NylonX blob lodged in the extruder, every PLA load snapped at ~10 in, and it took opening the extruder fully to dig it out.

⬜ Whether the X1C's newer firmware now behaves like the H2S has NOT been tested — until the operator says otherwise, drive it by hand.

The one rule: the nozzle must stay at nylon temp (280 °C) continuously from the moment the print ends until the purge runs clean. If it cools with nylon still in it, you get the plug. So you drive the temperature manually (Bambu Studio Device tab, or the printer screen) — do not rely on the auto Load/Unload routines, because those set their own temp (PLA's ~220 °C, too cool to melt nylon). That's the piece that was unclear before.

Exact sequence — NylonX from the external dryer → PLA:

  1. Print ends — don't let it cool. Immediately, in the Device tab, set the nozzle target to 280 °C and leave it there. This holds it hot through the whole swap. (Nozzle temp is fully manual here — you set the number, it holds.)
  2. Unload the NylonX. On the filament screen, Unload the external (Ext) filament — it retracts the nylon back up the PTFE toward the dryer. Pull the nylon out at the external port and move the dryer aside. Then re-check the nozzle is still set to 280 °C — if the unload routine dropped it, set it back.
  3. Run PLA through — by hand, into the external port, at 280 °C. With the nozzle held at 280 °C, feed a loose strand of plain PLA (or cleaning filament) into the same external port by hand until the extruder gears grab it, then tap the Device-tab Extrude (down) arrow repeatedly to push it through. Keep extruding until the ooze runs clean and translucent — no more dark/CF-flecked nylon. This is "when you run PLA through": step 3, at 280 °C, after the nylon is out. (Don't use auto "Load" — it would drop to 220 °C.)
  4. Cold pull (thorough finish). With that purge PLA still in, drop the nozzle target to ~90–100 °C, wait, then pull the strand straight out sharply. Repeat until the tip comes out clean (clean cone, no dark specks). Remove the purge strand.
  5. Now load your printing PLA normally. The melt path is clean, so load the AMS (or external) PLA the normal way — it heats to 220 °C and feeds with no jam. Start the print.

Why hand-feed the purge PLA into the external port instead of just loading the AMS? Because the AMS auto-load heats only to PLA temp (220 °C) and would ram the still-solid nylon plug. Purging manually through the external port is what lets you hold 280 °C and control exactly when PLA goes through. Once the nozzle is clean (step 5), the AMS load is safe.

If the AMS then sticks in a retry loop (confused filament state after the external-spool unload — see the 07FF-8003 / 0700-8003 troubleshooting rows): power the printer OFF, manually pull the filament back out of the AMS by hand, then power on — it should read clean.

Prevention: keep PLA dry (brittle/old PLA snaps in the cutter far more readily), and always run this hot purge on a NylonX → PLA swap. The reverse (PLA → NylonX) is safe — nylon temps remelt any leftover PLA.

Vent rationale (chimney effect, v6)

12 KESHY modules at 700 mA dissipate ~4.8 W inside the handle. Without venting, the enclosed PETG runs 15-25°C above ambient. With side-only angled vents, interior delta-T drops to ~12-15°C. Adding end-cap corner vents on both top and bottom faces turns the handle into a convection chimney: cool air enters through the bottom end vents, heats up around the modules, exits through the top end vents — driving delta-T down to ~8°C, well below PETG soft point (80°C) in any reasonable ambient.

End-cap vents are 7 × 3 mm rectangular through-cuts placed in the 4 corners of each end face, clear of the heatsink mount bosses, wire-exit slots, and cable grommet. Perpendicular cuts (no angle) — wand is indoor-only, so IP rating not needed.

9. Troubleshooting

Issue Cause Fix
Warping on U-bracket Large flat base + PETG Add brim, increase bed temp to 85C, ensure enclosure closed
Lens rings drooping Overhang without support Enable supports for lens rings only
Grip ridges not sharp Layer height too coarse Use 0.16mm or 0.12mm layer height
M3 holes too tight Printer shrinkage Drill out with 3.5mm drill bit
Lens plate won't drop into the skirt / sits high A key tab is not over its notch — plate a few degrees out, or tab/notch swelled The plate has 3-fold symmetry, so turn it until all three tabs drop into the notches; if none seat, ease the tab sides.
Head ring lifts off bed early (was NylonX; ASA since 2026-10-17 — the brim/bed advice still applies) Nylon warp; bed too cold / brim gap / fan Inner+outer brim 8 mm, brim-object gap 0, bed 60–65 °C + fresh glue (), fan 0% first ~5 layers. Check it is lip DOWN — lip-up drops the footprint from ~1120 to ~600 mm². See §3.1 "Bed adhesion — head ring + lens plate".
Head cap disc sags / fails just above the sleeve ⛔ Cannot happen since v9.9 — there is no disc over an open bore any more. The plate prints flat on the bed. If you are seeing this you are slicing a stale lumi_wand_cyl_headcap.stl; that part no longer exists.
Lens towers land between the LED stars, or the lens tips are scuffed Slicing a pre-v9.9 one-piece head cap, where the towers were on the part that turns Re-export and print the head ring + lens plate pair. See §3.1a.
Head ring won't thread on / thread crests damaged Pre-v9.9: support fused to the internal M70×3 thread and tore on removal. Since v9.9 the ring needs no support at all If supports are on for the ring, turn them off. If crests are already damaged, chase the thread by hand on the wand's external thread.
Shell half lifts off plate (standing vertical) Thin thread-ring end contact + warp Clean plate + brim, run the chamber door open (also cuts warp; PLA + PETG+ rule, NOT ASA — scoped 2026-10-14, PETG+ added 2026-10-17), keep it centred; add a raft (2–3 layers) if it still lifts. See §3.1 shell-halves note.
Bottom-most thread groove squished / slumped on a standing half Chamber too hot — heat softens the plate-side thread Print with the chamber door open — keeps that first thread turn cool. Field-found. PLA only (scoped 2026-10-14) — ASA/nylon need the chamber; never open the door on an ASA thread because of this row. See §3.1 shell-halves note.
Shell-half threads staircased / cap tight (flat prints only) Threads printed on their side in the flat fallback orientation Prefer vertical (clean self-supporting threads). If flat: test-thread and run the cap in gently, or lightly chase; the 0.4 mm clearance normally accommodates it.

PETG stringing Retraction too low Increase retraction to 1.5mm, lower nozzle temp 5C
