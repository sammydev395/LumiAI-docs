1. Servo-rail supply — ZK-SJ20 buck-boost

Decided (operator, 2026-11-18): the HV servo rail (12.0 V to the STM32 board, which feeds the servos) comes from a ZK-SJ20 300 W buck-boost CC/CV module with display (WAVGAT, AliExpress), ordered 2026-11-18, 1 unit, est. Nov 29 – Dec 5. It replaces the front-wall DROK (a diymore 12 A / 160 W LCD buck) on the HV build.

Why: the buck in service sags ~0.6–0.7 V at 10 A, and on battery it drops out of regulation (needs IN ≥ OUT + 0.8 V), falling to ~9.6 V at heavy load. The 20 A LM25116-type boards (60 × 53 × 28 mm) list a 3 V minimum dropout, so they can't make 12.0 V from 13.8 V. The ZK-SJ20 is a 4-switch buck-boost, so it holds 12.0 V from the PSU's 13.8 V and from a sagging battery. Load: servos only, ~8–9 A sustained, ~12 A peaks (~110–145 W); the wand runs off the 13.8 V rail, not through it.

On arrival: caliper the size, mounting holes and terminal positions for CAD; then the setup and bench gate below before any servo connection.

Setup before it ever connects to servos (bench, e-load, meter on the output):

  1. CV pot → 12.0 V (11.99–12.0), never above. Then seal the CV pot with a dab of varnish: it is a trimmer on a moving robot.
  2. MPPT pot fully down (≤ 7 V), so the board never throttles its output to hold the input. With it set higher, a battery sagging under load (HV undervolt cutover 9.5 V) would make it cut servo power.
  3. CC pot: set the current limit above the arm's measured peak (servos ~8–9 A, peaks ~12 A) and confirm it on the e-load.
  4. Power-on default ON: press ON/OFF for 2 s until the display reads ON, so the output comes up by itself after a power cycle.
  5. ⚠️ No overvolt setting and no key lock: the only output limits are the CV pot and the 300 W OPP. A buck-boost can output far above its input, so a mis-set pot can put 20 V+ on the servos. The 12.6 V software overvolt trip (Electronics_Bay_Design §1) is the protection; check the output on the meter after any handling.

Bench gate before it goes on a robot: e-load at 8, 10 and 12 A, from 13.8 V and from the battery; read the servo port and the board temperature (display °C). Decide on a fan (5 V port) from that run, check its noise against the bay's acoustic design, and confirm the terminal type (screw terminals in the photos) and the continuous current with no fan.

The one-rail rules, arbiter rules, acceptance and the 11-12…11-16 bench record, as they stood. The bench numbers were taken on the old DROK; the ZK-SJ20 needs its own run (§1).

  • Halo HV only, not the shipping configuration: the HV servo swap (all six HV, 9–12.6 V; all six together or none; the product releases with the LV servos). Halo and Nova stay LV (7.4 V servos, DROK 7.5 V). Everything below this bullet applies only after the swap. The HV supply is now the ZK-SJ20 buck-boost, not the DROK (operator 2026-11-18); the HV bay, carrier, fence and open items are tracked in HV_Bay_Design.md. The DROK bench data below is the record of why. One rail, as on LV: the existing DROK feeds the board, and the board feeds the servos through its bus port.
    • Setpoint 12.0 V (11.99–12.0) on the existing DROK (operator, 2026-11-16): at the board's 12 V maximum (Hiwonder; it covers every peripheral on the board's input rail), inside the HV servos' 9–12.6 V. Never above 12.0 V. Requirement (operator, 2026-11-16): the servo port stays ≥ 11.1 V on mains. With the path at ~0.072 Ω warm, the DROK's output under load must be ≥ 11.1 + 0.072 × I: 11.68 V at 8 A, 11.82 V at 10 A. At 12.0 V set it reads 11.88 V at 8 A (port ~11.3 V, ~0.2 V margin). ⏳ 10 A is not met on today's figures: the DROK sagged ~0.6–0.7 V at 10 A (11.8 V set → 11.1–11.2 V), so at 12.0 V set it would sit ~11.3–11.4 V, about 0.4–0.5 V short. Measure the DROK output at 10 A with 12.0 V set before relying on it. On battery the ~9.6 V floor is below 11.1 V but inside the HV servos' 9 V minimum. Bench: 8 A for 10 min at 12.0 V passed (above); the DROK held 11.5 A sustained at 11.1 V (heatsink ~28.5 °C; reaches ~11.6–11.8 A) and sags ~0.1 V at 8 A, ~0.6–0.7 V at 10 A. A higher setpoint does not raise the on-battery floor (below).
    • ⏳ Servo fuse: undecided (operator). The operator's view: the DROK's own protection is enough, so no fuse between the DROK and the board. On the bench, when the load asks for more than its limit it shows "OCP" and cuts its output and latches off; only its ON/OFF button restores it. ⏳ Open, operator to decide: on a ceiling mount nobody can reach that button, and the board, the servos and the Pi (powered from the board) stay dark after a trip. Still to check: whether OCP trips at all with IN− tied to OUT− as in the bay, whether short servo peaks can trip it, and whether a power cycle of its input clears the latch with its power-on default set to ON. Before deciding, repeat the DROK overload on the bench with its IN− tied to OUT−, as they are in the bay: the manual says its CC is invalid when they are tied, and today's cut-out was seen with them apart. If a fuse is added it is a 10 A 6 × 30 glass (F1's type) in the base's second opening; until then the robot carries F1 only, and the DROK-to-board run is protected by the DROK and, upstream, the PSU's own output limit (on AC) or F1 (on battery).
    • What the board carries (facts, not changes): all servo current crosses its input-to-port path (~0.068 Ω DROK-to-load incl. plug and pigtail, 2026-11-16; the HV arm draws ~0.68× the LV current for the same work, so it loses less there than the 7.5 V arm); its 16 V input tantalums sit at 75 % of rating at 12.0 V (polymer parts are fine; MnO2 parts are past the usual 50 % derating; the marking decides); Hiwonder has not tested six HV servos on it. Ultra's two-port plug test (4/6/8 A, up to ~11 A) measures the board path and the plugs. First confirm the two ports are in parallel, board unpowered and the meter's leads nulled (REL): port 1 V+ to port 2 V+ and port 1 GND to port 2 GND each read ≈ 0 Ω (a few tenths at most), V+ to GND on each port reads open or high (not a short), and which pin is V+ is metered at the input terminal, never taken from wire colour. Then powered at no load, both ports read the input terminal's voltage. Then with the e-load on port 1 only, port 2 (no current) should read what port 1 reads; if it falls well below, the ports are not one node. Test pigtails: JST-PH 2.0 female housings and crimp contacts (fit on the board confirmed 2026-11-15). Bus-servo port pin order: pin 1 (square pad) = GND (operator metered to the power port's − screw, 2026-11-16), middle = V+, far pin = servo data. Crimp the pigtails by net. A PH contact is rated 2 A and takes 24–32 AWG, so at 8–9 A over two ports each V+ and GND contact carries ~4–4.5 A, about twice its rating. The genuine JST PH contact takes 24–32 AWG with insulation OD 0.8–1.5 mm. The kit's clone contacts carry no rating, so assume the same. With 22 AWG (the operator's choice; the contact current is unchanged, the wire just runs cooler), the strands overfill the wire barrel and the insulation won't fit the insulation grip. So make one sample crimp first and check it: every strand inside the barrel, the barrel fully closed, the contact clicks and locks in the housing, and a firm tug doesn't pull it out. If the insulation grip can't close, put heat-shrink over the joint for strain relief. If the sample fails any check, crimp 24 AWG at the contact. A bad crimp is a high-resistance joint, the exact heat source the NTC watch below is for. Keep the pigtails short, hold each step only as long as the reading needs (~30 s), and tape the e-load's NTC to a port housing: stop the step if the housing gets hot or the port-side drop jumps between readings. Order and current split: run each port alone first (only that port's pigtail on the e-load, 3 A then 4 A), so all of the current is known to go through that one port's contacts; then both together for the total. Two ports on one load input share current by path resistance (crimps, contacts, lead length, board trace), not evenly by default, so measure the split: make both pigtails the same wire and length, and read the mV drop along each pigtail (housing to load end) with the load on. Same wire and length means the drop ratio is the current ratio (22 AWG ≈ 53 mΩ/m, so ~30 mV at 4 A over 150 mm). Stop if one side carries clearly more than half. The Fluke i410 clamp's ±0.5 A offset is too coarse at 3–4 A. In the arm, each servo chain draws only through the port it's plugged into. A servo stall dips the board's input along with the servo bus (Halo LV: 7.4 → 6.58 V, 11 %; the same dip at 12.0 V is ~10.7 V), so acceptance (d) checks the board and Pi under the heaviest load.
    • On battery the rail falls out of regulation at heavy load. The DROK needs IN ≥ OUT + 0.8 V (manual), so 12.0 V needs ≥ 12.8 V in, and on battery the DROK drops out of regulation almost at once (the pack rests at ~13.1 V). Nova's 10 Ah pack measured 13.1 V at rest and 12.0 V at 5 A on its own terminals (apparent ~0.22 Ω). Bench, DROK at 11.1 V with 11.5 A out from the PSC-100-12: ~10.97 V on AC; pulling AC switched to battery instantly (no dropout) and the output fell to **~9.85 V**, coming back to ~10.97 V when AC returned. Re-run with the setpoint at ~11.8 V and the pack partly discharged (13.2 V at rest): the same **~9.6 V**, only 0.1 V above the 9.5 V undervolt. The setpoint does not move the battery floor; the floor follows the pack (~0.22 Ω at ~12 A input), and a fuller pack raises it. Ultra's board-port test on battery alone (2026-11-16, pack 13.0–13.2 V, load ~10 A across both ports, not confirmed) read the same 9.6–9.7 V, 0.1–0.2 V above the HV undervolt. On a fully charged pack at ~6 A through the UPS (2026-11-16) the rail held **~10.5 V** for the first few minutes, 1.0 V above the undervolt; a full pack at ~10 A is not yet measured. Halo (LV) rode a real house outage on battery for ~29 min (2026-11-16 12:45–13:14): INA226-MAIN input 13.65 V falling to a minimum of 11.98 V, no reset or stack restart. Real arm draw is short peaks on one or two servos, not 11.5 A continuous. The LV configuration (7.5 V) keeps over 3 V of headroom on battery.
    • Overload: a servo overload is cut by the DROK's own protection (bay behaviour still to be confirmed, see the servo-fuse item), upstream by the PSU's output limit, or on battery F1, and the arm and board go dark. The thin servo chain cable is protected as in Hiwonder's stock arm (it carries the arm's total servo current, with no fuse of its own); the stall guard and the servos' own stall/over-temperature protection cover a stalled joint.
    • Rules (arm arbiter, both launch params per robot):
      • Overvolt: 12.6 V on HV (operator, 2026-11-16; the HV servos' top rating), 8.4 V on LV. The code today has only overvolt_v = 8.4 V, which sends the arm to rest; at 12.0 V every servo reads overvolt, so the HV launch config must raise it to 12.6 V or the arm rests the moment HV is powered. A trip at the 12.0 V setpoint itself would fire on normal readings.
      • Undervolt: 9.5 V (operator), a NEW rule (none exists in the code today), with a 10 s hold (operator 2026-11-18; fires 10–20 s after a drop, on the 10 s health-file writes). ⏳ Its action is the operator's call; proposal: alert + rest pose.
      • Swap day: read each HV servo's EEPROM vin min/max before first power-up (the HV servos' line-wide rating is 9–12.6 V; check each model's listing: the 12.0 V setpoint needs every one rated ≥ 12.0 V).

8. Hiwonder statements (support replies, quoted)

2026-11-13, answering the operator's 11-12 questions (including "Do the HX-35H brackets fit in the ArmPi Ultra elbow and wrist positions in place of the LX-15D brackets?"):

  1. Controller Board Input Voltage: The STM32 controller board supports an input voltage of up to 12V. However, you also need to consider whether the other peripherals connected to the controller board can operate within the same input voltage range.
  2. Servo Power Supply: The controller board passes its input voltage directly to the two bus servo ports. There is no voltage regulation between the input power and the servo ports.
  3. Driving the Six Servos at 11.1V: We have not tested this STM32 controller board with the six servos you listed operating at 11.1V. Therefore, we cannot confirm whether it can reliably handle their combined peak current under this configuration.
  4. Communication Protocol: The bus communication protocol is the same for these servos.
  5. Mechanical Compatibility and 3D Drawings: The two servo models have different dimensions, so they cannot share the same mounting brackets. The HX-35H brackets are not compatible with the elbow and wrist mounting positions on the ArmPi Ultra.
