1. Standing constraints & one-way gates

  1. INVRT=0 is mandatory — ⚠️ reversed 2026-09-01 by bench measurement. KESHY drivers are active-HIGH, not active-LOW: the PWM pin floats at 5.28 V with the LED lit, and tying it to IN− extinguishes. INVRT=1 would make a commanded 0 % drive the output HIGH = all 12 channels full-on — i.e. the old "INVRT=1 is mandatory" rule caused the failure it claimed to prevent. Firmware now clears the bit and read-back-verifies, halting loudly on failure, and sets OUTDRV=0 (open-drain) so the drivers' own 5.28 V pull-up supplies the high level (Integration §6.2a).
  2. Bench-test before epoxy — the 8329TCM bonds (3 LED stars → heatsink, TMP102 → heatsink underside) are irreversible. Every star channel must pass a powered bench test and the TMP102 must answer at 0x49 on a bus scan before mixing epoxy. 24 h cure, no power during cure (Assembly Guide §12.4).
  3. Full-bright ≤30 s without airflow + telemetry running.
  4. Thermal policy (firmware-enforced): fan <45 °C→20 %, 45–65 °C linear→100 %; LED hard cutoff >80 °C, restore <70 °C; sensor loss → fan 100 %.
  5. Bench PSU limit — ✅ NOT a limit (corrected 2026-09-01 by measurement). All 12 channels at full brightness draw ~2.4 A / 33 W at 13.8 V, not the ~6.7 A this line used to claim, so the 0–30 V/5 A bench PSU runs the whole wand with 2.6 A of headroom. No sequential sweeps, no partial coverage, no dependency on the battery pack for electrical bring-up. The old figure came from multiplying LED-side current (12 × 700 mA) by the input rail voltage — linear-driver arithmetic on a buck (§2.1 step 4a; Integration §5.3).

Parallel-track restructure (2026-08-21): the extension is not a week-4 afterthought — it is the test instrument. The LiLyGO is the I²C master for every bring-up step. ⚠️ The second reason this box used to give — "the battery pack is the only supply that can run all 12 channels (~6.7 A > bench PSU's 5 A)" — was WRONG and is withdrawn (measured 2026-09-01: all-12 is ~2.4 A, well inside the 5 A PSU). The restructure still stands on the first reason alone — the LiLyGO is the I²C master, so the extension electronics must come up in week 1, in parallel with wand bench work, and Week 4 stays mechanical integration + soak rather than first power. What changes is the scheduling risk: the battery pack is no longer on the critical path for any electrical gate, so a delay in the pack build no longer blocks LED bring-up. Gates that specified "on battery" for current reasons can run on the bench PSU; only genuinely pack-specific checks (BMS trip, buck brownout, runtime) still need it.

2.1 Driver bench-verify — all 12+3 KESHY modules

Refs: Integration §7.3, §13 Step 2

  1. Number each module 1–15 with a marker; make a checklist (module #, mA reading, pass/fail).

  2. Loupe each board: IC silk = XL4001E/XL400-family; sense network = 1R00 ‖ 1R00 ‖ R400. Anything else (e.g. R500‖R400‖R400 ≈ 1 A drive-hard SKU, or a PT4115) → set aside, NOT a build part (Integration §7.1 off-spec table).

  3. Prerequisite — the BENCH-TEST STAR (do this before step 4). Stars ship with bare pads, so nothing can be tested until one has wires on it. Pick one non-build star from the spares, solder pigtails to a single channel (or all four — see Soldering Guide §10), and mark it "BENCH TEST — not for build." This one star is the load for all 15 driver checks; the three build stars stay pristine until Phase 1/Phase 3. Alligator-clipping bare pads is not a substitute — the pads are small, the contact is unreliable, and a slip shorts the die.

    • No heatsink needed for this check. Each module is on for a few seconds at a time. Rest the star on a scrap of aluminium or ceramic tile if you like, but do not leave it lit while you write down readings — kill the PSU between modules.

  4. Current test per module: bench PSU 12–13.8 V → VIN+ pin 1 / VIN− pin 3 (the KESHY has no screw terminals, everything is on the 7-pin header: 1 VIN+ · 2 VIN+ · 3 VIN− · 4 VIN− · 5 PWM · 6 LED+ · 7 LED−); one channel of the bench-test star on LED+ pin 6 / LED− pin 7 — ⚠️ in that order; backwards reverse-biases the channel; DMM in series on the 10 A range. Expect 600–700 mA.

    • "DMM in series" = the meter becomes part of the LED loop, not a probe across it. Break the circuit at one point (easiest: driver LED+ → meter red lead in the 10 A jack, meter black lead → star +), so all the LED current flows through the meter. Dial to DC A / 10 A range. Never put a meter in the 10 A range across a supply — that's a dead short.
    • "PWM pin untouched" = leave it floating, nothing connected. With nothing on the pin the module's default state is LED ON at full current — that is the intended state for the current reading. Mechanism (measured 2026-09-01): the pin is held at 5.28 V by an internal pull-up, and the driver is active-HIGH, so floating = full brightness. (A 2026-08-28 edit deleted the original "an unconnected pin sits high internally" note as contradicting the active-LOW spec. The note was right and the spec was wrong — restored. See Integration §6.2a.) 4a. ### ✅ INPUT current measured 2026-09-01 — the 2.8× design error is confirmed and resolved
    Supply Input current Input power
    12.3 V 0.22 A 2.71 W
    13.8 V 0.20 A 2.76 W

    (One KESHY, one white/blue channel, 0.7 A measured on the output side. Predicted 0.19 A — measured 0.20–0.22 A.)

    • Constant input power within 2 % across a 12 % supply change. That is the constant-current buck signature: output is regulated at 0.7 A × Vf, so input power is fixed and input current scales inversely with input voltage. Back-solves to Vf ≈ 3.1–3.3 V at 80–85 % efficiency.
    • 12 channels at 13.8 V = 2.4 A / 33 W, not the documented 8 A / 84 W. Integration §5.3 independently said 34 W and was right; the 84 W came from multiplying LED-side current (12 × 700 mA) by the input rail voltage — linear-driver arithmetic on a buck.
    • What this unblocks: the 5 A bench PSU can run all 12 channels. §1 constraint 5, Gate E, Phase 3 step 4 and Assembly §16.4 were all gated on a number ~3× too large. The battery pack comes off the critical path for most of bring-up — which matters while the nickel strip is still in transit.
    • ⚠️ Change no working hardware over this. Tether and rail gauges are over-provisioned, not wrong; the 13.8 V split-rails topology stands. It is the reasoning that was bad, and it must not be reused to size a fuse, a BMS or a new cable. Full write-up + the list of superseded figures: Integration §5.3.
    • Still owed: the all-12 total measured at once, as confirmation.

  5. Polarity spot-check on ~3 modules (confirms active-HIGH — resolved on the first module 2026-09-01, Integration §6.2a; repeat on 2 more only to confirm the batch is consistent):

    1. Module wired and lit as in step 4 (PWM floating → LED on).
    2. Jumper the PWM pin (5) to VIN− (4). The LED must go OUT. That is the whole test — no 3.3 V source needed. ⚠️ Ground the signal to VIN−, never LED−: LED− is the low side of the string and switches at the buck frequency; it is not a ground. Header map: 1 VIN+ · 2 VIN+ · 3 VIN− · 4 VIN− · 5 PWM · 6 LED+ · 7 LED−.
    3. (Superseded step — kept for context: this used to say "jumper a 3.3 V source to PWM", expecting the LED to switch off. It does not. 3.28 V is above the driver's ~2.5 V full-brightness threshold, so it commands 100 %. Only a pull below ~0.3 V shuts it down.)
    4. LED must go OFF while the jumper is held, and come back ON when it's removed. That's the confirmation.
    5. 3.3 V only. Do not poke 12 V at a PWM pin. Never leave the jumper attached as a permanent state during the current test.

    Where the 3.3 V comes from (answered 2026-08-28).

    • ✅ LiLyGO 3V3 pin — use this. Regulated, current-limited by the board's own LDO, and it's on the bench anyway for §2.3. Two jumpers: LiLyGO GND → driver GND, LiLyGO 3V3 → driver PWM. The DIM input draws microamps, so the LDO doesn't care.
    • ⚠️ A single loose 26700 LiFePO4 cell — works, but put ~1 kΩ in series. Resting 3.2–3.3 V (up to ~3.4 V fresh off a charge) is a fine logic level here. The hazard isn't the voltage, it's that at §2.1 the cell has no BMS in circuit (the pack isn't built until §2.6) — a bare LiFePO4 cell will dump tens of amps into a slipped lead. A 1 kΩ resistor in the jumper caps a dead short at ~3 mA and shifts the logic level not at all. Loose cells only — never tap one out of an assembled pack.
    • USB charger — no. It's 5 V, not 3.3 V. Likely inside the DIM pin's absolute max for this chip family, but nothing in these docs establishes that, and a build part is the wrong place to find out. You'd also have to cut a cable to reach the rails and bond a mains SMPS secondary to bench ground for zero benefit.
    • "The bench PSU's second channel" — there isn't one. The Nice-Power is a single 0–30 V / 0–5 A output (Assembly Guide §1.1), and it is already occupied at 12–13.8 V feeding the module under test. If you ever need 3.3 V off that one rail, a divider works (10 kΩ / 3.6 kΩ from 13.8 V ≈ 3.7 V into a high-impedance pin) — but the LiLyGO is simpler.

  6. Do steps 1–5 on the full-length header pins, BEFORE clipping (they are the probe points). Then clip every pin to a ≤ 2.0 mm stub and solder the leads to the stubs per Integration §10.5.9 build card: VIN+ on 1 or 2, VIN− on 3 or 4, PWM 5, LED+ 6, LED− 7.

2.4 TMP102 address mod 0x48 → 0x49

Refs: Soldering Guide §7, technique §4.5; Assembly Guide §7.1

  1. Solder the 4-pin header first (350 °C, fine chisel tip — Soldering Guide §7.2).
  2. Cut the ADD0 trace: fresh #11 blade, 3–5 light strokes; continuity-verify the cut is OPEN before proceeding.
  3. Bridge ADD0 → V+: one solder bridge, fine conical tip; continuity-verify the bridge, and verify no short to GND.
  4. On the breadboard rig: bus scan must now show 0x49 (0x48 gone). Record it. Do not epoxy — that's week 2, after the stars pass.

  2. Contacts. Keystone 5222 (−) spring in one cap, 5221 (+) button in the other — molded-case family, 10.8 × 12.7 × 0.51 mm nickel-plated steel plates, each in a 0.9 mm INBOARD pocket held by 2× M1.2 × 2.5 through its own holes. Bring each lead (18 AWG silicone) up the 5.0 × 4.0 wire slot through the pocket centre from the outboard face and solder it to the back of the plate, plate lifted clear and cells out, before seating the plate. Strain relief is off the part and load-bearing — follow Soldering §16.5 exactly (it includes a 24 h RTV cure before the cartridge goes in). On the base cap, keep links A/C, taps, loops and slack out of the nut's sweep (r ≥ 9.5, the measured 13 mm-socket sweep) — a run between two adjacent slots lies at r ≥ 13.
  3. The two clover spacers are not optional, and they are SCREWED DOWN. They locate the cells (3.61 mm of slop in the bore → 0.25 mm) and, screwed to their caps with 4× M1.2 × 3 countersunk each, stop the whole pack sliding along the rod (bench, 2026-10-06: mandatory). Screw each to its cap before the cells go in; the upper one goes floor-toward-the-cap.
  4. Load four 26700s (71 mm, not 70). DMM each first: healthy LiFePO4 rests at 3.2–3.3 V; reject anything under 2.5 V and match all four to within ~50 mV. Assembled stack is 83.42 mm — that number is derived in CAD, not hand-summed (the hand-sum said 80.5 and was wrong by 0.61 mm).
  5. Output is 5 nets — pack+, pack−, B1, B2, B3 — on a 5-pin JST-XH, into the BMS. (Unrelated to the 6-pin joint connector; this one did not change.)
  6. Fit it in the extension. The cartridge goes into one OPEN clamshell half and the other closes over it — it cannot slide in or out axially (the Ø66 caps will not pass the Ø64 neck or the Ø56 stop ring). Cell changes mean opening the clamshell.

4.2 Gated bring-up — on extension power

Basis: Bring-Up Plan Phase 4 + Phase 5, adapted: no bay, so no 0x44/0x48. Same sequence from the bench side: Bench Phases — "After Phase 3: the one-way steps, in order". The assembled extension (done in §3.5) is the supply and master for every gate — bench PSU is the fallback only. Run in order; each gate must pass before the next:

  1. Gate A — bus: scan shows exactly 0x40 + 0x49 through the assembled rails, extension-powered through the 6-pin joint connector at the +Y cradle, which feeds the three rails as a star. Nothing else should answer — no CST816 on the non-touch bench board.
  2. Gate B — channels: per-channel 0→100→0 sweep of all 12 (web UI slider or Button 2 sweep). Watch for wrong-channel mapping against the Integration §10.3 channel map.
  3. Gate C — fan: ch13 at 20 % spins; 100 % roars; firmware curve responds to a warm TMP102 (touch it with a finger).
  4. Gate D — thermal bond proof: ≤30 s full-bright must raise TMP102-wand ≥5 °C (Phase 5 criterion); flat reading = bad bond or wrong sensor.
  5. Gate E — ALL-12 full-power. ⚠️ Corrected 2026-09-01: all-12 draws ~2.4 A, not ~6.7 A, so this gate no longer requires the pack — run it on the bench PSU as soon as the wand is cabled. Run all 12 channels at 100 % (≤30 s bursts until Gate F) and verify no brownout. Then repeat it on the pack for the checks that are genuinely pack-specific: no BMS trip, no buck/ESP32 brownout under load, and observed runtime.
  6. Gate F — soak: 1 h thermal soak on battery; heatsink <70 °C with the fan curve doing the work; note battery drain rate for the runtime spec.

5.0a Waveshare LCD board port — prerequisite for everything below

The board that goes into the shell is the Waveshare ESP32-S3-LCD-1.9 (With Touch), in hand in India. The bench LiLyGO stays on the bench.   2. Wand I²C → GPIO 15 (SDA) / GPIO 16 (SCL) — a dedicated second bus. The board's broken-out "I2C" is GPIO 47/48, which is shared by the CST816 touch AND the QMI8658 IMU; keeping the wand harness off it protects the touch panel and keeps the Gate-A criterion ("exactly 0x40 + 0x49") unchanged from the LiLyGO rig. Record it in config.h.
  3. New LovyanGFX panel config (lgfx_waveshare_s3.h) — 🔴 the panel is SPI, not parallel-8 (LCD_CLK 10 / DIN 13 / DC 11 / CS 12 / RST 9 / BL 14). The LiLyGO's Bus_Parallel8 config will not drive it; use lgfx::Bus_SPI, ST7789-family, 170 × 320.
  4. 🔴 Move Button 2 off GPIO 14 — that's LCD_BL on this board. The channel-sweep self-test is currently PIN_BTN_USER 14. Reassign to GPIO 0 (BOOT), the board's only other button, or drop the physical button on this SKU and drive the sweep from the web UI.
  5. 🔴 The on-board BAT_ADC (GPIO 4) cannot read our pack — it's wired to the board's 1S LiPo connector and tops out ~4.2 V. Add an external divider across the rocker-switched 12 V rail into GPIO 3 (ADC1): 100 kΩ / 20 kΩ puts 14.6 V at 2.43 V, draws 122 µA only while the wand is on. Calibrate against a DMM.

Bench-prove the ported firmware on the breadboard rig (Phase 1, Bench Phases) before the board goes anywhere near the shell: bus scan → exactly 0x40 + 0x49 on the dedicated bus → web UI dims a real star.
