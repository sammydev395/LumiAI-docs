Print Log

One row per printed part, added on the day it comes off the bed. It answers two questions: how many copies of each part exist, and for which robot, and whether each copy predates a design change. Rows are never edited after the fact; a reprint is a new row.

  • Date: the day the print finished (YYYY-MM-DD).
  • Part: the export STL name in cad/exports/stl/ (no folder). Use one row per part, even when several print on one plate.
  • Qty: how many copies of that part came off this print.
  • For: the robot the copies are for: Ultra, Halo, Nova, Halo HV, or spare / test. Use ? until the operator says, then fill it in. The only other in-place edit allowed is a dated follow-up appended to Notes (fit, failure in use). Several copies for different robots: one row per robot.
  • STL sha: the first 16 hex characters of the sha256 of the export that was printed. This is the design version. When a book or one-off prints from a renamed copy, hash the export it came from.
  • Source: the book and plate (X1C 33, H2S 32 @ f7431ee7) or the one-off folder in cad/3dPrint/.
  • Result: ok, failed (didn't finish), or broke (finished, failed in use). Say what happened in Notes. Failed and broken copies count as printed but not in service. When a copy is taken out of use, append a dated Notes follow-up containing discarded or retired; the checker then stops counting it as in service.

Run python docs/tools/print_log_check.py to see, for every part and robot, the copies printed, the copies in service, and whether the newest copy is out of date (CHANGED since printed) because the export changed afterwards.
