🔋 Compact UPS Power System for HiWonder STM32 + Raspberry Pi 5

⚙️ System Overview

A high-efficiency, single-path UPS setup using a MZMW PSC-100-12 UPS/Charge Function Switching Power Supply, a DROK DC Buck Converter (5.3-32 V in → 9 V out, set via CV pot, 8 A continuous) that replaced the LY-KREE (the LY-KREE failed at the 12 V battery-mode input; the DROK handles the full input range in both AC and battery modes), LiFePO4 battery pack, and the Lumi Wand v2.0 LED driver assembly (PCA9685 + 12× PT4115 / 3 W 700 mA buck modules) for the gripper-held wand head. Designed for a compact robotic arm or ceiling-mounted controller assembly.

⚠️ Charging Voltage Note: The UPS CH2 outputs 13.8V @ 2A, while the LiFePO4 battery requires 14.6V for optimal charging. The battery's built-in BMS will manage charging, but charging may be slower or incomplete. Consider using an external LiFePO4 charger for full capacity charging when AC power is available.

📌 Why this PSU (and not the SC-180): an earlier iteration used a ZJIVNV SC-180-12 (180 W). It worked electrically but the AC→battery switchover was not fast enough for the STM32 + Pi 5 combination — the bus dipped long enough to brown-out the Pi during AC drops. The MZMW PSC-100-12 was substituted because it switches instantly: the Pi/STM32 see a clean transition with no reset. Field-measured current capacity on this part is also generous: despite the 5 A nameplate on CH1 it sustains 10–12 A without thermal foldback in this enclosure, so the practical headroom is closer to ~138–165 W rather than the 69 W you'd compute from the nameplate.

Note: The 4-Channel Encoder Motor Driver (for mecanum chassis) is included for development/testing purposes only and is not part of the production configuration. The LED array (gripper LEDs) is used in both development and production configurations. When using the LED array, the motor driver typically won't be running simultaneously, reducing peak power requirements.

Bay wiring + Pi5 cable-adapter BoM: see Electronics_Bay_Design.md for the STM32 controller card specs, the Pi5 stack, and the right-angle HDMI / USB-A→USB-C / USB-C→USB-C / Cat6 cable selections chosen to fit within the bay envelope.

Split-rails topology (locked 2026-06-28). The wand LED rail was previously fed from the DROK at 10.6 V (shared with STM32). Under the new split, the DROK is STM32-only at 9 V, and the wand LED rail runs direct from the PSU 13.8 V via the bay dual-bus distribution block (D-1459 design of record 2026-09-29; Halo v1 as-built used a single modified APIELE barrier strip) mounted above the PSU top. Top row = V+ (bussed with red jumper strip), bottom row = GND (bussed with black jumper strip); the 5 internal front-to-back copper conductors are removed at receipt so the two rows are electrically separate. Measured wand draw is ~2.4 A at 13.8 V (Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §5.3), inside the tether's contact rating. The split removes the wand from contention with the STM32's ~8 A of downstream 5 V load on a shared 8 A DROK, gives the drivers more Vf headroom, and avoids a buck feeding a buck. LED drivers accept 5–35 V so 13.8 V is comfortably in spec (Vf-headroom = 10.4 V over the single-3W-LED ~3.4 V Vf). See Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §5.1 and Power_Bay_Design.md §11.3 for the barrier-strip assembly + copper-conductor removal mod. (Design iterations: 2026-06-28 first-cut = Wago 221-415 lever-nut splicers → 2026-07-29 morning = APIELE dual-row barrier strip PAIR (V+ + GND, 2 blocks) → 2026-07-29 afternoon = SINGLE modified APIELE block with internal conductors removed. Each iteration halved the mount hardware.)

🔋 Runtime Estimates

Pack: ALEAIVY LiFePO4 32700 4S1P — 10 Ah, 12.8 V, 128 Wh. Usable energy at the loads is ~101 Wh: ~90 % usable capacity to the 2.5 V/cell cutoff, then ~88 % for the PSC-100-12's battery-mode conversion up to the 13.8 V rails.

Scenario Load Runtime
Robot baseline only 45 W ~2.2 h
Baseline + wand at ~30 % duty 59 W ~1.7 h
Baseline + wand all-12 full white 78 W ~1.3 h
Baseline + wand full + arm moving ~112 W ~0.9 h

⚠️ The old line here read 10 Ah ÷ 4A = ~2.5 hours — that divides pack amp-hours by a load current and ignores both the usable-capacity fraction and the conversion loss, so it ran ~15 % optimistic. Superseded 2026-09-01.

Two things the table makes obvious:

  • The wand is no longer the dominant load. At the corrected 33 W it is ~42 % of a lit-and-idle robot; under the old 84 W figure it was ~65 %, and baseline-plus-wand came to 129 W for a ~47 min runtime. Wand-on endurance improves **~1.7×** on this pack.
  • Arm movement costs about as much as the wand. The motor driver is 32–36 W typical (8 A peak), so a moving arm roughly doubles what the wand added. Motion, not illumination, is the thing to budget against on battery.

🌬️ Cooling

Cooling is bay-level only — there is no fan mounted on the PSU and no fan on the DROK. Both sit at the back of the power bay in the back-wall exhaust airflow path. See Bay-level thermal architecture below.

Bay-level thermal architecture (supersedes per-component fans)

As of 2026-05-11, the cooling architecture is bay-level, not per-component:

  • 2× Noctua NF-A4x20 PWM (40 × 40 × 20 mm, 12 V, 4-pin) mounted on the back wall of the bay fence, running as exhaust (negative bay pressure)
  • Intake through louvers on the front wall (the Rectangle.002 envelope) and — where present — the battery-tray slope louvers on the side walls
  • DROK heatsink, PSU, LED driver PCB, and other hot components sit in the airflow path. No fan-on-heatsink — the DROK's original WINSINN 20 mm blower is retired (its 11 000 RPM hydraulic-bearing tone was right in the speech band, and "no more than 8 hr/day" duty rating made it unfit for 24/7 operation anyway).
  • Battery bay gets a printed baffle so it draws parallel intake rather than series flow through the hot electronics. LiFePO4 doesn't need to bathe in hot exhaust.

Why this is better than per-component fans:

Old (per-component) New (bay-level)

Tonal frequency DROK 20 mm @ 11 000 RPM → 1–2 kHz (speech band) NF-A4x20 @ ≤4500 RPM → ~300–400 Hz (below speech fundamentals)
Redundancy None — each fan is single point of failure for its component Either fan failed → other holds bay temp; alert via tach
MTTF WINSINN ~35 000 h (printer-class), Thermalright SLA-grade Noctua SSO2 >150 000 h, 6-year warranty

There is no PSU-mounted or DROK-mounted fan in this design — bay-wall exhaust handles both.

Fan control from the RPi 5 — bay-level PWM

The 2× back-wall NF-A4x20 PWM bay fans are driven directly from the RPi 5's hardware PWM peripheral (via the RP1 chip).

Wiring per bay fan (4-pin):

  • Pin 1 (black) → GND (shared with 12 V supply GND and Pi GND at one common ground point)
  • Pin 2 (yellow) → +12 V from the PSC-100-12 CH1 dropped/regulated rail
  • Pin 3 (green) → tach back to Pi GPIO 5 or 6 with 10 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V
  • Pin 4 (blue) → PWM from Pi GPIO 12

Both fans share GPIO 12's PWM signal in parallel (Noctua PWM inputs are high-impedance — one Pi GPIO comfortably fans out to 4–5 fans before edge integrity suffers).

Behavior defaults:

  • PWM frequency: 25 kHz (Noctua spec range 21–28 kHz)
  • Min duty cycle: 30 % (below this Noctua won't reliably start from stop; runs lower once spinning)
  • Boot kick: 100 % duty for 2 s at startup to overcome static friction reliably
  • Default fail-safe: if PWM peripheral is unavailable or signal floats, the fan runs at 100 % (Noctua's internal pull-up makes "no signal" = "full speed", which is the safe failure mode)
  • Tach is open-collector: ignoring tach is fine; reading it back enables fan-failure detection (zero pulses while PWM duty > 0 for > 5 s → publish to /diagnostics)

A small ROS 2 bay_thermal_node owns the PWM output, optional tach inputs, and a thermistor read (DROK heatsink) via an ADS1115 (Ø~3.5 mm bead NTC bonded with thermal epoxy, wires routed away from mains). It publishes /bay_thermal/{drok_temp, fan1_rpm, fan2_rpm, fan_duty} at ~1 Hz and runs a simple PI loop or lookup table for duty vs temperature.

LiFePO4 cold-charge cutoff (commercial deployment)

LiFePO4 cells are sensitive to charging below freezing — current flowing into the cell at sub-0 °C plates metallic lithium on the anode, causing irreversible capacity loss. Discharging at sub-zero is fine; charging is not.

For shop / indoor / heated-garage deployments this is irrelevant. For commercial product deployment (unheated garage, northern climates, cold-storage facilities), the on-robot BMS must refuse to charge when cell temperature is below 0–5 °C (typical safe minimum is 0 °C; conservative spec is 5 °C).

The ALEAIVY LiFePO4 32700 4S1P pack we use has a built-in BMS but verify whether that BMS implements low-temperature charge cutoff — many low-end BMS chips do over-charge / over-discharge / short-circuit protection but skip the cold-charge interlock. If our BMS doesn't have it natively, options:

  • Add an external NTC thermistor on the cell pack feeding a comparator that opens the charge MOSFET below 0 °C
  • Use the existing INA226 cell-pack monitor + Pi-side firmware logic: cut the CH2 charge enable signal (small MOSFET in the charge path) when measured cell temperature is below threshold (INA226 spec/wiring: see EXTERNAL doc)
  • Specify a BMS with native low-temp cutoff for production batches (Daly, JBD smart BMS variants have this; cost adds ~$5/unit)

This goes on the production qualification checklist before any commercial unit ships.

Mains routing rule (AC inlet adjacent to bay-fan power lines)

With the AC inlet now sharing the back wall with the 2× 12 V fan power runs, mains routing matters:

  • Hot/neutral on separate channel from low-voltage signal wiring (PWM, tach, PIR signals, I²C). Print a divider rib into the bay fence to enforce physical separation — assemblers can't accidentally route signals through the AC channel.
  • IEC ground lug bonded to a chassis ground point (any metal in the base — the vendor base plate is anodized aluminum, so it's an option). Provides a safety bond and a known reference for EMC behavior.
  • Strain relief on the IEC cable inside the bay — the inlet body should never carry mechanical load from the AC cord
  • Keep fan +12 V leads physically separated from the IEC inlet's hot/neutral by at least 6 mm of air or 1.5 mm of insulated barrier (matches UL creepage / clearance for SELV vs mains separation in low-voltage equipment)

For Phase 2/3 production this is a CE / UL pre-compliance item.

🧰 Assembly / Mounting Tips

  • Mount PSC-100-12 and DROK converter on an aluminum or polycarbonate base for airflow.
  • Add a 10 A inline glass fuse (6x30mm) in fuse holder between battery positive and UPS CH2 BAT+.
  • Keep battery leads short (< 20 cm). Battery connects directly to UPS CH2 via 10A fuse on positive side.
  • Mount converter case on metal for heat spreading.
  • Tie all grounds (PSC-100-12, converter, STM32, Pi, Motor Driver, LED Driver) together using 14 AWG wire.
  • Verify output voltage (9.0 V ± 0.1) before connecting loads.
  • Motor Driver Power: Connect 2-pin power port directly to CH1 (12V) output via power distribution block or Y-splitter.

🔌 Fuse Selection

Selected Fuse System:

  • Fuse Holder: uxcell 6x30mm Inline Screw Type Fuse Holder
  • Wire: 16 AWG Red Wire (rated for ~22A, suitable for 10A fuse)
  • Fuse Type: 6x30mm Glass Fast Blow Fuse
  • Rating: 10A
  • Installation: Screw terminals for easy wire connections, replaceable glass fuse design

Why This Fuse:

  • 16 AWG wire provides adequate current capacity (~22A) for 10A fuse with good safety margin
  • Glass fuse allows visual inspection to check if fuse is blown
  • Screw terminals provide secure connections
  • Compact design compared to bulky waterproof ATC/ATO holder
  • 6x30mm is a standard glass fuse size, readily available

Note: The 10A fuse is oversized for the 2A charging current, but provides headroom for battery discharge current and is a common automotive fuse rating. For charging-only applications, a 3A or 5A fuse would be more appropriate.

Why 5.5×2.1mm Barrel:

  • Compact: The 5.5×2.1mm barrel takes up less space than an XT60 — useful in the tight power-bay layout
  • Standard DC Barrel: A common, readily-available standard DC barrel connector
  • Adequate for this path: Works fine for the ≤~10 A battery charge/discharge path on UPS CH2
  • Easy Disconnect: Battery can be quickly disconnected via the barrel connector for replacement

Connection Method:

  • Battery comes with 5.5×2.1mm female barrel connector pre-installed
  • Use 5.5×2.1mm male barrel connector with wire pigtail (16-18 AWG wire) for connection to UPS CH2
  • Positive wire (center pin) connects to UPS CH2 BAT+ through 10A fuse
  • Negative wire (outer sleeve) connects to UPS CH2 BAT-
  • 10A fuse installed between barrel connector positive wire and UPS CH2 BAT+ (soldered connection)

  • One-converter design (no dual bucks, no wiring mess).
  • LiFePO4 battery with built-in BMS provides safety and protection.
  • Safer than regular LiPo - more stable chemistry, longer cycle life.
  • Higher energy density than SLA batteries.
  • Full UPS behavior via PSC-100-12 (instant switchover).

🚗 Motor Driver Power Connection (Development/Testing Only)

⚠️ IMPORTANT: The 4-Channel Encoder Motor Driver is included for development and testing purposes only. It is NOT part of the production configuration. Production systems will only include the STM32 controller, Raspberry Pi 5, and gripper LEDs.

4-Channel Encoder Motor Driver (HiWonder Product)

Power Specifications:

  • VM Power Supply Range: 5–15 V (perfect for 12V from PSC-100-12 CH1)
  • Single Channel Rated Current: 2 A continuous
  • Single Channel Peak Current: 2.5 A
  • Total Maximum Current: 8 A continuous (4 channels × 2 A), 10 A peak
  • Power Port: 2-pin connector (VM+ and GND)

Connection Method:

  1. Power Distribution: Split CH1 (12V) output using a power distribution block or Y-splitter

    • One branch → DROK converter (for STM32/Pi5)
    • One branch → Motor Driver via spade quick-splice off the power-switch output

  2. Wiring:

    PSC-100-12 CH1 (12V) ──► Power Distribution Block
                         ├─► DROK converter (9V output)
                         └─► Power Switch OUTPUT ──► brass piggyback / quick-splice spade
                               (+ and ground) ──► 14 AWG soldered to Motor Driver 2-pin power port

  3. Ground Connection: Ensure motor driver GND is connected to common ground (PSC-100-12, converter, STM32, Pi5)

🔌 Motor Driver Connector: Spade (piggyback brass quick-splice)

The Part: Piggyback Spade Connectors — 3-Way Adapter, Dual Male to Single Female, Brass Quick-Splice Spade Terminal.

Why Spade:

  • Fits the bay: The previous bullet/barrel-style board connector was too physically large/awkward for the tight power bay; the brass piggyback spade quick-splice is compact and low-profile.
  • No main-run cut: The 3-way dual-male→single-female adapter piggybacks the dev-only motor branch directly onto the existing power-switch OUTPUT terminals — no cutting or desoldering the main distribution wire.
  • Tool-free: Quick-splice push-on/pull-off; easy to add or remove the dev/test motor driver without rework.
  • Brass: Good conductivity and current capacity for the dev-only motor load (≤8 A typical, 10 A peak).

The Method:

  1. Remove Header Pins: Remove the original 2-pin header from the motor driver PCB.
  2. Solder Wires: Solder a 5–10 cm length of 14 AWG wire directly to the motor driver 2-pin power port pads (VM+ and GND).
  3. Spade Terminals: Crimp/attach a spade terminal to the free end of each 14 AWG wire.
  4. Piggyback Quick-Splice: Use the brass 3-way piggyback adapter (dual-male → single-female) to quick-splice each motor-driver lead onto the corresponding power-switch OUTPUT terminal — one on positive, one on ground. No inline barrel connector, no cut to the main distribution wire.

🛠️ Strain Relief for PCB Soldering:

When soldering 14 AWG directly to a PCB (after removing header pins), the wire is much heavier than the board's original traces were meant to support. Vibration can cause copper pads to "lift" or the wire to snap at the solder joint.

Stress Relief Methods:

  1. Stress Loop: After soldering, create a small stress loop in the wire before it exits the board area

  2. Silicone/Adhesive: Use a small dab of neutral-cure electronics-grade silicone (or high-temp hot glue) at the base where the wire meets the board to provide strain relief

  3. Zip Tie Support: If the board has mounting holes, zip-tie the 14 AWG wire to a nearby pillar or hole so that any "tugging" on the wire pulls on the zip-tie, not the solder joint

  4. Mounting: Ensure the board is securely mounted to prevent vibration-induced stress on solder joints

  5. Current Considerations:

    • CH1 Load Current: Up to 13.5 A (162 W max) at 12V — this is the load/output current capability
    • CH2 Charging Current: 1.5 A at 13.8V — this is for charging batteries
    • DROK converter: ~7.5 A typical at 12V input (at full load, ~60 W output, accounting for ~85% efficiency)
    • Motor Driver: 2.7–3.0 A typical (all 4 motors running at 8–9 W each = 32–36 W total), up to 8 A peak (~96 W)
    • Total typical load: ~10.2–10.5 A, well within the 13.5 A load capacity
    • Headroom: ~3–3.3 A available for additional loads or peak motor activity
    • The PSC-100-12 has sufficient capacity to power both the converter and motor driver simultaneously without issues

  6. I2C Connection: Motor driver connects to controller via I2C port (separate from power)

    • I2C port provides 5V output (1A max) for powering controller if needed
    • SDA/SCL for motor control communication

  7. RPi5 CPU heatsink fan power — STM32 I²C port (ONE-OFF repair for this unit, decided 2026-05-22):

    ⚠️ NOT the production design. Production / standard build powers this fan from the RPi5's own dedicated 4-pin fan header (JST-SH 1.0 mm — gives 5V + firmware PWM + tach, and does not touch the 40-pin header). This I²C-port tap is a one-off workaround for THIS specific unit only, because its Pi5 fan header is physically broken/dead. Do not propagate it to other units or to the BOM/assembly spec.

    The RPi5 active-cooler / heatsink fan is a 5 V, always-on, 2-wire fan (<200 mA). On this unit it is NOT powered from:

    • the RPi5's dedicated 4-pin fan header — that header is physically broken/dead on this unit (the reason for this one-off; the prod path above); or
    • the 40-pin GPIO header 5V pins — those pins are being freed for the FFC header-extension into the power bay (see ../EXTERNAL_SENSOR_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md).
    • Source = the second (free) STM32 I²C port: 5V + GND pins (the mecanum motor driver occupies the first I²C port; the I²C-port 5V rail is rated 1A max per item 5 above — the fan's <200 mA fits with margin). The fan is hard-on whenever the controller switch is ON; no PWM (the Pi-side PWM/tach is gone with the broken header — acceptable for a CPU fan).
    • Wiring = one dedicated fan→I²C-port wire pair. NO Y-tap / no splice into the Pi 5V rail (rejected: cabling is already congested and bay space is tight; there is plenty of room to route a single clean wire run from the fan to the I²C port).
    • Do NOT use the 3V3 header pin (the core board derives 3V3 from +5V via a small RT9013-33 LDO, ~500 mA, and 3.3V won't reliably start a 5V fan — per the STM32 core-board schematic armpi_ultra/docs/.../SCH_Schematic1_2023-09-20.pdf) or the bus-servo ports (full input voltage 7.4–12 V — would destroy a 5V fan).
    • Bench check before connecting: meter the chosen I²C-port pin for ~5 V (the expansion headers bring out both +5V and 3V3 — confirm you're on +5V, not 3V3).

UPS Behavior:

  • During AC power: Motor driver receives 12V from CH1
  • During battery backup: CH1 continues to provide 12V (powered by battery via UPS)
  • Motor driver continues operating seamlessly during power transitions

🔔 Battery Voltage Monitoring

LiFePO4 Battery Voltage Monitoring:

  • LiFePO4 battery has built-in 4S balanced BMS for cell-level protection
  • BMS provides overcharge, over-discharge, and short-circuit protection
  • Monitor battery voltage via UPS CH2 or add a simple voltage monitor across battery terminals
  • Recommended minimum voltage: ~10V (BMS will cut off to prevent over-discharge)
  • Full charge voltage: 14.6V (optimal, but UPS CH2 provides 13.8V)
  • Nominal voltage: 12.8V

Optional Voltage Monitor:

  • Simple 12V battery voltage monitor/alarm can be added across battery terminals
  • Set alarm threshold to ~11V to provide warning before BMS cut-off
  • Provides visual/audible alert when battery voltage drops too low
  • BMS will automatically disconnect battery if voltage drops too low to prevent damage

💡 LED Power Branch — Lumi Wand v2.0

The LED branch on this UPS now powers the Lumi Wand v2.0 assembly: a 12-channel constant-current LED driver feeding 3× RGBW LED stars in a CNC-machined aluminum heatsink, packaged in a printed PETG handle that the arm gripper holds. The legacy single-board IRFZ44N MOSFET driver is retired.

Architecture:

PSC-100-12 CH1 (12 V) ──► Master Power Switch ──► Power Distribution Block
                                                    ├─► DROK buck (9 V → STM32/Pi5)
                                                    └─► DROK adjustable buck (set to 10.6 V)
                                                            │
                                                            ▼
                                                    arm tether: 6 nets, 2×3-pin at 13.8 V
                                                            │
                                                            ▼
                                                    Wand handle (PETG clamshell):
                                                       PCA9685 (I²C addr 0x40)
                                                       12× PT4115 buck driver modules
                                                            │
                                                            ▼ 24-wire 26 AWG silicone bundle
                                                    Wand head (CNC heatsink):
                                                       3× 4-channel RGBW LED stars
                                                       (12 LEDs total, ~33 W peak)

Key specs:

  • Driver IC per channel: PT4115 (or 3 W 700 mA XL4001E-class equivalent — see Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §6.2 for module-sourcing notes)
  • Channel current: 700 mA constant
  • Channel count: 12 (3 stars × R/G/B/W)
  • Total peak power: ~33 W (Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §5.3, measured 2026-09-01) (this section's "~84 W" was wrong even for the retired architecture it describes — same LED-side-current × rail-voltage error)
  • Supply: 10.6 V from DROK buck (bumped from the legacy 9 V; the higher rail keeps the PT4115 happy at 700 mA over the LED Vf)
  • Control: I²C from RPi 5 → PCA9685 PWM → DIM input on each PT4115
  • Custom PCB: kicad/Lumi_Wand_12CH_Driver_PROD/ (12-channel production board, in fab — gerbers in kicad/gerbers_prod_v2/)

Why this architecture:

  • Constant current (PT4115) instead of PWM low-side switching (IRFZ44N) — LED brightness is stable and color-accurate; doesn't shift with supply droop or LED Vf drift over temperature.
  • 12 independent channels (vs. the old 4-channel single-RGBW MOSFET board) — drives 3 stars individually, enabling beam-shaping, colour mixing per-emitter, and per-star fault isolation.
  • I²C control (1 PCA9685 vs. 12 GPIO pins) — keeps the tether thin: 6 nets in two 3-pin legs (13.8 V / GND-power / GND-logic + SDA / SCL / 3V3).
  • Gripper-held handle — no permanent end-effector mod; the arm grasps the wand's PETG handle inside its 55 mm jaw envelope and can drop it for tool-change.

For mechanical assembly, thermal-bonding the LED stars to the CNC heatsink, lens fitment, and the printed alignment fixture, see Lumi_Wand_Integration.md. For the CNC heatsink and base-plate machining, see CNC_Machining_Guide.md. For the printed parts (handle, head cap, alignment fixture), see 3D_Printing_Guide.md.
