Multi-Robot Bench Power Rail – UPDATED (ArmPi Mini, ArmPi FPV, ArmPi Ultra, ROSMASTER X3 Plus)

Single 12 V 1500 W PSU • Kohree 12-Way Fuse Block • ArmPi Ultra DROK Buck Converter + Motor Driver • Yahboom 12 V LiPo + 19 V UPS-C • 3× USB-C Touch Displays • NETGEAR GS305 Ethernet Switch

1. System Overview

This document describes the complete power rail for four robots mounted along a bench:

  • Hiwonder ArmPi Mini (Raspberry Pi 5 + STM32 + Waveshare UPS 3S, 12 V input)
  • Hiwonder ArmPi FPV (Raspberry Pi 5 + STM32 + Waveshare UPS 3S, 12 V input)
  • Hiwonder ArmPi Ultra (Raspberry Pi 5 + STM32 via DROK buck converter + 4-Channel Encoder Motor Driver, 12 V input)
  • Yahboom ROSMASTER X3 Plus (Jetson Orin Nano, internal 9600 mAh Li-Po pack, UPS-C module)

Additionally, the system powers three AOC 16T15 15.6" IPS touch displays via USB-C ports (5 V required) and a NETGEAR GS305 5-Port Gigabit Ethernet Switch (5 V, 0.7 A required).

The system is powered by a single HONGPOE MS-1500 adjustable switching power supply set to 12 V, feeding a Kohree 12-way marine fuse block. Main distribution uses Fermerry 12 AWG silicone wire. Robot drops use 16 AWG wire (except ArmPi Ultra which uses 14 AWG). Display and ethernet switch power is provided via a single 12 V to 5 V buck converter with USB-C outputs for displays and adapter connection for the switch.

IMPORTANT UPDATES:

  1. Yahboom ROSMASTER X3 Plus: In addition to the existing 19 V path (via the DROK buck-boost converter → UPS-C → Jetson Orin Nano), the robot also requires a dedicated 12 V feed to its internal Li-Po/BMS charge input.

  2. ArmPi Mini and ArmPi FPV: Both robots now require separate 12 V feeds - one for the Waveshare UPS 3S (which powers the Raspberry Pi 5) and one for the Hiwonder STM32 expansion board. This provides better power isolation and allows independent operation of the UPS and motor control systems.

  3. ArmPi Ultra: Added power distribution for ArmPi Ultra robot with dual 12 V inputs via XT30 connectors:

    • Input 1 - Main PSU: 12 V → 15 A fused branch → 14 AWG drop → XT30 Male → XT30 Female (on Master Power Switch) - connected when AC mains available
    • Input 2 - UPS Battery: UPS CH1 (12V @ 13.5A) → 14 AWG → XT30 Male → XT30 Female (on Master Power Switch) - connected when AC mains fails
    • Note: Only ONE input is connected to the power switch at any given time (either main PSU OR UPS battery, not both)
    • After Switch: Master Power Switch (DC) → Power Distribution Block → Splits to DROK buck converter (9 V 12 A output, with 20mm cooling fan) → HiWonder STM32 controller → Raspberry Pi 5, and 4-Channel Encoder Motor Driver (5–15 V input, 8 A continuous, 10 A peak, powered from 9V) → Mecanum Chassis Motors
    • AC Power to UPS: 120V AC (14 AWG) → MR30 Male 3-Pin → MR30 Female 3-Pin (on UPS AC input)
    • Master power switch provides convenient on/off control and seamless switching between main PSU and UPS battery backup

  4. USB-C Touch Displays + Ethernet Switch: Three AOC 16T15 portable monitors are powered via USB-C ports, each requiring 5 V at 2 A (10 W per display). A NETGEAR GS305 5-Port Gigabit Ethernet Switch is also powered from the same converter, requiring 5 V at 0.7 A (3.5 W). Power is derived from the 12 V bus using a single XWST DC-DC 12V/24V to 5V Buck Converter (20 A output capacity) with USB-C pigtail connections for displays and adapter connection for the switch.

3. Detailed Per-Branch Wiring

3.1 Main Supply and Distribution

  • HONGPOE MS-1500 PSU set to 12.0 V output.
  • 10 AWG wire from PSU output → 40 A MIDI fuse (in MIDI fuse holder) → Kohree 12-way fuse block input.
  • MIDI fuse holder rated for 30–200 A, splash-proof, flame retardant, provides circuit protection.
  • MIDI fuses rated: 40 A, 58 V DC (well above 12 V operating voltage, providing excellent safety margin).
  • Kohree block provides fused 12 V outputs and a common negative bus for all loads.
  • Negative/Ground Connections: All negative/ground wires from loads (robots, buck converters, etc.) connect to the common negative bus on the Kohree fuse block. This is typically the negative terminal(s) at the top of the block or the dedicated negative bus bar running along the side of the block.

Fan replacement — Noctua (build note, validated 2026-05): swap worn/noisy rail fans for Noctuas (e.g. NF-A6x25 FLX).

  • Wiring recipe: keep the fan's factory connector, fit a Noctua NA-EC1 extension cable, cut its far (source) end, crimp a JST-XH 2-pin (+12 V + GND; tach wire unused), and run it to a fused 12 V branch on the Kohree block. Do not mate a homemade connector directly to the fan's own plug — use the NA-EC1 so the fan keeps its factory connector and only the source end is your XH (a homemade plug into Noctua's shrouded housing can seat without making contact).
  • Vibration first: fit the Noctua silicone anti-vibration mounts (in the box) instead of hard screws — that alone cut perceived noise ≈ 50 % (the PSU case was acting as a sounding board).
  • Tune RPM per-fan, not globally (this corrects the earlier "skip the resistor adapters entirely" guidance): run well-located fans at full 3 K; for a fan that's noisy because of its position / the PSU's airflow geometry, drop only that one with a Noctua speed reducer (RC12 / LNA, ~3 K → 1.6 K). Validated: reducing the single offending fan to 1.6 K (other left at 3 K) took the PSU to zero audible noise with negligible cooling loss — the residual noise was one fan resonating/churning against its spot, not the fan itself. So don't blanket-reduce (loses airflow in a warm garage) and don't blanket-full (leaves the problem fan screaming) — reduce the problem fan only.

3.2 ArmPi Mini Branch (Hiwonder ArmPi Mini)

The ArmPi Mini requires TWO separate 12 V power paths:

(1) 12 V for Waveshare UPS 3S

Path: 12 V from Kohree fuse block → [5 A ATC fuse in the block] → 16 AWG drop along the bench → 12 V input of Waveshare UPS 3S → UPS 3S → 5 V / system power to Raspberry Pi 5

(2) 12 V for STM32 Expansion Board

Path: 12 V from Kohree fuse block → [5 A ATC fuse in the block] → 16 AWG drop along the bench → 12 V input of Hiwonder STM32 expansion board

Notes:

  • Keep ground shared with the main bus for both branches.
  • Both branches are independently fused at 5 A each.
  • Barrel connectors are used at the robot end (see "Connectors at Robot End" below).

Connectors at Robot End:

  • STM32 Board: 3.0mm × 1.1mm male barrel connector with 20 AWG pigtail
  • UPS 3S: 5.5mm × 2.1mm male barrel connector with 16 AWG pigtail

3.4 ArmPi Ultra Branch (Hiwonder ArmPi Ultra)

The ArmPi Ultra uses TWO 12 V power inputs that connect to a master power switch (DC), which then splits to feed the DROK buck converter (which powers STM32/Pi5 and Motor Driver) and the LED Driver. The UPS provides seamless power switching between AC mains (via main PSU) and battery backup.

Power Path: Input 1 - Main PSU (12V from Kohree fuse block): 12 V from Kohree fuse block → [15 A ATC fuse in the block] → 14 AWG drop along the bench → XT30 Male connector → XT30 Female connector (on Master Power Switch input)

Input 2 - UPS Battery Backup (13.8V from PSC-100-12 / PSC-150-12 CH1): UPS CH1 (13.8V @ 5.0A) → XT30 Male connector → XT30 Female connector (on Master Power Switch input)

After Power Switch: Master Power Switch (DC) at robot location → Power Distribution Block (after switch) → Splits to two branches:

  • Branch 1: DROK buck converter (9 V 12 A output, with 20mm cooling fan) → HiWonder STM32 controller → Raspberry Pi 5 (5 V via STM32), and 4-Channel Encoder Motor Driver (9 V input) → Mecanum Chassis Motors (Development/Testing only)
  • Branch 2: Lumi Wand v2.0 LED driver (PCA9685 12-ch I2C + 12× PT4115 / 3W 700 mA buck modules) → 3× RGBW LED stars (gripper holds the wand handle) (Production)

DROK Buck Converter Specifications:

  • Model: DROK DC Buck Converter (5.3-32V to 1.2-32V, 12A adjustable)
  • Input Voltage: 5.3–32 V (handles both 12V battery and 14.2V AC modes)
  • Output Voltage: 1.2–32 V adjustable (set to 9 V)
  • Output Current: 8 A continuous, 12 A with cooling
  • Output Power: 120 W continuous, 160 W max with cooling
  • Efficiency: ~95%
  • Size: 82×52×32mm
  • Features: LCD display (shows input/output voltage, current, power), CV/CC potentiometers, ON/OFF button
  • Cooling: WINSINN 20mm blower fan (20×20×6mm, 12V, 0.05A) mounted on heatsink
  • Input Current at 12V/14.2V: ~5.4 A typical (depending on load and efficiency)
  • Note: Replaces LY-KREE ZS240910 to enable operation in both AC (14.2V) and battery (12V) modes. LY-KREE requires minimum 12.1–12.2V input and fails when UPS outputs 12V in battery mode.

4-Channel Encoder Motor Driver Specifications:

  • Model: HiWonder 4-Channel Encoder Motor Driver
  • VM Power Supply Range: 5–15 V (operates from 9 V bus via DROK converter)
  • Single Channel Rated Current: 2 A continuous
  • Single Channel Peak Current: 2.5 A
  • Total Maximum Current: 8 A continuous (4 channels × 2 A), 10 A peak
  • Power Port: 2-pin connector (VM+ and GND)
  • Note: Development/Testing only - NOT included in production configuration. Motor driver now receives 9V from DROK converter (motor driver accepts 5-15V, 9V is within range).

Lumi Wand v2.0 LED Driver Specifications:

  • Architecture: PCA9685 16-channel I²C PWM driver (12 channels in use) → 12× constant-current buck driver modules (one per LED channel) → 3× 4-channel RGBW LED stars
  • Driver modules: PT4115 single-channel CC bucks running at 700 mA each (or equivalent 3 W 700 mA XL4001E-class modules — see Lumi_Wand_Integration.md)
  • LED stars: 3× RGBW (one R, one G, one B, one W per star = 12 channels total)
  • Input Voltage: 10.6 V (via DROK buck on the wand bench supply, bumped from the legacy 9 V — see DROK voltage memory).
  • Control: I²C from RPi 5 → PCA9685 → 12× PWM-DIM into PT4115 modules
  • Peak Power: ~33 W at the input (~27 W of LED) (Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §5.3, measured 2026-09-01).
  • Mechanical: custom CNC-machined aluminum heatsink, PETG handle clamshell, the gripper holds the wand handle
  • Wire Gauge: 26 AWG silicone bundle (24 cores; palette 2026-09-30 — earlier revisions said 24 or 28 AWG) from driver PCB up the wand body to the LED stars
  • Custom KiCad PCB: kicad/Lumi_Wand_12CH_Driver_PROD/ (12-channel production board, in fab)
  • Replaces: the legacy single-board IRFZ44N MOSFET driver (low-side switching, 4-channel RGBW only). The old design is no longer used in production.

Master Power Switch:

  • Type: DC power switch (similar to Compact UPS system)
  • Location: Mounted at robot location, receives 12V input via XT30 connector
  • Function: Provides convenient on/off control for entire ArmPi Ultra system
  • Rating: Must handle combined load (15 A minimum recommended)
  • Input Connector: Single XT30 Female connector (accepts either main PSU 12V OR UPS battery 12V, but not both simultaneously)

12V Power Input Connectors (XT30):

  • Main PSU 12V Line: XT30 Male connector on 14 AWG wire from Kohree fuse block (12V from main PSU)
  • UPS Battery 13.8V Line: XT30 Male connector on UPS CH1 output (13.8V @ 5.0A)
  • Power Switch Side: Single XT30 Female connector (accepts either input - selector switch operation)
  • Why XT30: Compact connector rated for 30A, perfect for 12V/13.8V power connections. Smaller than XT60, ideal for power switch input.
  • Operation: Either the main PSU 12V line OR the UPS battery 13.8V line is connected to the power switch at any given time (not both simultaneously)

Notes:

  • Two 14 AWG lines run to robot location (one from main PSU, one from UPS CH1)
  • Both inputs use XT30 Male connectors; power switch has single XT30 Female connector
  • Only one power source is connected at a time (main PSU when AC is available, UPS battery when AC fails)
  • Master power switch allows turning entire robot on/off without accessing fuse block
  • UPS provides seamless power switching between AC mains and battery backup
  • Power distribution block splits single 12 V feed (after switch) to DROK converter and LED Driver (Production). DROK converter then powers STM32/Pi5 and Motor Driver (Dev/Test) from its 9V output.
  • Keep ground shared with the main bus
  • Production load: ~5.4–6.4 A typical at 13.8V/12V (DROK converter ~5.4A + LED Driver <1A), within 15 A fuse rating. UPS CH1 provides 5.0A continuous but handles brief spikes above this due to superior transient response.
  • Development/Testing load: ~8.1–8.4 A typical at 13.8V/12V with motor driver, or ~5.4–6.4 A typical with LED array (motor driver and LED array typically don't run simultaneously). Motor current spikes are handled by capacitors and PSU transient response.
  • The DROK buck converter provides isolated 9 V power for the STM32/RPi5 system and motor driver
  • The motor driver operates from 9 V (via DROK converter) for stable operation in both AC and battery modes (Development/Testing only)
  • Cooling: Both PSC-100-12 / PSC-150-12 PSU and DROK converter require cooling fans.
    • PSU top fan (replaced 2026-05-09): original Thermalright TL-B6B 60×60×25 mm (~25 dB at 3000 RPM, audibly noisy at the bench) → Noctua NF-A6x25 FLX 60×60×25 mm 12 V 3-pin (1600/3000 RPM, 19.3 dB max, ships with Low-Noise + Ultra-Low-Noise adapters that drop it further to ~13 dB). Drop-in: same 60×60×25 mm form factor, same 12 V supply, same 4-corner M3 mount, same 3-pin connector. Result: zero audible noise at the bench. Same swap is planned for the Compact UPS / robot PSU — see Compact_UPS_Power_System_HiWonder_RPi5.md.
    • DROK fan: WINSINN 20mm blower (unchanged) — enables 12A operation on the DROK heatsink.
  • The LED driver powers gripper LEDs / power LED held in the gripper setup (Production)
  • For more details on the DROK buck converter setup, STM32 power distribution, and LED driver, see Compact_UPS_Power_System_HiWonder_RPi5.md in this Power folder (or docs/Power/Compact_UPS_Power_System_HiWonder_RPi5.md from repo root).

AC Power Connection to UPS (110V Line):

  • AC Input: 120V AC (14 AWG) runs alongside ArmPi Ultra 12V drop from main AC distribution
  • Connector: MR30 3-pin connector system
    • MR30 Male 3-Pin: On AC power line (110V/120V AC input)
    • MR30 Female 3-Pin: On PSC-100-12 / PSC-150-12 UPS AC input
  • Connection: MR30 male plugs into MR30 female on UPS to provide AC power to the UPS
  • Purpose: Supplies AC mains power to the PSC-100-12 / PSC-150-12 UPS for normal operation and battery charging
  • Wire Gauge: 14 AWG for AC line (runs parallel to 12V DC drop)

Connectors at Robot End:

  • AC Power Drop Line: Wiring nuts (temporary), will replace with MR60 connector when available
  • 12V/13.8V Power Lines (Dual Input):
    • Main PSU 12V: XT30 Male connector on 14 AWG wire from Kohree fuse block (12V from main PSU)
    • UPS Battery 13.8V: XT30 Male connector on UPS CH1 output (13.8V @ 5.0A, 14 AWG wire)
    • Power Switch Input: Single XT30 Female connector (accepts either main PSU 12V OR UPS battery 13.8V, but not both simultaneously)
  • Connection Method: Either the main PSU XT30 Male (12V) OR the UPS battery XT30 Male (13.8V) plugs into the single XT30 Female connector on the Master Power Switch, allowing seamless switching between main PSU and UPS battery backup

3.5 Yahboom ROSMASTER X3 Plus – UPDATED Dual Feed

The Yahboom ROSMASTER X3 Plus requires TWO power paths:

(1) 12 V for Internal Li-Po/BMS (NEW BRANCH)

The robot includes a 9600 mAh Li-Po battery and onboard BMS, which expects a 12 V supply for charging. The 12 V feed charges the battery, and the battery then powers the Yahboom ROS robot control board V3.0 (STM32F103RCT6 expansion board), which serves as the motor controller and peripheral interface board.

Yahboom STM32 Expansion Board Details:

  • Model: ROS robot control board V3.0 with STM32F103RCT6 (Yahboom YB-ERF01-V3.0)
  • Power Input: Single 12 V DC input via T-type connector (connected to 9600 mAh Li-Po battery)
  • Power Outputs:
    • 2× DC 12V outputs (for motors and other 12V peripherals)
    • 1× DC 5V output
    • 1× Type-C 5V output (Raspberry Pi 5 power supply protocol)
  • Features: 4-channel encoder motor drive, 4-channel PWM servo control, serial bus servo support, 9-axis IMU (ICM20948), CAN bus communication
  • Standby Current: ~50 mA (board itself draws minimal power)
  • Load: The board receives power from the battery via its 12V input, then distributes it via its outputs to motors, servos, and other peripherals, accounting for the higher current draw on this branch

Power Path: 12 V from Kohree fuse block → [7.5 A ATC fuse] → 16 AWG drop to Yahboom main DC input / BMS charge input → Charges 9600 mAh Li-Po battery via BMS → Battery connects to STM32 board's 12V input (T-type connector) → STM32 board distributes battery power via its outputs:

  • 2× 12V outputs → motors and 12V peripherals
  • 1× 5V output → 5V peripherals
  • 1× Type-C 5V output → Raspberry Pi 5 (if used)

(2) 19 V Path for Jetson Orin Nano via UPS-C (EXISTING)

The Jetson Orin Nano is powered through a Waveshare UPS-Power-Module-(C), which expects 15–19 V input. To supply this, a DROK buck-boost converter is used to boost from 12 V to 19 V. This powers the Jetson Orin Nano along with all peripherals: 2 cameras (monocular + stereo RGB/IR), void output box, 7DOF arm, Lidar, and 4-wheel mecanum chassis.

Path: 12 V from Kohree fuse block → [10 A ATC fuse] → 10–12 AWG into DROK DC 6–30 V → 0.5–30 V buck/boost module → DROK configured to output 19.0 V → 19 V into Waveshare UPS-C input → UPS-C → regulated 19 V to Jetson Orin Nano SOC board and all peripherals.

Grounding:

  • The 12 V feed (Li-Po/BMS) and the 19 V feed (UPS-C) must share common ground back at the Kohree fuse block negative bus to avoid reference issues.

Connectors at Robot End:

  • Li-Po/BMS Branch: 5.5mm × 2.5mm 90-degree barrel connector with 16 AWG pigtail
  • 19V (DROK) Branch: 4.0mm × 1.7mm 90-degree barrel connector with 16 AWG pigtail

3.6 USB-C Touch Display Power (3× AOC 16T15 Monitors) + NETGEAR GS305 Ethernet Switch

All three AOC 16T15 15.6" IPS touch displays and the NETGEAR GS305 5-Port Gigabit Ethernet Switch are powered from a single XWST DC-DC 12V/24V to 5V Buck Converter (20 A output capacity). Each display requires 5 V at 2 A (10 W power consumption). The NETGEAR GS305 switch requires 5 V at 0.7 A (3.5 W power consumption).

Power Path: 12 V from Kohree fuse block → [10 A ATC fuse in the block] → 12 AWG or 14 AWG drop to converter location → XWST DC-DC Buck Converter (12 V input, 5 V output) → 5 V output from converter (20 A capacity, ~6.7 A used: 6 A for 3 displays + 0.7 A for switch) → USB-C pigtail connections (3× USB-C pigtails wired to converter output) → Display USB-C power inputs → 5 V DC barrel connector or USB-C adapter (wired to converter output) → NETGEAR GS305 switch power input

Buck Converter Specifications:

  • Model: XWST DC-DC 12V/24V to 5V Buck Converter
  • Input voltage: 10–32 V DC (operates from 12 V bus)
  • Output voltage: 5 V DC (fixed)
  • Maximum output current: 20 A
  • Maximum output power: 100 W
  • Protection: Over-voltage, over-current, reverse connection, overload, over-temperature, short-circuit protection

USB-C Pigtail Wiring:

  • Each USB-C pigtail has 2-pin bare wire ends (red/positive and black/negative)
  • Connect pigtail red wire to 5 V+ output terminal on buck converter
  • Connect pigtail black wire to GND/negative terminal on buck converter
  • USB-C connector end plugs directly into display USB-C power port

NETGEAR GS305 Switch Power:

  • Model: NETGEAR 5-Port Gigabit Ethernet Unmanaged Essentials Switch (GS305)
  • Power requirement: 5 V at 0.7 A (3.5 W)
  • Power source: XWST buck converter 5 V output (shared with displays)
  • Connection: Wire 5 V+ and GND from converter output terminals to switch power input (typically via barrel connector adapter or USB-C adapter, depending on switch model)

Notes:

  • Single converter powers all 3 displays + ethernet switch (total load: 6.7 A at 5 V, well within 20 A capacity)
  • Converter should be mounted in a central location or near the displays and switch
  • Ensure proper heat sinking/ventilation for the converter (100 W capacity, ~33.5 W actual load)
  • Use quality USB-C pigtails rated for 3 A minimum (22 AWG is acceptable for 2 A per display)
  • For switch power connection, use appropriate adapter (barrel connector or USB-C) or wire directly if switch accepts bare wire input
  • All negative/ground connections from converter, displays, and switch connect back to Kohree fuse block negative bus

4.3 Power Supply Capacity Analysis

PSU Specifications:

  • Voltage: 0–24 V adjustable (set to 12 V)
  • Current: 0.5–62.5 A adjustable
  • Maximum power at 12 V: 12 V × 62.5 A = 750 W

Capacity Assessment:

  • Typical load: 30.8 A / 62.5 A = 49% of PSU capacity (with motor driver) or 28.0 A / 62.5 A = 45% of PSU capacity (with LED driver, production)
  • Peak load: 55.6 A / 62.5 A = 89% of PSU capacity (with motor driver) or 48.1 A / 62.5 A = 77% of PSU capacity (with LED driver, production)
  • PSU Headroom: 6.9 A remaining (11% capacity) at peak load with motor driver, or 14.4 A remaining (23% capacity) with LED driver

Master Fuse Analysis:

  • Master fuse: 60–70 A MIDI fuse (upgraded from 40 A)
  • Typical load: 30.8 A / 60 A = 51% of fuse capacity (with 60 A fuse, motor driver) or 28.0 A / 60 A = 47% of fuse capacity (with 60 A fuse, LED driver)
  • Peak load: 55.6 A / 60 A = 93% of fuse capacity (with 60 A fuse, motor driver, at limit) or 48.1 A / 60 A = 80% of fuse capacity (with 60 A fuse, LED driver)
  • Peak load: 55.6 A / 70 A = 79% of fuse capacity (with 70 A fuse, motor driver, provides headroom) or 48.1 A / 70 A = 69% of fuse capacity (with 70 A fuse, LED driver)
  • Note: Use 60 A MIDI fuse for typical operation, or 70 A MIDI fuse for additional headroom during peak loads. The 40 A fuse will trip during peak loads and must be upgraded.

Recommendations:

  • Set PSU current limit to 60–65 A for safety margin (peak load is 55.6 A with motor driver, 48.1 A with LED driver)
  • Master fuse upgraded to 60–70 A MIDI fuse to accommodate peak loads (~56 A with motor driver, ~48 A with LED driver)
  • All branch fuses are appropriately sized for their loads
  • System operates at 89% of PSU capacity at peak load with motor driver (77% with LED driver) - monitor for thermal issues and ensure adequate ventilation
  • Consider monitoring actual peak loads - if consistently below 50 A, a 60 A fuse is appropriate; if approaching 60 A, use 70 A fuse

7. Final Summary (What Changed)

The original three-robot bench power rail design used a single 12 V 1500 W PSU feeding a Kohree 12-way fuse block to supply:

  • ArmPi Mini via 12 V → UPS 3S
  • ArmPi FPV via 12 V → UPS 3S
  • Yahboom ROSMASTER X3 Plus via a 12 V → 19 V boost path into the UPS-C → Jetson Orin Nano

Updates made:

  1. Yahboom ROSMASTER X3 Plus: Added a dedicated 12 V feed for its internal Li-Po/BMS system:

    • New: 12 V → 7.5 A fused branch → Yahboom Li-Po/BMS input
    • Existing: 12 V → 10 A fused branch → DROK → 19 V → UPS-C → Jetson Orin Nano

  2. ArmPi Mini and ArmPi FPV: Updated to provide separate 12 V feeds for UPS and STM32:

    • UPS Branch: 12 V → 5 A fused branch → Waveshare UPS 3S → Raspberry Pi 5
    • STM32 Branch: 12 V → 5 A fused branch → Hiwonder STM32 expansion board

  3. ArmPi Ultra: Added power distribution for ArmPi Ultra robot with UPS backup:

    • Input 1 - Main PSU: 12 V → 15 A fused branch → 14 AWG drop → XT30 Male → XT30 Female (on Master Power Switch) - connected when AC mains available
    • Input 2 - UPS Battery: UPS CH1 (12V @ 13.5A) → 14 AWG → XT30 Male → XT30 Female (on Master Power Switch) - connected when AC mains fails
    • Note: Only ONE input is connected to the power switch at any given time (either main PSU OR UPS battery, not both)
    • AC Power to UPS: 120V AC (14 AWG) → MR30 Male 3-Pin → MR30 Female 3-Pin (on UPS AC input)
    • After Switch: Master Power Switch (DC) → Power Distribution Block → Splits to three branches:
      • Branch 1: DROK buck converter (9V 12A, with 20mm cooling fan) → HiWonder STM32 → Raspberry Pi 5, and 4-Channel Encoder Motor Driver (9V) → Mecanum Chassis Motors
      • Branch 2: MOSFET LED Driver Board (IRFZ44N) → Gripper LED Array / Power LED (held in gripper setup) (Production)
    • Motor driver and LED driver typically don't run simultaneously (motor driver for dev/test, LED driver for production)
    • Master power switch provides convenient on/off control and seamless switching between main PSU and UPS battery backup

  4. USB-C Touch Displays + Ethernet Switch: Added power distribution for three AOC 16T15 portable monitors and NETGEAR GS305 ethernet switch:

    • Single XWST 20 A buck converter: 12 V → 10 A fused branch → Buck converter → 5 V output → 3× USB-C pigtails → All 3 displays + NETGEAR GS305 switch
    • All displays and switch powered from one converter (6.7 A total load: 6 A for displays + 0.7 A for switch, well within 20 A capacity)
    • USB-C pigtails with bare wire ends connect directly to converter output terminals
    • NETGEAR GS305 switch connects to converter 5 V output via barrel connector adapter or USB-C adapter

Each robot has independent power paths for better isolation and reliability. All three displays and the ethernet switch share a single buck converter, which is sufficient given the 20 A capacity. The system uses 8 of the 12 available fuse positions in the Kohree block. Note: Master fuse upgraded from 40 A to 60–70 A MIDI fuse to accommodate peak loads (~59 A).
