0. Conventions — read first

  • Static handling. ESD-safe mat + wrist strap at the bench for any SMD work (referenced via cross-refs to the Soldering Guide). PCA9685, TMP102, INA226 modules are CMOS — they tolerate handling without ESD precautions but the bench setup is cheap insurance.
  • All soldering technique, tip care, iron specs, and per-part solder procedures live in Electronics_Soldering_Guide.md. This guide cross-refs that one whenever a procedure involves heat + filler.
  • JST-XH 2.54 mm is the fleet-standard connector. Every in-house cable uses JST-XH on at least one end. PEBA-style crimper for the crimps. ✅ Live housing sizes: 2, 3, 4, 6. The 3-pin carries all four tether/inter-rail power and signal legs (PWR OUT, PWR IN, SIG OUT, SIG IN) and is now the most-used size in the build; the 6-pin is the +Y joint at the wand↔︎extension seam. 5-pin and 7-pin housings in stock are spares only — don't design anything new onto them. See §3 for the crimping procedure.
  • All "no soldering" claims in design docs assume the breakouts arrive with their factory headers populated. Verify on receipt. If a board arrives without the header populated, add it to the §3.x JST-XH header-solder operation list before continuing. (Full status table for every module in this build is in §0.1 below — read it before unpacking new modules.)
  • One-bridge-per-row safety (INA226 address pads). Re-stated here from EXTERNAL_SENSOR_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md §2 because it's easy to forget on the bench: never have two bridges in the same address row simultaneously — it shorts VCC to GND through the row trace.
  • Always run a continuity check before bridging anything. Silk letters are unreliable across vendor lots (see the GVLA/GULA correction recorded in the EXTERNAL doc). Function ID by multimeter against known rails on the header is deterministic and takes 30 seconds.

Pull-up reconciliation — bay node (the full bus-wide reconciliation, including the wand node, lives in Lumi_Wand_Integration.md §11; per EXTERNAL doc §2):

  • TMP102 (bay) 4.7 kΩ — KEEP intact (sole primary pull-up at the power-bay node).
  • INA226 10 kΩ ("01C" SMD) — LIFT both (§5.3). Reason: TMP102 already pulls hard at the power-bay node; the third pair just over-stiffens that node without helping the wand-end run.
  • Effective parallel at the bay node: 4.7 kΩ alone — in-spec for 100 kHz I²C. The wand node's pull-ups (TMP102-wand 4.7 kΩ + PCA9685 ~10 kΩ, ≈ 3.2 kΩ effective) are wand-side hardware: Integration §11.

The same-row-double-bridge safety (re-stated because it's life-or-board): INA226 + PCA9685 both have addr-select pad rows. Bridging two pads in the same row ties one rail directly to another via the row trace. On INA226: e.g. A0:G + A0:U bridges GND↔︎VCC → 3V3 rail short → can pop the Pi 3V3 LDO or the INA226 VCC pin. On PCA9685: less catastrophic (the addr pads tie to a single addr pin per pad), but still incorrect. Always desolder the existing row bridge before adding a new one. End state: exactly 2 bridges on each INA226 (one per row), 1 bridge on PCA9685 (A0), 0 net jumper changes on bay TMP102, 1 cut + 1 bridge on wand TMP102.

4. Power-Bay G469 Terminal Block + FFC Chain Mount

Goal: extend the entire RPi5 40-pin header into the power bay as labeled screw terminals. This is the foundation for every external-sensor wire and the wand-tether I²C + 3.3 V tap (see EXTERNAL_SENSOR_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md §2 inter-bay routing).

NVMe HAT context: Ultra and Halo each carry the Waveshare PCIe TO M.2 USB HAT+ (NVMe boot SSD, 4 USB 3 ports, INA219 at 0x40), on the Pi5's PCIe FPC and stacked on the GPIO header with its own 40-pin pass-through on top. The inverted FPC breakout plugs onto that pass-through, so no extension header is needed (Electronics_Bay_Design.md §3.6 and the Pi5 stack section). The Geekworm X1001 it replaced is retired.

Parts (BOM lines 1–4 in EXTERNAL_SENSOR_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md §13). Naming convention used throughout this section:

  • Elec-bay breakout = MECCANIXITY FFC/FPC board, female 2×20 variant. Mounts on the Pi5 GPIO (via the tall header, inverted per Electronics_Bay_Design.md §4). Female socket faces the Pi5 GPIO male pins.
  • FFC = 40-pin 0.5 mm pitch A-type FFC ribbon, 500 mm length. Connects the two MECCANIXITY breakouts. Current part per Electronics_Bay_Design.md §3.5 (MECCANIXITY 40 P 0.5 mm A-type 500 mm 10-pack).
  • Power-bay breakout = MECCANIXITY FFC/FPC board, male 2×20 variant. Mounts on the PSU top. Male 2×20 header on top mates with the terminal breakout below.
  • Terminal breakout = Geekworm G469 RPi GPIO Terminal Block Breakout. Female 2×20 header on the bottom (mates the power-bay breakout's male header); 40 labeled screw terminals + a pass-through 2×20 male header on top. See the "Terminal breakout — Geekworm G469" box below for pitch, ferrules and fit. The G469 pinout is identical to the Pi GPIO map and is shown in the G469 reference images below.

⚠️ TERMINAL BREAKOUT — Geekworm G469. Chosen over the CZH-Labs D-1352 (operator verdict: "garbage quality, totally"; its cramped clamps would not reliably hold thin signal wire). Hand-landing thin wire into screw terminals is not a production-grade interconnect (labor-heavy, pull-out-prone, no vibration rating) — hence ferrules on every landing (below). The G469 (~$12) has a sturdier build, per-GPIO terminals on 4 terminal blocks breaking out all 40 pins, plus a pass-through 2×20 male header. It drops onto the power-bay male 2×20 header — friction-fit, no bolts.

Product / sourcing: Geekworm G469 (official) · Amazon B0DMNJ17PD · AliExpress ~$6.99, faster ship

⚠️ The G469 is ALSO 2.54 mm pitch — it is not a bigger-terminal part (established 2026-09-01)

The vendor drawing gives a 52 mm terminal-block span carrying 20 terminals per edge → 2.6 mm, i.e. 2.54 mm pitch. Geekworm publishes no pitch or AWG spec (checked the wiki and the listings); the drawing is the only hard evidence and it is unambiguous.

So the G469's advantage is clamp QUALITY and per-GPIO labelling, not terminal SIZE. Whether it uses a rising-cage clamp (captures the strands) rather than a bare screw tip is unverified; with a ferrule on every landing the strands are never under a bare screw.

✅ The violet ferrule IDENTIFIED — it is an AIRIC 26 AWG part (2026-09-23)

The ferrule that takes four 26 AWG conductors is from the AIRIC 26 AWG/Gauge Wire Ferrule Insulated Connectors pack — not a DIN E0208. Four 26 AWG is 0.516 mm² of copper against an E0208's Ø0.8 mm bore = 0.503 mm² (103 %), so it would not fit an E0208; the AIRIC part takes it (bench-crimped and as-built on consecutive terminals). It is also the G469 ferrule for a single 22 AWG conductor.

⚠️ The AIRIC “2 AWG” and “26 AWG” listings sit next to each other. Check the gauge on the bag, not the thumbnail.

ℹ️ One ferrule per terminal, most is three conductors, and the capacity tripwire in check_g469_map.py holds at four.

Build with FERRULES — E0308 on 24 AWG, E0208 on 26 AWG, AIRIC violet on 22 AWG or multi-conductor. They fit the G469 terminals, and the operator's ferrule crimper crimps them correctly (gate below). As-built (operator, 2026-09-28): every G469 landing goes through a ferrule — nothing on this block is bare or tinned. Three things the drawing makes obvious that the table does not:

  1. ⚠️ The 2nd PL011 lands on screws you will have labelled CE0 and MISO. The board ships blank and you label it yourself (§4 step 3), and those labels are Pi function names, not GPIO numbers — while the ZE15-CO's UART is GPIO8/9, the SPI chip-select and MISO pins in the default alt-function. So nothing within reach of those two terminals will say "UART". Same trap in miniature for the SPS30, except there the label (TXD/RXD) happens to agree with the use.

  2. ✅ Eleven terminals share a ferrule — and that is a non-issue. Every terminal is badged with its conductor count: ×2 on pins 1, 2, 4, 6, 9, 29, 31, 32 and ×3 on SDA (3), SCL (5) and 3V3 (17).

    ⚠️ The 3V3 rail carries five loads across its two screws — pin 1 takes INA226 VCC + the wand, pin 17 takes bay TMP102 VCC + both 10 kΩ tach pull-up legs (it is 6 rows from the tach screws, pin 1 is 14). The whole rail draws ~11 mA, so the constraint is conductors per screw, not load.

    ⚡ GND (pin 9) is the wand's star ground. Without a separate logic return, the wand's SDA/SCL/3V3 would come home on the power leg's 22 AWG GND beside the 2.4 A LED switching current, putting the wand's I²C reference 102 mV above the Pi's. All six tether nets arrive on ONE 6-pin at the wand's end plate; GND-logic is one of them and lands here on pin 9.

    Do not land it at the distribution block. Two grounds meeting at two bay nodes that are already tied makes a parallel return — ~0.85 A of LED current would divert through the Pi. It is safe only because the wand's rail GND bus is cut, so the drivers return on GND-power and only the PCA9685 + TMP102 return here. Pin 9 is the one place the two grounds meet. Integration §10.5.2

    ✅ Bench-settled 2026-09-23. Four 26 AWG crimp into one ferrule cleanly, and nothing on this board needs four — three is the most any terminal carries:

    1. Push the G469's underside socket straight down onto the header — flush, no visible pin gap; friction fit is the only retention (do NOT use the M2.5 ears). Either way round.

    • Both ends: FFC blue stripe UP (contacts face DOWN toward the connector's PCB-side fingers). This works despite the elec-bay breakout being inverted — bench-verified 2026-08-02. Close each flip-lock only after the FFC is fully seated.
    • Why no flip: A-type FFC is straight-through (wire N at end A → wire N at end B). Each MECCANIXITY board's PCB traces map its socket pin N to FPC-connector wire N (intrinsic to the board design; unaffected by the physical mounting orientation of the board). Inverting the elec-bay breakout changes where pin 1 sits in 3D space but does NOT change the pin-1-to-wire-1 electrical mapping. Combined with the step 1 rotation rule (FPC end at STM32) that puts breakout pin 1 physically over Pi5 GPIO pin 1, the whole chain — Pi5 pin 1 → elec-bay breakout pin 1 → wire 1 → power-bay breakout pin 1 → G469 pin 1 — is straight-through with no flip anywhere.
    • Route: the FFC exits the inverted elec-bay breakout and folds 180° back over the top of the breakout PCB, then routes to the electronics-bay back-cover big cable hole. Through the back-cover hole, the FFC drops down through the base-servo hole in the Hiwonder black base plate into the power bay area (bundled with the OAK-D S2 cable, iFLYTEK mic extension, and STM32 servo bus cable per Electronics_Bay_Design.md §4 Route A).

    • Reads ~3.3 V → correct. Also probe pin 4 (labeled 5 V) vs GND → should read ~5 V. Assembly is good.
    • Reads ~0 V or floating → the FFC is not seated, or one flip-lock is open. Re-seat both ends (both blue-stripe UP).
    • Reads any GPIO signal (~0 or 3.3 V depending on IO state), NOT rail voltage → pin-1 ↔︎ pin-40 swap end-to-end. Most likely cause: the elec-bay breakout is 180° mis-rotated (FPC connector pointing AWAY from the STM32 instead of toward it — see step 1's physical rule). Power off, rotate the elec-bay breakout 180°, re-test. Less likely: one end of the FFC was inserted blue-stripe DOWN by mistake.

5.1 Mechanical install — PSU-top, in series on CH1

What this section does (in one line): put the INA226 in the PSC-100-12 CH1 +V current path so all CH1 current flows through its on-board 2 mΩ shunt, and land its logic header at the G469.

Procedure:

  1. Identify the PSC-100-12 CH1 +V output on the PSU top. Trace the existing wire that runs from CH1 +V → the Master Power Switch input. This is the wire we're going to cut and insert the INA226 across.
  2. Cut that wire on the LOAD side (i.e. between PSC-100-12 CH1 +V output and Master Power Switch input). Two ends now exist: the source end (still soldered to PSC-100-12 CH1 +V) and the load end (still going to Master Power Switch input). Never cut on the GND side, never cut between battery and BMS.
  3. Mount the INA226 on the carrier: on a printed INA adapter over the carrier's posts (INA226-MAIN and INA226-BATT side by side on one shared adapter; INA3 on its own), screwed through the board's dead mounting pair, never the live shunt pads (§11.3.9). ⏳ The adapters are released after one board is calipered.
  4. Wire the 4 screw terminals (keep leads short — the 2 mΩ shunt is on-board, no external shunt or long sense-lead runs):
    • SOURCE end of the cut wire (goes to PSC-100-12 CH1 +V) → ISENSE+ (orange, terminal pin 2). 18 AWG.
    • LOAD end of the cut wire (goes to Master Power Switch input) → ISENSE− (green, terminal pin 3). 18 AWG.
    • V+(VBUS) (orange, terminal pin 1) → short jumper wire (18–20 AWG is fine, no current flows here) over to the ISENSE+ terminal so both share the same source-side node. This is what gives the INA226 its bus-voltage reading.
    • GND (green, terminal pin 4) → PSC-100-12 CH1 GND / bay star ground. 18 AWG.
  5. Verify the current direction is correct. Current must flow: PSC-100-12 CH1 +V → ISENSE+ → [2 mΩ shunt on board] → ISENSE− → Master Power Switch. If you swap ISENSE+ and ISENSE−, the INA226 still works but reports current as NEGATIVE (magnitude is right, sign is wrong). Fix by unscrewing and swapping the two wires under ISENSE+ / ISENSE−.

5a.6 Monitoring + alerts spec (for Fleet to implement — Path C1, never the model)

Per CLAUDE.md / ADR 18: a small read-only systemd logger (the hat_power_logger pattern) plus a check.sh on system cron every minute that reads the CSV and prints OK/ALERT → skill-alert → tg-alert. Nothing polls the chips except the logger.

Logger sampling — built to see sub-second events: a plain 1 s sample would miss a chattering inlet entirely.

  • Sample as fast as the conversions allow (~100 Hz with AVG = 4 and 1.1 ms conversion times on both channels). Log per-second min / max / mean for batt_A, pack_V (INA226-BATT), the HAT INA219 5 V where a HAT exists, and CH1 bus V where INA226-MAIN exists (Halo, Nova; Ultra has none). One CSV line per second.
  • Register-latched alert — catches events BETWEEN polls with NO extra wire:
    • The INA226 compares every conversion against its Alert Limit and, with LEN = 1 in the Mask/Enable register (06h), latches the AFF flag until Mask/Enable is read.
    • INA226-BATT: enable SUL (shunt-voltage-under) with the limit at −0.3 A × 2 mΩ = −0.6 mV = −240 LSB (2.5 µV LSB, two's complement in register 07h). Any discharge excursion latches.
    • Halo/Nova INA226-MAIN: BUL (bus-under) at 13.3 V latches any CH1 dip.
    • The logger reads Mask/Enable once per second, records the AFF bit as an alert_latched column, and the read clears it.
    • The INA219 on the HAT has no alert function, so its per-second min carries that role.
  • The physical ALERT pin stays unwired (GPIO26 remains reserved, per the landing map) unless a hardware latch is ever wanted. The register latch gives the same capture in software.

5b. INA226-DROK (INA3) — third INA226 on the servo-rail buck output (all builds)

11.2.2 Wiring — cut off stock 4-pin, spade-lug the 12 V pair, land GPIO wires direct at G469

Design decision (locked 2026-08-05): no JST-XH connectors on the bay-fan run. The 12 V pair (V+ / GND) terminates in E0508 ferrules at the HL-PBB cage end and is soldered + heat-shrink joined to the fan's stock lead at the fan end. The GPIO pair (PWM / TACH) is landed directly into the G469 screw terminals in 26 AWG (signal palette; v1 as-built 24 AWG — see the gauge note below). No intermediate housing anywhere on the fan run.

Procedure per fan:

  1. Cut the stock 4-pin connector off the Noctua, leaving the full stock lead length attached to the fan (~250 mm).
  2. Separate the 4 wires. Strip ~5 mm at the cut end of each.
  3. Yellow (V+) + Black (GND) 12 V pair — extend and terminate at the HL-PBB:
    • Solder each wire to an extension of 22 AWG silicone hookup wire, sized to reach from the fence back wall (fan) to the distribution block on the carrier. Heat-shrink the solder joint (3 mm × 15 mm shrink, centered).
    • At the HL-PBB end of each extension: crimp an E0508 ferrule. Land in the assigned cage (§11.3 D10-D13: fan #1 A3/B3, fan #2 A4/B4). V+ and GND both run straight — no inline diodes (fans are Pi5-PWM-controlled and tolerate 13.8 V direct within Noctua's spec margin).

  5. Green (TACH) — E0308 ferrule into G469 GPIO5 (fan #1) or GPIO6 (fan #2): strip untinned; the tach wire shares its ferrule with that pin's 10 kΩ pull-up pigtail end (×2 badge). One fan's tach per screw terminal — do NOT parallel the two green wires; each fan's tach must be read independently by bay_thermal_node.
  6. If the fan's stock green + blue leads don't reach the G469 as-is, extend them in 26 AWG (signal palette; v1 as-built 24 AWG) (solder + heat-shrink, same joint technique as the 12 V pair — but not the same gauge; see the gauge note below), then land the G469 end.
  7. DO NOT BUILD — the signal-GND jumper (distribution-block GND bus → G469 pin 34) is NOT INSTALLED and is on hold. B5 carries the MusRock GND (§11.3.3); if this jumper is ever proven needed, it does NOT go on B5 — see the forward path in the §11.3.3 B5 row and the box under “Why the signal-GND jumper” below. Each fan's only ground wire (black) stays on the distribution block per step 3.

STEP 7 IS ON HOLD — not installed, and probably should not be (2026-09-30)

As-built: there is no jumper in the bay.

The Pi already shares a reference with the fans' ground (the fans read PWM and drive tach relative to the distribution block's GND bus where their black wires land): the ZK-SJ20 is non-isolated, so its input− and output− are the same node, and the chain Pi GND → STM32 → Deans → ZK-SJ20 → D9 → B1 already ties Pi ground to the fan ground bus. The jumper would not create the reference — it would add a second, parallel one.

The margins do not need it:

Signal Threshold Margin Ground offset needed to break it
PWM (Pi → fan) ~2.1 V V_IH 3.3 − 2.1 = 1.2 V 1.2 V
TACH (fan → Pi) ~0.8 V V_IL V_CE(sat) ~0.3 V → 0.5 V 0.5 V

The IR drop along the existing path is on the order of 30–150 mV at load — 8–40× inside the tighter margin.

And a jumper carries two risks:

  1. It is a parallel return, not a reference. From Pi GND to the B bus there would be two routes: the 5 V cable ground (~4 mΩ) and the jumper (~32 mΩ with contacts). Current divides by resistance, so the jumper takes roughly 11 % of the Pi's return — order of half an amp, against a table that calls it “mA-scale signal return only”. A wire fitted to be a quiet reference would instead carry a share of the Pi's switching return.
  2. 🔴 It is an unfused sneak path. If the Deans connector backs out or D9 loosens, the entire Pi return — up to 5 A — comes home through a 22 AWG signal wire. Nothing in the bay limits it.

⚠️ Those resistance figures are estimates from wire tables, not measurements — the current could be 50 mA or 1.5 A. The structure of the argument does not depend on them: it is a parallel path, current divides by ratio, and the fault mode is real.

Decision: leave it out. If a defined reference is wanted later, get it by making the power ground stiff, not by adding a second path.

The measurement that would settle it — with the bay at full load, DC between G469 pin 34 and the HL-PBB B bus. Under ~100 mV and the jumper buys nothing. ⏳ Blocked until the wand electronics are finished, the new wand is printed, and the 6-wire tether is run from the bay up through the arm base — there is no full-load condition before that.

Fan motor current returns on each black wire to the distribution block. Do not move or split a fan's black wire onto the G469 — that would route motor current through the Pi's ground.

Why TACH is per-fan (unlike the wand fan): the bay_thermal_node monitors each fan's RPM independently so it can detect a single-fan bearing failure and log/alert rather than assuming both are healthy. Wand fan is a single-unit trust; bay fans are redundant + monitored.

PWM pull-up / level: Noctua PWM input threshold is ~2 V; Pi5 GPIO 12 output at 3.3 V drives it cleanly with no level-shifter. 25 kHz is the standard Noctua PWM frequency — set that in the bay_thermal_node PWM output config.

TACH pull-ups (REQUIRED at the G469): tach output is open-collector — needs a 10 kΩ pull-up to 3.3 V on each of GPIO 5 and GPIO 6. Two options:

  • (a) External 10 kΩ pigtail at the G469 (RECOMMENDED — 10 kΩ discretes are in bench stock): cut ~50 mm of hookup wire, solder a 1/4 W thru-hole axial 10 kΩ resistor in-line at the middle, heat-shrink the resistor + solder joints, and leave the two wire ends untinned — each goes into the shared ferrule at its landing. Land one end in the GPIO5 (or GPIO6) G469 screw terminal, the other end in a 3V3 G469 screw terminal. Repeat for the second fan tach. Two pigtails per build (one per fan tach). The GPIO5/GPIO6 and 3V3 terminals are not adjacent on the G469, so the in-line pigtail is required — you can't land a bare axial resistor directly between two screw terminals.
  • (b) Fallback — Pi's internal ~50 kΩ pull-up in the bay_thermal_node (pigpio or lgpio set_pull_up_down) — zero-solder, but the internal pull is 5× weaker than the external 10 kΩ, which gives slower rise times and may miss tach pulses at high RPM.

Recommendation: use (a). External 10 kΩ is stiffer → sharper edges → reliable pulse counting at all RPM. Now that 10 kΩ discretes are in bench stock (bench-verified 2026-08-03), the 30-second-per-fan solder step is the right default. Reserve (b) as a bring-up shortcut only if the discretes aren't handy — but expect erratic RPM at high fan speed and plan to add (a) later.

11.2.3 Cable management + bench-verify

  • Twist the V+ / GND pair for each fan (twisted-pair, ~1 turn per cm) inside the bay from the fan lead all the way to the HL-PBB. The 12 V rail carries small but continuous current + the PWM switching creates a differential-mode aggressor pair; twisting the fan power leads reduces radiated noise onto the I²C bus and the wand tether.
  • Route TACH + PWM signals separately from the power pair where they enter/exit the G469 area — use different sides of the cable channel.
  • Both fans' blue PWM wires land together under the G469 GPIO12 screw (no separate Y-splice needed — the screw clamp is the joint).

Bench-verify (before closing up the bay):

  1. Power up the bay 12 V rail (no PWM signal — GPIO 12 floating).
  2. Both fans should spin at ~4500 RPM (the Noctua default with PWM disconnected = 100 % duty via internal pull-up in the fan).
  3. Multimeter check at each fan's V+ solder joint (fan lead to extension, right where the heat-shrink ends): 13.8 V ± 0.1 (direct from the distribution block — no diode drop). If reading is 0 V, the ferrule isn't seated in its block cage or the solder joint at the fan lead is open.
  4. Bring up the bay_thermal_node and issue a 30 % PWM duty command. Both fans should drop to ~1500 RPM.
  5. Cover one fan with a fingertip momentarily; the corresponding TACH-side GPIO (5 or 6) reading should drop to 0 RPM. Release — should recover.
  6. Repeat for the other fan. Both TACH channels must respond independently — if fan 1's cover-test drops the fan 2 TACH reading, the two green wires were accidentally paralleled somewhere (wrong — separate them).

  • AC rocker (3-pin illuminated): three blades total —

Fork-lug landing technique — the fleet standard for every bay screw terminal (locked 2026-08-07):

Every PSU screw-terminal landing inside the bay uses a Kuject-kit insulated fork/spade crimp lug. INA226-MAIN and INA226-BATT take E1008 ferrules (operator, 2026-11-16); if a ferrule won't seat in a terminal, that landing goes bare (twisted strands, never tinned). The ZK-SJ20's IN and OUT terminals take E2508 ferrules on the doubled 18 AWG (bare twisted strands if one won't seat), and INA3's IN+ / IN− take bare twisted 2 × 18 (§5b.4). For the forks (size-matched to the screw stud; check the fork stud hole against the screw shank before crimping), with the fork bent 60–90° upward after install to clear the tight lateral gap between the fence and the PSU / battery / DROK. Install sequence, in order (never out of order):

  1. Crimp first — wire into fork-lug crimp barrel, ratchet crimper.
  2. Slide the fork under the partially-loosened screw head from the side (pivot direction of the fork is whichever side has cable-run clearance).
  3. Tighten the screw down.
  4. Bend the fork upward using needle-nose pliers pivoting around the screw as the axis. 60-90° is the sweet spot.

⚡ The HL-PBB distribution module is the standing exception to all of the above — every landing there is a FERRULE, never a lug, and nothing is bent: E1008 for 18 AWG, E0508 for 22 AWG, E0208 for 26 AWG signal wires. Strip to the ferrule's length, not the terminal's — see the strip-length note in §11.3.1.

Never bend before crimping (crimp doesn't hold well on a pre-bent wire). Never bend against a tightened screw (twists the fork rather than pivoting it — screw must already be tight, but the fork tang moves independently of the fork mouth around the screw shank). Never bend > 90° (work-hardens the fork tang metal and stresses the crimp).

Post-bend inspection: the rigid PVC / nylon insulator collar on the Kuject red-band forks doesn't like to flex sharply. If a hairline crack appears at the fork-to-insulator junction after bending, wrap a single lap of electrical tape or slide a short piece of 6 mm heat-shrink over the crimp barrel to restore the insulation. Cheap insurance especially on the mains-adjacent PSU L / N terminals.

Exceptions to the fork-lug standard:

  • Terminal-breakout clamps (fan signals + all sensor pigtails): ferrules on every landing — E0308 (24 AWG), E0208 (26 AWG), AIRIC violet (22 AWG or multi-conductor); as-built 2026-09-28, nothing bare or tinned. E0508 is banned at the G469.
    • The G469 is 2.54 mm pitch (§4). A 2.54 mm terminal takes ~1.1–1.4 mm of wire entry. E0308 / E0208 (Assembly Guide §1.2): bore Φ0.8 · OD Φ1.1 · collar Φ1.9 · barrel 8 mm, both sizes identical externally. Barrel is inside the 1.1–1.4 mm entry; collar clears 2.54 mm pitch by 0.64 mm. The XHF kit has nothing smaller than E0508, so these are separate purchases.
    • ✅ Ferrule gate PASSED — bench-verified 2026-09-23. The operator's ferrule crimper crimps both E0308 (24 AWG) and E0208 (26 AWG) correctly (a Φ1.1 mm barrel is below the smallest die on most self-adjusting square crimpers; this one does not flatten it), and the crimped ferrules fit the G469 terminals. No 4-indent crimper needed.
    • Do not tin. Solder cold-flows under sustained clamp pressure and the joint backs out — on these terminals that surfaces months later as an intermittent I²C/UART/GPIO fault, which is a genuinely miserable thing to chase. Low current doesn't protect you; contact resistance does the damage, not heat.
    • Strip to the ferrule's spec length (10 mm for E0308/E0208, Assembly Guide §1.2) and bottom the strands out in the barrel — no bare strand outside the ferrule. It is a dense terminal strip and a stray strand reaching its neighbour is the other failure mode.
    • Twist tightly in the strands' own lay direction before inserting into the ferrule (all conductors of a shared ferrule together), so nothing frays out of the barrel.
    • Tug-test every landing, and re-torque after the first thermal cycle — screw terminals relax on stranded wire.
    • Already-built and working? Leave it. Re-torque and tug-test at the next service rather than rebuilding a functioning harness on principle.
  • STM32 blue terminal block (D14, D15): small cage-clamp (~M2.5); bench-check with the smallest Kuject red-band fork before committing — fall back to a ferrule (never tinned wire) if the fork doesn't seat cleanly.
  • INA226 D1a jumper: short bare-wire jumper across two adjacent screws on the same board — bare tinned is standard, no fork needed for a same-board jumper.
  • Earth bond (A5) at the PSU ⏚ terminal: ring lug (fully enclosed), so it can't slip out from under a loosened screw.

11.3.5 Wiring procedure

  1. Power off + capacitor discharge. AC rocker OFF (kills PSU AC input) AND DC rocker OFF. Wait 30 s for PSU caps to bleed. Multimeter-verify 0 V at the HL-PBB input feed AND 0 V at the PSU CH1 output before starting.
  2. Continuity sanity on the module (unmodified, straight out of the bag): IN-A → any A cage should read SHORT (one V+ net); IN-B → any B cage SHORT (one GND net); any A cage → any B cage OPEN. If A↔︎B reads short the board is defective — swap it.
  3. Prep the wires — strip 10 mm and crimp the gauge-matched ferrule: E1008 for the 18 AWG segments (D2/D4/D7/D9), E0508 for 22 AWG (D5/D6/D8a/D10–D13/D16/D17), E0208 for the 26 AWG-class signal wires. 7 mm is the cage's bare-wire spec; all three ferrules in use strip to 10 mm (see the DIN table in §4) — stripping to 7 leaves the 8 mm barrel under-filled. Never tin — solder cold-flows under clamp pressure (2026-08-14 rule).
  4. Land each ferrule in its assigned cage per the §11.3.3 table — open the cage screw, bottom the ferrule out, torque down, tug-test. One conductor per cage, except where the port map doubles one: up to 3 conductors in ONE ferrule (operator 2026-11-15), the ferrule sized by their TOTAL cross-section (today only B6 on Creator builds: LAMP 22 + PHONE-IN GND 22 in one E7508). Strip both to 10 mm, twist together, crimp once, tug-test each conductor.
    • Fan branches (A3/B3, A4/B4): 22 AWG from the cage → solder + heat-shrink joint at the fan lead — no diodes. See §11.3.3 fan-branch spec.
    • MusRock feed (A5/B5, D16/D17): 22 AWG twisted pair, A5 direct. The signal-GND jumper is on hold — do not build (§11.2.2 step 7).
  5. Confirm the wand-tether pair (V+ at A1, GND-power at B1) exits the block bundled into the wand tether run (GND-logic is the third conductor and comes from G469 pin 9, never this block). Confirm the fan branches are zip-tied to the harness and multimeter-verified: at each fan's V+ extension wire (near the solder joint), 13.8 V ± 0.1 with PSU on.
  6. Dress the landings — no bare ferrule barrel proud of its cage, wire bundles strain-relieved so nothing side-loads a cage.
  7. Bench-verify before power-up — with everything wired but PSU still OFF, continuity-check:
    • IN-A → every used A cage: short (V+ bus)
    • IN-B → every used B cage: short (GND bus)
    • Any A ↔︎ any B: OPEN
    • PSU CH1 +13.8 V terminal → wand tether V+ pin: short
    • PSU CH1 GND terminal → wand tether GND-power pin: short
    • ZK-SJ20 IN+ → A bus: short (A1); ZK-SJ20 IN− → B bus: short (B1)
    • PSU CH1 +V (INA226-MAIN ISENSE−) → block input A: short only with the DC rocker held ON, open with it OFF (v2: the rocker sits upstream of the whole bus)
  8. Power up. Multimeter at the wand-tether JST-XH male end: V+ to GND-power should read 13.6-13.9 V (essentially no drop across ferrule + cage).
  9. STM32 branch check. Multimeter at the ZK-SJ20 output: 7.50 V ± 0.05 LV / 12.0 V HV with the STM32 stack booted (the buck was set and bench-gated before fitting, §5c; never trim it on the robot unless the reading is off).

Thermal — the one real design risk. ⚠️ The PSU has NO fan. Cooling is bay-level only: 2× NF-A4x20 exhaust on the fence back wall pull air front → back, and the PSU's hex top grating is its only vent (UPS doc, Cooling). A carrier spanning the PSU top can choke it unless it's mostly open. The deck height sets the air gap above the grating; the ZK-SJ20's heatsink hangs through to 3.5 mm above it. Requirements:

  • ≥ 50 % open area over the PSU footprint: a rib/lattice frame with boards on bosses, not a solid plate.
  • Long openings aligned front → back with the bay airflow.
  • Keep the PSU's hottest zone fully open (the downstream +Y half — "TMP102 intent" below; confirmed by the survey).
  • Acceptance: PSU-top temperature at steady load with the carrier fitted ≤ +5 °C over the same load without it. One reading each, folded into the pending PSU-top thermal survey.
  • Height: PSU top → deck → tallest board (ZK-SJ20 coil top Z 86.6 under the Z 89.7 lid, HL-PBB raised over the bars, G469 stack) must clear the bay lid. CAD checks against the fence envelope.

TMP102 intent — fan-loop source (answered 2026-10-22): the bay has ONE TMP102 (0x48), and its only job is to tell bay_thermal_node how hot the air the fans are exhausting is.

  • The I²C cluster over the battery is NOT a valid fan-loop position. The battery tray gets its own baffled parallel intake air (UPS doc, Cooling), so a sensor there reads COOL air while the PSU heats up. The loop would under-respond: a false negative on its one job.

  • Required position: over the PSU, in its plume, on the exhaust half (between the PSU's hot zone and the back-wall NF-A4x20s), on 8 mm posts, so it reads moving air, not the carrier. Keep ≥ 20 mm lateral offset from the Mus-14 heatsink (it sits over the PSU too).

  • Short bus runs don't argue for the cluster: the bus already spans the 500 mm FFC + a 600 mm tether. The TMP102's own 4-pin cable to the G469 is the same few-cm job wherever it sits, and an extra 100 mm is nothing at 100 kHz. The two INA226s can cluster freely (keep INA226-MAIN's 18 AWG CH1 runs on the power edge; the few hundred mm of extra 18 AWG is a ~30 mV drop at 8 A).

  • Position: CAD's layout puts it at X 184–209, Y 1–26 on 8 mm posts, in the PSU's downstream half. The PSU's length and the bay airflow both run along Y (intake −Y front wall, exhaust +Y back wall), so the downstream air is the warmest.

    • Survey → sanity check: a hand-thermocouple sweep at 15 mm height across the PSU's downstream half. The position passes if it reads within 2 °C of the hottest point found; otherwise move the set to that spot.
    • Survey conditions for a fair comparison: same steady bay load, both fans on the same fixed duty (loop off), ≥ 30 min settle per position, ambient recorded.
    • Mus-14 clearance is locked by a CAD hard gate check (edge-to-edge plan rectangles; the RESULT fails if < 20.0).
    • TMP102 cable: the 4-way chain housing plugs onto its soldered JST-XH header (§6.1); keep the plug clear of the Mus-14 and the buck heatsink.
    • We don't use the Qwiic plug. The bay TMP102 is wired through its 0.1" header row (§6), and the generic boards on order (§16b.3) may have no Qwiic socket at all. Route the 4-pin cable from the 0.1" row.

  • G469 support posts: the carrier gives the G469 2× M2 support posts (operator: header-only was wobbly). They stop 0.3 BELOW the seated underside (Z 62.80 vs 63.10); the gate holds them 0.1–0.5 under, so there is no header preload ✓ — shim at fit rather than force; header height is a parameter. If the G469's mounting holes are plated/GND-tied, a metal self-tap alone in plastic is harmless, but never give the hole a metal path to anything else. Meter the holes once.

  • A battery-zone temperature reading would be genuinely useful later (LiFePO4 charge-temperature limits), but that's a SECOND sensor at 0x4A or 0x4B (0x49 belongs to the wand). Not in scope now; recorded so the cluster spot isn't confused with it.

With ~6–7 mm heads that means a slot of ≥ ~17 mm centred within ±1.5 mm, so 20 mm works if it's centred. CAD: confirm against the measured row.
  • Wire exits: the LV pair (−V/+V → INA226-MAIN) leaves the slot upward at the −V/+V end. Mains never crosses the carrier TOP face.

Gated ≥ 8 mm jacket-to-jacket from every LV conductor (nearest 8.65). Conditions:
    1. Wire: 18 AWG silicone, 600 V-rated (the operator's spool, marked 600 V / 6 A, checked 2026-11-06). Unmarked silicone hookup is never used on mains: check the print on the spool. Current check: the PSC-100-12 draws about 2.2 A RMS at 115 VAC at full 100 W load, with no PFC, and less at 230 V. That is about a third of the wire's 6 A, and nothing else is on the AC side. The 6 A rating is far below the 15–20 A wall breaker, though, which is one more reason for the slow-blow inlet fuse (T3.15A or T5A) while the PSU is an uncertified clone.
    2. Single insulation, no sleeve, on Ultra (operator): every mains wire runs as-is in its 600 V silicone jacket, over the carrier and at the BATT crossing. The separation from LV is that jacket plus the ≥ 8 mm spacing and the printed rib (item 3); the printed fork is only the support. Halo revisits sleeving if Ultra shows a safety issue; a sleeved wire (~4–5 mm OD) needs the carrier's mains forks resized.
    3. Its own forks, in its own lane. Never share a fork, tie or bundle with ANY LV conductor. ≥ 8 mm from every LV route, board, screw, ferrule and fork. Crossings with LV only at 90 ± 10°, mains above, in its own raised hold (one sits over F1's nose end, 9.4 mm clear).

The L / N / E bundle (Ø3.70–4.00, mains only) rests in a saddle on the tower top (R 2.1, 1.0 deep, R 1 edges) and is held by one zip tie through the cap's tunnel: snug, not cinched, mains only, no LV in or touching the tie, head and flush-trimmed tail on the cap side away from the jacket and from LV (nearest LV to a mains tie, loop and head modelled: 11.35).     5. Never over the carrier's top face or the LV half of the slot: the run reaches the strip through the slot's +X opening at the L/N/⏚ end.
    6. Strain relief at BOTH ends: a fork or tie within 30–50 mm of the C14 Fastons and within 30–50 mm of the PSU screw terminals, so a tug never loads a termination. Termination workmanship is exactly what 10-22 was about: tug-test every mains termination (§16.1).
    7. Earth (green/yellow) is the LONGEST conductor at each termination, so it's the last to break. Earth bond stays a ring lug (A5).

  • Keep-out: no LV board, screw head, ferrule or termination within ~8 mm of an L/N/⏚ screw (conservative design target, above reinforced-insulation creepage for 250 V).

Bay I²C daisy chain (operator rule, 2026-10-23): G469 → INA226-MAIN → INA226-BATT → TMP102 → INA3, one 4-wire 26 AWG chain.

  • Better for the bus than a star: a linear trunk with near-zero stubs. The bus as a whole is one T at the G469: the bay chain one way, the wand tether's signal leg the other. Total capacitance stays well inside 400 pF at 100 kHz.
  • How each board joins the chain: a female 4-way housing (JST-XH or Dupont) on each board's 4-pin logic header, every contact DOUBLE-CRIMPED with the incoming and outgoing 26 AWG wire. Two 26 AWG ≈ 0.26 mm², inside the PEBA 28–22 range and the 22 AWG barrel. Tug-test every double crimp. The end board (TMP102) gets single-wire contacts.
    • Not two wires soldered into one header hole: unserviceable, and it stresses the pin.
    • Not splice-Ys: extra joints in a moving harness.
    • Trade-off accepted: unplugging a mid-chain board breaks the bus downstream, which is fine for bench service.
    • The GODIYMODULES logic header is 4-pin GND / SCL / SDA / VCC (ALERT is a separate pad, left unwired).
  • ⚡ GND rule — ONE logic-to-power tie, at INA226-MAIN, as today. INA226-MAIN's GND screw carries the CH1 power return (D3/D4 land on it), so its logic GND IS power GND. That board is the system's single logic-to-power ground tie, with the chain GND going to G469 pin 9 (the star reference). INA226-BATT's GND screw stays EMPTY: the battery − goes straight to CH2 BAT−, never through that screw. Landing any power ground on it creates a second tie, the parallel-return loop the pin-9 rule forbids. TMP102 GND is chain-only. On carrier builds pin 14 is free (Halo v1's TMP102 GND lands there, §6.5).
  • 3V3: from G469 pin 17 or 1, whichever has ferrule budget. Chain current is a few mA.
  • Pull-up recap (one bus, so pull-ups anywhere are equivalent): all three INA226s lifted (01C); bay TMP102 4.7 k (SparkFun) or ~10 k (the generic boards on order, measured at arrival, §16b.3); wand TMP102 4.7 k; wand PCA9685 ~10 k; HAT INA219 on the pass-through unknown, measure. 13.1 Bench prep — set jumpers + pots BEFORE assembling into the fence

This is one-time per module; do all 4 at the bench, then install. Per EXTERNAL_SENSOR_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md §2 (PIR row) + Appendix E.

Vendor datasheet (PDF): SR501 Motion Sensor.pdf — full HC-SR501 user guide (Handson Technology) including BISS0001 IC schematic, pot direction diagrams, trigger-mode timing examples, and the annotated board-layout figure below. Read this if you need any detail beyond the four steps below.

Board layout — pin, jumper, and pot locations (from the datasheet):

  1. Retriggerable jumper (yellow shunt, right side of board — see figure above): set to H (retriggerable — HIGH for entire motion window; each new motion re-arms the delay). Jumper is 3-pin labeled L/H — move the shunt to the H position.
  2. Time-delay pot (left orange trimpot): per datasheet, CW = longer delay (fully CW ≈ 5 minutes) / CCW = shorter delay (fully CCW ≈ 3 s). Target: rotate fully CCW to reach the ~3 s minimum, then a hair back CW to seat off the end-stop. Verify with a multimeter — OUT should hold HIGH for ~3 s after the last motion.
  3. Sensitivity pot (middle orange trimpot): per datasheet, CW = LOW sensitivity (fully CW ≈ 3 m detection range — REJECTS most thermal noise) / CCW = HIGH sensitivity (fully CCW ≈ 7 m — MORE false triggers). Target for our bay: fully CW (LOW sensitivity) so the PIR triggers on a person walking ~3 m past the fence but rejects HVAC / fan-plume / sun-on-wall thermal drift. ⚠️ HC-SR501 clones vary — some lots ship with the sensitivity pot direction inverted from the datasheet. Bench-verify by dialing to what SHOULD be minimum sensitivity per the datasheet, then check: if the PIR still fires on a hand wave at ~7 m distance, the direction is inverted on your lot → dial the opposite way.
  4. Verify OUT line: with 5 V applied to VCC, wave a hand in front of the sensor → OUT line goes HIGH (3.3 V) → multimeter confirms. If OUT stays low or stays high, the module is faulty — replace.

13.3 Wire to G469
