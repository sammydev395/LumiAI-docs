CNC batch bench prep — OAK-D camera plates + Ø54 heatsink pucks

Orchestration doc for the recurring CNC batch that cuts OAK-D camera mount plates (3× on one aluminium sheet) and Ø54 wand heatsink pucks (2× machined in place from the finned bar). Chains the three per-part docs below, locks the order, and carries the single-SHARPRO protection rules.

First instance: 2026-10-21 — 3 OAK-D plates + 2 Ø54 pucks, this doc's content was authored for that run and stays accurate for repeats on the same stock.

Order (operator, 2026-10-21): 3 OAK-D camera plates first, then 2 Ø54 wand heatsink pucks.

Why plates first: only ONE SHARPRO 1/8" O-flute on hand (no spare — operator-confirmed 2026-10-21). 6061 aluminium plates at 2 mm are low-risk (clean-cutting alloy, thin stock, single pass depth-wise); the finned 6063-ish bar is 10 mm deep, through fins, with chip-packing risk. If the bit dulls mid-session we lose the pucks, not the plates.

Stock reality (operator photos + answers, 2026-10-21)

Camera-plate stock

One 2 mm 6061 aluminium SHEET (uxcell, ~300 × 150). Previous cut consumed ~115 mm at one end (T-shaped cutout + slot + 2 small holes). Clear usable area: ~185 × 150 at the other end. Nest 3 plates on this one sheet; no separate 100 × 60 blanks on hand.

6061 is the preferred alloy for OAK-D plates — cuts cleanly (vs the gummy 1060 that's on the base-plate stock), good stiffness and yield for the camera-on-wrist-servo load, standard for anodizing / finishing if the operator wants a coloured plate later.

Spare on hand (fallback): 5052 Al, 200 × 200 × 2 mm, blue sandblasted / anodized. Not needed if the 6061 nesting works.

Heatsink-puck stock

300 × 60 × 10 mm FINNED heatsink extrusion, black-anodized. Longitudinal fins on ONE face; 10 mm total height = base + fin height (operator-confirmed). Likely 6063 alloy (common for extrusions); unverified but machines similarly to 6061. ~200 mm clear bar is left past the old Ø54 cutout at one end (~100 mm consumed from a 300 mm length). Machine 2 pucks IN PLACE from the bar — no sawing (operator decision).

Nesting layout

3 plates on the 300 × 150 6061 sheet

Mount the sheet with the 150 dimension along machine X, 300 dimension along machine Y. Used end (T-cutout) at high-Y; clear end at low-Y. Scribe 3 NC-zero marks along the sheet's Y axis before clamping. The plate was lifted +6 mm Y on the camera end 2026-10-29 (BigClaw-ear clearance) and is asymmetric about the NC zero — each scribe is 3 mm south of its plate's physical centre; tail/clear-end side faces Y=0, camera side faces Y=300:

Plate NC-zero scribe (X_sheet, Y_sheet from clear-end edge) Operator action
1 (X_centre, Y=29.5) Jog, zero XY at scribe, run NC
2 (X_centre, Y=89.5) Jog, zero XY at scribe, run NC
3 (X_centre, Y=149.5) Jog, zero XY at scribe, run NC

Each plate's cutter envelope: X ± 47.22 (long axis along machine X, plate's long 94.44 dim), Y − 24.06 to + 30.05 (short dim 54.11 along Y, plate 51). Scribes spaced 60 mm → 5.89 mm web between plate cutter envelopes. Plate 3's camera-side envelope at Y = 179.6, leaves 5.45 mm margin to the Y = 185 boundary before the used region. Plate 1's tail-side envelope at Y = 5.44, leaves 5.44 mm margin to the clear-end edge. Web toe clamps optional per operator judgment.

Hold-down (sheet-level):

  • 3M VHB or foam double-sided tape full-back under the sheet → rigid against spoilboard, prevents flex as plates are cut out.
  • Edge clamps on the sheet's long edges (clear of the plate row + used end).
  • M0 bolt-down per plate — 4× M3 × ~15 mm SHCS through each plate's just-cut OAK-D clearance holes, self-tap into virgin spoilboard beneath. Finger-snug.

No NC changes needed. Operator re-zeros XY per plate on the same loaded NC. No 90° rotation; the sheet orientation picks the right axis alignment.

2 pucks along the finned bar

Mount the whole bar flat on the fixture plate, base-face DOWN (fins UP). Clamp bar at both ends past the cutting area.

Puck Centre on bar (X_bar from clear-end edge of bar) Fixture-plate M3 taps (local to puck centre)
1 X = 37 (~10 mm margin past old Ø54 rim at bar's used end) (+6.928, +4), (−6.928, +4), (0, −8) at X_offset = 37
2 X = 102 (puck 1 + 65 mm centre-to-centre) same local pattern at X_offset = 102

Centre spacing 65 mm → 54 mm puck + 11 mm web of bar between pucks. Puck 2's upper edge at X = 129; leaves ~71 mm of bar past puck 2 for a future puck.

Pre-tap the fixture plate at 6 positions total (3 per puck). The fixture plate becomes puck-specific — stays with this bar-end machining workflow.

No NC changes needed. Operator zeros XY at puck 1 centre, runs NC, re-zeros XY at puck 2 centre (jog +65 mm in X), runs NC.

Waste behaviour, bar vs. 60 × 60 blank:

  • Orbit R=30.175 vs. bar half-width 30 → clips the bar's +Y and −Y edges at ±3.24 from each puck's X centre.
  • No 4 loose corner offcuts (that was a 60 × 60 blank artifact). The bar stays structurally continuous between pucks and to the clamped ends.
  • Puck comes free as the only loose piece after OP2. Confirm its 3 body bolts are still in before OP3 — the free puck is held ONLY by them.

Protecting the single SHARPRO bit (no spare)

One Ø3.175 1-flute O-flute, 1/4" shank, in 1/4" OZ8 collet. If it dulls or breaks, the session stops.

Feeds + DOC (both parts)

Part S (rpm) F XY (mm/min) F plunge DOC per pass Passes Rationale
Plates (6061, 2 mm) 18000 300 (NC default) 100 0.5 mm 5 (Z = −3.0 breakthrough) NC-defaults ship; 6061 cuts cleanly at 0.017 mm/tooth. No F override needed.
Pucks (6063 extrusion, 10 mm through fins) 18000 300 100 1.0 mm 10 (Z = −10.05) NC default — reduce F override to 50% if chatter at fin peaks

6061 does not demand the F=600 override that 1060 would. Run the NC's native feeds. Keep an eye on the flute for Al pickup — 6xxx can still weld if the cutter dulls, just much less aggressively than 1060.

Lubrication + air

Air blast or compressed-air hose on both parts, continuous. 6061 produces short chips that evacuate well with air alone; mist / cutting fluid is nice-to-have, not mandatory on the plates (unlike with the 1060 base-plate stock on hand, which stays on the shelf today).

For the pucks, air blast is important during OP2/OP3 — chips pack between fins.

Stop rules (if the bit starts to dull or weld)

Stop IMMEDIATELY if any of these appear:

  1. Chatter sound — pitch rises from steady whine to a chirp or screech. Fin-depth passes most likely to trigger.
  2. Chip appearance changes — chips go from flakes to STRINGERS or MUSH. Stringer = cutter is dragging material, not shearing. Mush = material is welding to the flute, then being re-dropped.
  3. Visible aluminium on the flute — pause between passes, inspect the cutter. Al appears as a silver smear or built-up blob on the cutting edge.
  4. Hot smell / smoke — cutter is overheating, likely dulling.
  5. Finished edge quality — rim shows parallel feed marks that are uneven in depth. Chatter is "polishing" the edge instead of cutting it.
  6. Material welded in a plate hole or puck slot — pause, pick out with a pin, inspect flute.

Response:

  • Stop at the next M0 (don't abort mid-pass if avoidable).
  • Clear the flute (brass pick, isopropyl alcohol, soft brush).
  • Resume. If chatter / weld returns within 1 pass, END THE SESSION on this bit. Switch to the Milwaukee drill alone (for OP1 only) and save the SHARPRO for a replacement run.

Replacement spec (for the order list)

SHARPRO 1/8" O-flute, 1/4" shank — exact-match spec:

  • Cutter diameter: Ø3.175 mm (= 1/8")
  • Shank diameter: Ø6.35 mm (= 1/4")
  • Flute count: 1 (single-flute O-flute, open-flute for Al chip evacuation)
  • Flute length: ≥15 mm (long enough for the heatsink puck's 10 mm Z depth plus clearance)
  • Overall length: ~50 mm (nominal)
  • Coating: uncoated OR ZrN (titanium-nitride variants build Al faster)
  • Geometry: polished O-flute (not Z-flute or compression)

The 6 answers to the ask

Camera plates (OAK-D, 6061 Al, 300 × 150 sheet)

  • Stock + Z depths: 2 mm 6061 Al (uxcell) sheet, NC max cut Z = −3.0. On 2.0 mm stock → 1.0 mm into spoilboard. In the clean-breakthrough window. No NC change needed — unchanged CameraMount_Oak-D.nc stands.
  • Nesting: 3 plates on ONE sheet, NC-zero scribes at Y=29.5 / Y=89.5 / Y=149.5 from the sheet's clear-end edge (each scribe 3 mm south of its plate's physical centre — camera-end lift 2026-10-29 made the plate asymmetric); re-zero XY per plate on the same NC. Webs 5.89 mm, end margins 5.44 / 5.45 mm. Full-back VHB keeps sheet rigid as plates sever; add web toe clamps if operator judges them needed.
  • Tool: SHARPRO 1/8" O-flute in 1/4" OZ8. No spare on hand — protect with air blast, inspection after each plate.
  • Alloy: 6061, clean-cutting. NC-default feeds (F=300 at S=18000, 0.5 mm DOC per pass, 5 passes to Z=-3.0). Mild burrs expected; 400-grit deburr. Fallback stock: 5052 Al 200 × 200 × 2 mm (blue anodized) on the shelf.
  • Stiffness: 2 mm 6061 OK for OAK-D on wrist servo. 6061 is already the preferred alloy.
  • Runtime: ~15 min per plate × 3 = ~45 min batch.
  • M0 bolt-down: 4× M3 × 15 mm SHCS per plate, through just-cut OAK-D clearance holes, self-tap into virgin spoilboard.

Heatsink pucks (v9.10, Ø54, finned 6063 extrusion, machined from the bar)

  • Stock: 300 × 60 × 10 mm finned extrusion (base + fins = 10). Clear bar ~200 mm past the old Ø54 cutout. 2 pucks fit.
  • Workflow change: machine IN-PLACE from the bar. No sawing. Zero XY at puck 1 centre (X=37 from bar clear-end), run NC, re-zero at puck 2 centre (X=102, +65 mm from puck 1).
  • Waste: no 4 loose corner offcuts (that was a 60 × 60 blank artifact). Orbit clips ±Y bar edges at ±3.24 from each puck X; bar stays structurally continuous.
  • Fixture plate: 6 pre-tapped M3 at puck 1 and puck 2 offsets. The 3-bolt hold-down works through fins (M3 × 25 SHCS passes through fin peak + base above each hole into fixture tap).
  • Tooling: T1 Milwaukee 1/8" cobalt (1/8" OZ8) for OP1; T2 SHARPRO 1/8" O-flute (1/4" OZ8) for OP2 + OP3. Collet change at puck's own M0 #1.
  • Fin cutting: cutter hits fin peaks first, then base. Chips pack in fin valleys — air blast during OP2/OP3 is mandatory. If chatter at fin peaks, F override 50%.
  • Alloy: likely 6063 (common for Al extrusions); unverified. Machines like 6061 (slightly softer), chip-weld risk lower than 1060 but WD-40 helps.
  • Runtime per puck: ~10 min cutting + ~15 min operator overhead. ~50 min batch for 2 pucks.

Hazards

  • Plates on 6061: chip evacuation + flute pickup are the risks. One SHARPRO, no spare. Air blast on, inspect flute between plates, STOP at the first stringer or stuck Al. 6061 behaves cleanly — this is low-risk relative to the pucks.
  • Puck fin cutting: chatter at fin peaks (thin standing walls) + chip packing in fin valleys. Air blast + F override 50% if chatter.
  • Bar workholding: VHB under the clear region; clamp at both bar ends. If bar flexes during OP2 contour, puck X zero drifts.
  • Puck M0 #2: old v9.10 doc assumed 4 loose corner offcuts; from the bar, there are NO loose offcuts. M0 #2 is a chip-clearance pause only (clear chips packed in fin valleys around the orbit; the puck is the only loose piece and is held by 3 body bolts).
