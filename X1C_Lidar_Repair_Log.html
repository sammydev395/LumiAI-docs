Bambu X1C — Micro Lidar failure & repair log

Printer: Bambu Lab X1-Carbon Combo, purchased 2024-01-30 (MatterHackers, order 101-0000300-7857002). Warranty expired 2025-01 — all parts paid.

Problem (first seen ~2026-07, logged 2026-10-05)

The build-plate marker read fails. Every print start takes 3–5 restarts before the marker reads successfully. Three months of this.

The reader is the Micro Lidar on the bottom of the toolhead. It reads the ArUco tag printed on the build plate, and it is also what drives flow-dynamics calibration and first-layer inspection. Bed levelling does not use it — that is the nozzle force sensor — which is why levelling has been unaffected throughout.

Ruled out before ordering

Recorded so nobody repeats three months of it:

Tried Result
Plate marker cleaned with IPA 91, many times no change
Lidar lens cleaned, many times no change
Ambient light removed (room already dark) / added, many times no change
Reading on both halves of the plate fails on both — so not localised marker damage

⚠️ The diagnosis is this line: a camera light aimed at the marker is what works best. If the read only succeeds with external illumination, the module's own emitter output has fallen off. That is a degrading lidar, not a dirty one — and it is why cleaning never helped and never would.

Why two parts and not one

The machine is the old "Single Red Laser" build — ✅ confirmed by direct observation 2026-10-05: a blue laser is visible during the first-layer scan. Despite the name, that variant is a blue + IR pair (the dual-red build shows two red dots and no blue), and it carries a small extra PCB on the toolhead. Its lidar SN reads 00ExxA.

The single-red lidar and its TH board are discontinued. The sanctioned replacement is the dual-red pair — lidar and TH board, installed together. A dual-red lidar will not work on the old single-red board. This pairing is exactly what Bambu ships as the warranty replacement when a single-red machine fails, so it is the official upgrade path, not a workaround.

✅ SN read off the old lidar on removal, 2026-10-09: 00E09B3C.

⚠️ It contradicts the 00ExxA rule above, and the rule is what loses. The build is confirmed single-red by direct observation — the blue laser during the first-layer scan — and an eyewitness beats a parse of a serial whose field layout we never established (it is 8 characters; 00ExxA/00ExxB is 6, so which character is the variant marker was an assumption, not a fact).

Do not use the 00ExxA / 00ExxB rule to buy or reject a part again. It came from a third-party article, it was never confirmed against Bambu documentation, and the one time it could be tested against a known machine it gave the wrong answer. Identify the variant by laser colour during a scan: blue visible = the old single-red (blue + IR) build; two red dots and no blue = the dual-red build.

Nothing about this session's repair changes — the dual-red pair was the sanctioned replacement for this build, it was fitted as a set, and the build-plate marker read has worked on every attempt since.

Until the parts arrive — printing continues, detection ON

The operator is running with build-plate detection left ON and simply restarting the job until the marker reads. Once it gets past the plate read, the print runs normally. Nothing has been disabled.

That is the better trade while it still reads at all, because everything stays armed:

Check State Notes
Build-plate detection ON costs 3–5 restarts per print start
Flow dynamics calibration ON lidar-driven, still working
First-layer inspection ON lidar-driven, still working
Bed levelling ON nozzle force sensor, never affected
Spaghetti detection ON chamber camera, never affected

📌 Diagnostically this matters. The lidar still does flow calibration and first-layer inspection fine — it is specifically the marker read that fails. The ArUco tag is the lowest-contrast target the module is asked to resolve, so a weakened emitter loses that one first while the brighter tasks still pass. It is degradation, not a dead module, and it is consistent with the camera-light tell.

The off switch is the fallback, not the current practice. Disabling build-plate detection removes the restarts, but it also takes flow calibration and first-layer inspection with it, and then nothing verifies the installed plate against the slice — plates 1–2 of lumiAi_Complete.3mf need Engineering, the rest Cool. Only reach for it if the read stops succeeding altogether before the parts land.

Did it work?

A gate that cannot fail is not a gate — so record the number, not an impression.

  • Baseline before the swap: 3–5 restarts per print start for a successful marker read.
  • After install, re-enable detection and record first-read success over the next 5 print starts.

📌 2026-10-08 — THE CLEAREST STATEMENT OF THE FAULT: IT HAS NO MARGIN LEFT. After three failed reads in a row on the new plate, the operator wiped the lidar lens with IPA and the next read went straight through. His words: "it seems to be on the edge here and even a little lens clouding degrades it enough to not be able to read the marker."

That is the whole three months in one sentence. A healthy lidar reads an ArUco tag through a slightly clouded lens without noticing. This one needs the lens perfect to clear the threshold at all — so every clean restored it to just barely working, it drifted back under within a few prints, and the cleaning appeared to "work" while fixing nothing. It also explains why a camera light helped: both are the same thing, propping a weak signal over the line.

Cleaning buys prints, not a repair. Wipe the lens before a long job as a stopgap until the swap.

OVERTURNED, same day. The marker-read failures came back on the NEW plate. One clean first read was luck, not a pattern — which is exactly why it was logged provisionally with "record the next five print starts" rather than as a verdict. The next ones failed.

✅ So the fault IS the lidar. Same symptom on a brand-new plate with an unworn ArUco marker rules the marker out as the cause. The Micro Lidar + TH Board Set in transit are the REPAIR, not spares — do not return them.

The superseded entry is kept below, because the experiment was the right one to run and the reasoning was sound; it just came back the other way.

🔶 2026-10-08 — FIRST EVIDENCE, AND IT POINTS AWAY FROM THE LIDAR. A brand-new Bambu Textured PEI plate went on (bought for an unrelated reason — PETG kept coming off the Engineering plate) and the plate-marker scan read first time, no restarts. Against a 3–5 restart baseline on the old plate.

That is the diagnostic that was never run: the same lidar, a different marker. If it holds, the fault is a worn ArUco marker on the old plate, not the lidar module — and three months were spent cleaning and then replacing a sensor that was reading a degraded target.

⚠️ One read is not a pattern. Record the next five print starts on the new plate before concluding. If they all read first time, the lidar is fine.

⬜ If confirmed: the Micro Lidar + TH Board Set become spares — not confirmed; they are the repair. Worth keeping rather than returning given this machine has eaten two print heads and a lidar-class fault in ~33 months — but the $135 bought insurance, not a fix.

📌 Second time in two days a cheap consumable turned out to be the real cost: a $40 plate here, and before it the two years of adhesion trouble the same plate would have prevented. Both were invisible because they presented as "printing is just like this."

📌 2026-10-11 — THE NEW SET FAILS THE SAME WAY, AND THE EVIDENCE MOVES UPSTREAM. "Failed to read the build plate marker" is back on the PEI plate — on the brand-new Micro Lidar + V9 TH board, after roughly 15 good prints. Firmware current (01.12.00.00). Plate edge cleaned with soap and water, then IPA; door closed, lights out — no change. Two observations carry the diagnosis:

  1. A phone light on the marker makes it read more often — the same prop that worked on the old lidar.
  2. The lidar's own white capture LED is BRIGHT but now flashes only for an extremely short moment over the marker — and "now" is the word that matters: it used to stay on longer. Something is degrading, and firmware does not degrade.

An external research session (2026-10-11, transcript held by the operator) frames it in a way that fits every data point this log holds:

  • The marker read is CAMERA + WHITE LED; calibration uses the red laser lines, not the LED — which is why the 6-step calibration passes while the marker read fails (Bambu HMS 0C00-0100-0001-000A is exactly this class).
  • TWO lidars and TWO TH boards have now "failed" the same way, each after ~15 prints. A fault that follows the symptom through a complete parts swap is not in the parts. Prime suspect: the USB-C toolhead cable — it carries toolhead power, it flexes on every move, and a worn one can pass data while sagging under the LED's load, cutting the flash short exactly as observed. Replacement is in the Partsbuilt cart, $12.99.
  • ⚠️ This REFRAMES two closed entries rather than merely adding one: the 10-08 "the fault IS the lidar" verdict and the 10-10 "lens cleaning is the known failure mode" stood on the same misread — the missing margin was LIGHT, not lens clarity or module health. Lens wipes, the phone light and a fresh high-contrast marker are all one move: propping a starved exposure over the threshold.

The gate, so this stays a measurement and not an impression:

  1. Slow-motion phone video of the white flash during the plate check BEFORE the cable swap, plus a printer log export.
  2. Swap the USB-C toolhead cable. Same video after.
  3. Re-enable detection and record first-read success over the next 5 print starts.
  4. Flash still short on the new cable → it is the AP-board side: Bambu ticket with both videos + the log (micro lidar + AI selected). Two replaced lidars, two TH boards and a new cable is the strongest case this machine can make.

Meanwhile (operator, 2026-10-11): BUILD PLATE DETECTION IS OFF and printing is fine. Detection off costs nothing in print quality — levelling is the force sensor, and flow-dynamics / first-layer inspection still run. The one lost guard is the wrong-plate check: plate type drives bed temp, so keep each job's plate set to Textured PEI (the X1C is all-PLA at 55).

Marker stock: the operator printed Bambu's marker sheet (Plate_QR_US letter.pdf — 4× each of Textured / High Temp / Engineering / Cool, dashed cut lines). Before applying one: caliper the printed square against the original on the plate (~5 mm; a scaled print is the usual homemade-marker failure), cut on the dashed line keeping the white quiet-zone, and seal inkjet with a MATTE clear coat — gloss glare is exactly what a short flash cannot punch through. A fresh marker buys margin; the cable test above is the repair path.
