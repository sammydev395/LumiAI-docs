LumiAI Electronics — Phased Bring-Up Plan

Purpose. Sequenced electrical + software bring-up of the LumiAI Ultra bay + wand + arm build after every physical component is already mounted (bay STM32 + Pi5 stack, G469 + MECCANIXITY FFC chain, PSU, HL-PBB 13.8 V distribution block, INA226-MAIN, TMP102-bay, TMP102-wand, PCA9685, 12× LED drivers, LED MCPCB stack, wand fan, 2× bay fans, 4× HC-SR501 PIR array on the fence slants, SPS30 + ZE15-CO on the base plate, arm servos, BigClaw gripper, ZK-SJ20 servo-rail buck, AC inlet + 2× rockers, all harnessing).

Bay v3 (carrier b6a53ce7), every build from 2026-11-26: one printed power-bay carrier (Power_Bay §11.3.9), the HL-PBB block, the ZK-SJ20 servo-rail buck (7.50 V), F1 the only fuse, the DC rocker upstream of the whole 13.8 V bus. Bay I²C devices daisy-chain: 0x44 MAIN · 0x45 BATT · 0x47 INA3 (buck output) · 0x48 TMP102. Build packs (parts, mount order, crimp + cut list, wiring, checks):

  • Ultra = Creator desk build: phone (IP6518), no wand tether, no PIRs (operator 2026-11-25). Assembly Guide §16f.
  • Halo = ceiling build: 4 PIRs + the wand tether, no phone, no SPS30 / ZE15. Its new bay is pre-built off the robot and swapped in, in about half a day, on the day its ZK-SJ20 has passed its gate (~Dec 6); until then Halo runs on its old bay. Assembly Guide §16g.

Phases 4 (wand TMP102), 5 (wand) and 6 (PIRs) apply to Halo (ceiling) only; Ultra skips them.

Read this doc top-to-bottom in order. Each phase gates the next — do not skip. Every phase points to the authoritative wiring / mod / node documentation elsewhere; this doc is the sequencing and go/no-go layer, not a re-derivation of wiring.

Applies to: LumiAI Ultra production build, 2026-08-05 onward, and LumiAI Halo (host raspberrypidev, the Ultra-gen2 build; arm only, no wheels). Where a step names Ultra's host raspberrypistage, Halo uses raspberrypidev (192.168.1.158). Per-unit progress: Build status — bay v3 rebuild. Owning workstream: LumiAI-Ultra electronics build.

Phase 0 — Power-only bring-up

Goal: everything electrically UP, nothing intelligent yet.

Authoritative reference: the build pack's checks: §16f.5 (Ultra) / §16g.6 (Halo, the swap), built on Power_Bay §16.1–16.2. The ZK-SJ20 passes its Power_Bay §5c bench gate before it goes into any bay.

Before any power (system off):

  • Earth: C14 E ↔︎ PSU case metal < 0.1 Ω. Tug-test every mains termination.
  • No shorts: HL-PBB A ↔︎ B, PSU +V ↔︎ −V, BATT + ↔︎ − at the pack connector.
  • BATT lead metered end-to-end for polarity before it ever mates. Ultra: PHONE-IN polarity at the IP6518 pads.
  • INA226 shunts ~2 mΩ; the I²C nets metered back to their G469 pins; G469 pin 1 ↔︎ Pi GPIO 1 through the FFC.

Steady state after Phase 0:

  • PSU CH1 = 13.8 V ± 0.1.
  • DC rocker ON: HL-PBB A bus = 13.8 V, B bus = GND.
  • Mus-14 USB-C = 5.0–5.2 V no-load, before the HAT cable goes in (it feeds the HAT's own loads; the HAT doesn't backfeed the Pi).
  • ZK-SJ20 = 7.50 V at the unmated Deans; mated, the STM32 blue terminal reads 7.5 V, the STM32 boots and powers the Pi5 from its 5 V port (green + red LEDs; SSH reachable). Halo: until its buck lands (§16g.7), the STM32 and the Pi stay dark; only the power-side lines here can be checked.
  • Battery: F1 fuse in, pack mated; INA226-BATT reads positive (charging).
  • Both bay fans at full RPM (PWM floating = 100 %).
  • Ultra: IP6518 cool with no load, then charges a phone.
  • Halo: wand feed at A2 = 13.8 V vs B2; at the wand end's 6-pin, pin 1 = 13.6–13.9 V vs pin 2 (GND-power), not pin 6 (GND-logic) (§10.5.2).
  • G469 polarity + pin-1 check (§4 step 5): pin 1 = 3.3 V, pins 2 / 4 = 5 V vs GND. Any other reading fails Phase 0 and gates every downstream phase.
  • i2cdetect -y 1: 0x44, 0x45, 0x47, 0x48 (Halo + wand: also 0x49). INA3 (0x47) reads positive with the arm powered.

Hazards:

  • The DC rocker is upstream of the whole 13.8 V bus: DC OFF kills the block, the buck, the Mus-14, the fans, the phone charger and the tether. The PSU and its mains strip stay live whenever the C14 is plugged in, and the battery keeps CH1 up through AC loss. Unplug the C14 and unmate the battery before touching the bay.
  • F1 (10 A, at the pack) is the only fuse. Every block branch is unfused by design.
  • The ZK-SJ20's CC is headroom (18 A); it limits, it doesn't latch. Its CV must never sit above 8.4 V (the servo-bus voltage).
  • Fans at full RPM are loud. Expected; don't chase it.
  • Both illuminated rockers must light when ON. A dark lamp = LAMP tap wrong; always-lit = LINE / LOAD swapped.

Phase 1 — Arm servo bus wake-up + per-joint verification (CONDITIONAL — skip by default)

When to run this phase:

  • A servo was replaced on this arm (e.g., the 2026-04-28 elbow-servo cannibalization — see memory/project_2026_04_28_arm_slam_incident.md; the burnt elbow servo was replaced by cannibalizing one from the parts bin), or
  • The arm is a fresh build with never-verified servos (e.g. Halo, 2026-09-27 — see Phase 1 as run on Halo), or
  • The servo daisy-chain cable was re-terminated / re-routed and needs continuity re-confirmed, or
  • The STM32 firmware was flashed / updated and the arm-protocol side wants a smoke test before general use.

When to SKIP this phase (the default):

  • Routine bring-ups on a known-good arm. This Ultra was upgraded from an older ArmPi FPC (added a custom base-servo plate + bigger base servo to convert to Ultra); all the arm-cavity servos are the original ArmPi-FPC Hiwonder units that have been running on this frame for months and are known-good. No routine reason to re-verify them each build. Go straight to Phase 2 (BigClaw gripper).

Goal (if the phase runs): arm servo daisy-chain live on the STM32 protocol; each joint jogs individually under motor_controller_node. No HOME command yet.

Preconditions: Phase 0 complete. Servo daisy-chain cable landed at STM32.

Bring-up steps (bench-only, arm supported by hand):

  1. Boot STM32 + Pi5. Verify the STM32 arm-protocol serial port is present under its udev-pinned name: /dev/stm32_ultra on Ultra, /dev/stm32_halo on Halo. Both are pinned by physical USB port, because a second CH340 can swap ttyUSB0/ttyUSB1 at boot — and that second CH340 is permanent on Halo: the iFLYTEK R818's serial cable lives on Pi5 black USB2 #2 at Phase 7.5 and on a HAT port from Phase 7.6, pinned as /dev/iflytek_halo alongside /dev/stm32_halo. Never configure a raw /dev/ttyUSBn — a wrong-port open resets the STM32 and snaps the arm to startup pose. Opening this port resets the STM32 — read the reset hazard in Cross-phase safety references before running anything that opens it.
  2. Launch motor_controller_node in read-only mode (no command topic bindings). Verify current servo positions publish on /joint_states — each joint reports a sane pulse/angle within its declared range.
  3. Support the arm by hand. Send a single 5° jog to the base joint only. Verify motion is smooth and stops at the commanded position.
  4. Repeat for shoulder, elbow (the replaced servo — extra attention), wrist, wrist-rot. Each joint individually, arm supported by hand between commands.
  5. Verify no bus timeouts, no servo brownouts, no thermal cutouts on any servo (STM32 will report packet failures if a servo drops off).

Phase 1 as run on Halo (2026-09-27) — procedure and lessons

This is the procedure that actually brought Halo's arm up, in order, including the traps hit on the way. It uses the general servo replacement procedure for the per-servo steps.

Target servo map (same on Ultra and Halo): 1 = gripper (HTS-25L), 2 = wrist_rot, 3 = wrist, 4 = elbow (LX-15D), 5 = shoulder, 6 = base (HTS-25L). The bus is a daisy chain wired 6 → 5 → 4 → 3 → 2 → 1, so unplugging one servo drops everything downstream of it.

Step 1 — Set each servo's ID on the bench pigtail, one servo alone on the STM32 bus port. Read the ID first; never trust the ID a "new" servo ships with. On Halo the elbow arrived pre-programmed as ID 6 and the base as ID 1. Follow reprogramming a servo's ID. Broadcast ID writes (0xFE) are only safe with exactly one servo connected.

Step 2 — Index each horn at pulse 500 before coupling it to its linkage. Command the bare shaft to 500, then couple, then tighten the horn's center screw fully. Follow horn indexing. The Halo base was found with its horn center screw missing and the linkage clocked 90° off: it drove under motor torque but slipped and bound under load, and presented as a servo that "won't move". Decoupled, the same servo swept full range. Re-coupled correctly it passed full range (0 ↔︎ 1000) under the full arm load.

Step 3 — Audit IDs on the full chain before any motion. Ping IDs 1–6 (and a wider range). A servo that is powered (LED on) but silent means two servos share an ID — their replies collide. On Halo the wrist also carried ID 4, colliding with the elbow. Fix without a broadcast write and without cutting the chain: with the chain ending at the elbow, move the elbow to a temporary ID (4 → 14), plug the wrist back in and move it 4 → 3, then move the elbow 14 → 4.

Step 4 — Match the pose to Ultra (pose parity) so one calibration serves every arm. Read Ultra's live /ultra/joint_states, convert to pulses (pulse = pulse_at_zero_rad + rad / radians_per_pulse, from ultra.urdf.xacro), move Halo to the same pulses one joint at a time, and compare side by side. Correct each mismatch with the servo's EEPROM deviation offset: set the offset, force a real move (e.g. target + 14 → target — an offset change on a servo holding a steady target did not visibly apply), compare, then save the offset to EEPROM. About 4.2 counts per degree. Mechanical mismatches get fixed mechanically first — on Halo the wrist servo mount and the gripper assembly were each rotated 180° to match Ultra. Final Halo offsets: 1: 0 · 2: +34 · 3: −23 · 4: +47 · 5: −2 · 6: +50 (base +50 also centres it on the chosen forward mark). Result: the same pulse gives the same pose on both robots, so Halo runs Ultra's arm calibration unchanged.

Step 5 — Bring up the ROS stack, dry run first. Halo uses arm-only variants in hiwonder_ros2_control (repo armpi_ultra): description/halo.urdf.xacro (no wheel joints, no IMU), config/halo_controllers.yaml (joint_state_broadcaster + arm_controller), launch/halo_arm.launch.py (namespace /halo, joy + arm_teleop). The launch defaults to dry_run:=true, which never opens the serial port — use it to prove the nodes, controllers, namespace and DDS before going live.

Step 6 — Go live only from the firmware startup pose. Park the arm at [gripper 210, wrist_rot 500, wrist 110, elbow 825, shoulder 600, base 500] first, then launch with dry_run:=false. Opening the port resets the STM32, which drives every servo to exactly that pose at full speed; starting there makes the snap zero-distance. On activation the hardware interface re-seeds its targets from the servos' real positions (and refuses to activate if that read fails), so the arm then holds still. Verify /halo/joint_states converts back to the startup pulses.

Step 7 — Jog, then teleop. Gamepad: hold B + sticks = arm, X with B = 2× turbo, SELECT = latched E-stop. Start with the speed cap low (arm_max_rate:=0.5), then raise it to the default 3.4 rad/s once every axis has been driven through full range.

Vendor SDK traps (ros_robot_controller_sdk.py), learned the hard way:

  • bus_servo_enable_torque(id, 1) unloads (servo goes limp) and bus_servo_enable_torque(id, 0) loads — the reverse of what the name says. The vendor's own unloadBusServo() passes 1.
  • bus_servo_stop() takes a list of IDs ([6]), not a single integer.
  • Do not run per-command scripts: every script opens and closes the port, and every open/close resets the STM32. Hold one session open for the whole bench job.

Goal: gripper opens + closes on ROS2 command; no bus contention with arm servos.

Preconditions: Phase 1 complete. Gripper servo (Hiwonder HTS-25L) already installed in the BigClaw jaw per memory/project_arm_gripper_upgrade.md.

Bring-up steps:

  1. Verify gripper appears in /joint_states at its own servo ID (bus is still the arm daisy-chain).
  2. Command the gripper to open slowly (fraction of full stroke first). Verify motion + no bus errors.
  3. Command close. Verify jaw closes without stalling before target.
  4. Repeat open/close 5 × in a row — check for consistent latency and no thermal cutout.
  5. Object test: insert a wand-diameter cylinder in the jaw. Verify jaw closes on it and holds without slip.

6.0 Pot + jumper adjustment — at the BENCH, before the PIR goes into the fence

Source: SR501 Motion Sensor.pdf (Handson Technology). Page references below are to that PDF. Same procedure as Power_Bay_Design.md §13.1; this is the bring-up copy with the reasons.

⚠️ Do this BEFORE mounting. After mounting, the pots cannot be reached. The board screws flush on the fence's inner wall with the component side facing into the bay, so the outside shows only the dome through the Ø24 opening. The pots sit along the long edge opposite the header, adjuster faces pointing into the bay at Z ≈ 9–41 from the plate. With the carrier fitted (its underside at Z 44, 3 mm above the board top), a screwdriver can't get at them. Even with the carrier off, the lid and arm are on top, so on a built robot any re-adjustment means taking the bay apart. Halo's four PIRs were bench-set before mounting (2026-08), so don't re-adjust them; walk-test only (6.2). Each new board (Nova, spares) is set and walk-tested on the bench first.

6.1 Bench walk test (each board, before it goes into the fence)

  1. Power the board at 5 V (VCC/GND), with a meter or logic probe on OUT. Wait 60 s. The module "requires nearly a minute to initialize" and can false-trigger during that time (p. 1 to p. 2).
  2. Hand-wave at ~0.5 m: OUT goes HIGH (3.3 V). Stop moving: OUT drops after ≈ 3 s, then the ≈ 3 s blind period.
  3. Keep waving continuously for ~10 s: OUT must stay HIGH throughout. That proves the jumper is at H.
  4. Walk across the dome's view at ≈ 3 m: it should trigger. Walk across at ≈ 5 m: it should mostly NOT trigger. That proves sensitivity is at the low end.
  5. Label the board with its position (FL / FR / BL / BR) once it passes, then mount it.

6.2 Walk test once hung (the robot in its installed position, 60 s after power-on)

  1. Use ros2 topic echo /presence/pir. Walk a circle of ~2.5–3 m radius around the robot at normal pace. Each bit should go HIGH in its own quadrant: FL / FR toward the front (+Y) corners, BL / BR toward the back, with overlaps where neighbouring cones meet (sides 39°, back 15°; EXTERNAL_SENSOR_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md E.2).
  2. Front blind lane: walk straight toward the FRONT wall along its centreline, starting at ~4 m. At the datasheet's 110° cone there's a 13° gap between the FL and FR cones there, ~0.14 m wide at the wall and ~0.8 m wide at 3 m (E.2). Record the distance at which FL or FR first triggers, or "none". Then walk the same line ~0.5 m to each side; that should trigger the FL / FR on that side. Write the results in E.2. This tells us how wide the lane really is on our boards (the PDF's own bound is 120°, which would leave only a 3° gap).
  3. Stand still for 5 min. No bit stays HIGH (the node warns at stuck_high_warn_s: 300).
  4. If one PIR false-triggers in place (fan plume, sun, HVAC), check first that its sensitivity is really at the CW end, on the bench before mounting. A PIR already at minimum sensitivity that still false-triggers is an environment issue (baffle / aim), not a pot setting. On a built robot, record it; don't open the bay to chase it.

Cross-phase safety references

  • Arm safety: no HOME / multi-joint pose until Phase 8 sign-off per the gamepad integration plan.
  • STM32 resets whenever its serial port is opened or closed (verified on Halo, 2026-09-27). On these Hiwonder controllers DTR is wired to the STM32 reset line. Every open or close of the port — a script exiting, a stack restart, even stty — reboots the board, and the firmware then drives every servo to its startup pose [210, 500, 110, 825, 600, 500] at full speed. On Halo this tipped the base over once. Clearing the port's HUPCL flag did not prevent it. Rules: clamp the base; hold one serial session open for a whole bench job instead of per-command scripts; open or restart anything that owns the port only with the arm already parked at the startup pose, so the snap is zero-distance. Restarting any ROS node other than ros2_control_node (for example arm_teleop) does not touch the port and is safe. Recognise the signature: joints reading ≈ 210/500/110/825/600/500 right after a port open mean the board just reset.
  • Power discipline: AC rocker OFF between wiring changes. The DC rocker is upstream of the whole 13.8 V bus (bay v3): DC OFF darkens the block and everything on it, but the PSU and its mains strip stay live while the C14 is plugged in.
  • Wand thermal discipline: do not hold full-bright > 30 s on the LED bank without airflow. TMP102-wand telemetry (Phase 4) must be live before any sustained LED work.
  • Hazard-sensor discipline: do not blow into the SPS30 intake (moisture kills the laser). Do not over-provoke the ZE15-CO (electrochemical cell wears with cumulative exposure).
