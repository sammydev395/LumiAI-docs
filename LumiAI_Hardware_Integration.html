Lumi integration notes — battery

  • Fleet-standard UPS battery for the LumiAI bay per Power/Compact_UPS_Power_System_HiWonder_RPi5.md.
  • Wiring: connect BAT+ → 10 A fuse → PSC-100-12 CH2 BAT+; connect BAT− → PSC-100-12 CH2 BAT− directly. Keep leads short (< 20 cm) to minimize IR drop during the fault-clearing peak the BMS may need to sink.
  • Pack-side connectors as shipped (ALEAIVY, verified 2026-08-05): the pack has two connectors on the discharge leads — a male XT30 (main power, ~15 A continuous / 30 A burst rated) and a small JST (almost certainly the BMS balance port — 4-pin JST-XH-class, low-current signal only; NOT intended as a power path). Do NOT use the JST for the PSU CH2 connection — it's not gauge-rated for the ~5-7 A continuous UPS current, let alone peaks. Leave the JST unused (the BMS is internal to the pack and self-balancing per spec; no external balance charger needed).
  • Bay-side connector — cut XT30, replace with heat-shrink Faston spade pair (fleet-standard for the LiFePO4 tap, locked 2026-08-05): a female XT30 is not in bench stock. Cut the pack's male XT30 off, strip the two discharge leads, and terminate with male + female 6.3 × 0.8 mm insulated Faston spades from the Kuject 320-pc heat-shrink terminal kit (see Electronics_Assembly_Guide.md §1.2 consumables Kuject row) — blue band (AWG 16-14), matching the pack's ~16 AWG discharge leads. Kuject terminals have integrated heat-shrink insulation on the crimp barrel: strip, crimp with the Haisstronica ratchet crimper, then hit with the heat-shrink gun until the barrel-sleeve seals watertight around the wire. Convention: male Faston on the pack's +V lead, female Faston on the pack's GND lead (or vice-versa, pick one and be consistent across the fleet). Mating pair goes on the PSU CH2 leads with the opposite polarity, with the 10 A fuse in-line on the +V leg between the Faston and the PSU CH2 BAT+ screw terminal. Why heat-shrink Faston and not bullets or bare screw: already in bench stock (Kuject kit, no additional SKU); crimp-only, no solder; heat-shrink integrated, no separate insulation step; blade rating (~15-20 A for a blue-band 6.3 mm Faston) is comfortable margin over the 5-7 A steady UPS current + BMS fault-clearing peaks; keys the polarity by dissimilar (M/F) blades so the pack can't be re-plugged backwards after service.
  • Cold-charge cutoff caveat: if this build ships into an unheated garage / northern climate deployment, verify the BMS implements low-temperature charge cutoff (< 5 °C). Many low-cost BMS chips lack this — see Power/Compact_UPS_Power_System_HiWonder_RPi5.md §"Cold-charge" for the mitigation options (external NTC comparator, Pi-side firmware cutoff via INA226 + MOSFET, or spec a BMS with native cold-charge cutoff for production).
  • Storage / long-term care: for units that sit unpowered > 1 month, top-charge to ~13.6 V (~90 % SoC) before shelving. LiFePO4 self-discharge is low (~3 %/month) but sitting at full charge accelerates capacity fade.
  • Do NOT deep-discharge repeatedly to the BMS cutoff (10 V) — reduces cycle life. Set the Pi-side UPS-management logic to trigger a graceful shutdown at ~11.5 V pack voltage (leaves ~15 % SoC as a floor).

Lumi integration notes — DROK

  • Fleet-standard STM32 rail source for the LumiAI bay: DROK input from PSC-100-12 CH1 (13.8 V) via distribution-block position A2, output to the STM32 controller's blue terminal-block power input at 7.5 V (Hiwonder's own adapter voltage; it is also the arm's servo-bus voltage, ≤ 8.4 V ceiling) (see Power_Bay_Design.md §11.3 for the wiring path + power sequencing; Electronics_Bay_Design.md §4 Route B for the physical cable route).

  • Bay mount: DROK mounts INSIDE the electronics bay on horizontal posts on the fence front vertical wall; the fence has external cutouts for the DROK's LCD, FUNC buttons, and V/I pots so the user can read / adjust without opening the bay (see Power_Bay_Design.md §11.3 fence-wall layouts + the CAD reference image fence_front_drok_mount_and_access_cutouts.png).

  • Bench-calibration procedure — DO THIS BEFORE LANDING D14/D15 ON THE STM32 (unloaded, safe sequence, added 2026-08-09):

    Vendor manual: DROK Buck Boost.pdf for the FUNC-button sequences + LCD field definitions.

    1. AC rocker ON, DC rocker ON → DROK sees 13.8 V input (LCD Vin field). Since the DC rocker sits between distribution-block A2 and DROK input (per Power_Bay_Design.md §11.3 wiring segment map row D7/D8), both rockers must be ON for the DROK to have input power.
    2. STM32 disconnected — D14/D15 wires terminated in XHF ferrules on the STM32 end, but NOT yet inserted into the STM32 blue terminal block. This is the "unloaded" state: DROK is powered but has no load; safe to sweep V-set through its full range without damaging anything downstream.
    3. V-set pot (CV) — turn slowly clockwise, watch the LCD Vout field. Target: 7.50 V ± 0.05 (tighter than the ±0.1 default in the Assembly Guide checklist — you're setting once for the lifetime of the build, so spend the extra 30 seconds getting it exact). Multimeter on the DROK output terminals is more accurate than the LCD for the final trim — LCD typically lags actual by 20-50 mV. Final adjustment with the multimeter reading.
    4. I-set pot (CC) — turn it fully CW and leave it there. In the bay the DROK's CC does not work, so it is not a fault backstop. The DROK manual: IN− must not be tied to OUT−, or the CC function is invalid. In the bay they are tied outside the DROK: OUT− runs to the STM32 ground, which the Pi shares through the STM32's 5 V port, and the Pi's ground runs back to the distribution block through the HAT's Mus-14 feed. A high CC setting also keeps the CC limit from throttling the servos at their peaks. Bench check (Ultra's DROK, out of the bay, wired straight to an e-load with its IN− and OUT− kept apart): 8 A continuous at 11.5 V out, the heatsink barely warm with the fan on; then, after the CC pot was run from fully counter-clockwise up to its clockwise end, 11.0 A at 11.1 V (~122 W) with the CC light off (held ~30 s, fan on, fed from the PSC-100-12 with its battery connected, so the pack supplied the input above the PSU's ~7.7 A; the DROK display reads ~1.5 % high). With the pot at its clockwise end it goes to about 11.6–11.8 A (the operator's reading). The earlier "CC at ~9 A" was the pot setting, not the DROK's limit. The CC pot is the only current setting (no menu override). Sustained hold at 11.5 A for 5–6 min or more, 11.1 V setpoint (bench, fan on, PSU battery connected): the DROK heatsink stayed at ~28.5 °C, the PSC-100-12 case reached ~33 °C (with its fan), and the output held ~10.97–10.98 V at the load. The stock heatsink and fan are enough.
    5. DC rocker OFF → DROK output drops to ~0 V (LCD Vout may show ~0.1 V residual from its own draw). This confirms the DC rocker is actually in the DROK power path and gives you a safe state before landing D14/D15.
    6. NOW land D14/D15 on the STM32 blue terminal (via the XHF ferrules on the STM32 end + Kuject M4 fork lugs bent up on the DROK output end).
    7. DC rocker ON → STM32 boots, Pi5 boots off the STM32's 5 V/5 A external port.

    Post-calibration under normal load: LCD reads Vin ~13.8 and Vout ~7.5. The LCD Iout and Pout fields read low in the bay: the DROK senses current on the same IN−/OUT− path, and part of the return current goes around it through the shared ground, so never use them as the rail's current. If Vout sags noticeably below 7.5 V under load, the DROK is thermally folding back (check bay ventilation).

  • Why 7.5 V: the STM32 board passes its input straight to its bus-servo ports, so with today's LV servos (6–8.4 V) the DROK setpoint is the arm's servo voltage; the board itself (Hiwonder: input up to 12 V) is held at 7.5 V on every LV build, Hiwonder's stock adapter voltage (7.5 V 6 A). Never set the DROK above 8.4 V while LV servos are fitted. At the HV servo swap (a future upgrade, not the shipping configuration; the product releases with the LV servos) the same DROK is re-set to 11.1 V (≤ ~11.6 V: the board's 12 V maximum binds) and still feeds the board, which feeds the servos, with a 10 A 6 × 30 glass fuse between the DROK and the board. The shipping configuration carries one fuse, F1 (battery → PSU); after the swap, two only: the servo fuse and F1. Full design: Electronics_Bay_Design.md §1 Servo and board power.

  • Do NOT feed non-STM32 loads from the DROK — the split-rails topology (locked 2026-06-28) sized the DROK for STM32-only. Any new bay load should go to a distribution-block spare position (A5/A6 with its B return) directly at 13.8 V, not to the DROK.

Hiwonder bus servo replacement — general procedure (with servo-ID reprogramming)

Applies to any arm-bus servo swap on an Ultra-class arm (Ultra, raspberrypidev gen2, any future LumiAI arm): elbow add-back, gripper HTS-25L swap, or any post-failure replacement. This is the authoritative procedure for future reference; specific installs (BigClaw, etc.) reference it below.

Why the ID matters (context)

Hiwonder bus servos (LX-225, HTS-16L, HTS-25L, etc.) share a single half-duplex UART daisy-chain driven from the STM32's bus servo port. Every servo on the chain must have a unique 1-byte ID (typically 1–253; 0xFE = 254 is broadcast). A new servo out of the box defaults to ID 1. Wiring an ID-1 new servo onto a chain that already has an ID-1 gripper produces bus collisions — both servos try to answer the same query, the STM32 sees framing errors, and NEITHER servo works reliably. Always reprogram a new servo's ID before daisy-chaining it into a working arm.

Ultra ID convention (from project MEMORY.md "Arm Home Positions"): 1=gripper 2=wrist_rot 3=wrist 4=elbow 5=shoulder 6=base (reversed from joint numbering). Yahboom uses 6=gripper. Confirm per robot before assigning.

ArmPi Ultra per-joint servo model (verified 2026-05-21):

Joint Servo ID Model Torque (approx) Notes

Shoulder 5 Larger-class (verify per unit — model not recorded) — Bears the arm-plus-payload moment; not LX-15D
Elbow 4 Hiwonder LX-15D ~15 kg·cm Verified 2026-05-21
Wrist 3 Hiwonder LX-15D ~15 kg·cm Verified 2026-05-21

When ordering replacements, match the joint's original class — an under-torqued replacement will stall / brownout under load; an over-torqued one adds weight and can over-drive the mechanical linkage. IDs 5 + 6 (base, shoulder) bear the arm-plus-payload moment and take the largest servos; IDs 3 + 4 (wrist, elbow) are LX-15D on the ArmPi Ultra family.

Preferred transport: isolate the servo on a debugger dongle

The safest way to reprogram an ID is with a dedicated Hiwonder USB Servo Debugger dongle — a small USB-serial adapter with a 3-pin JST servo port. The target servo is the ONLY device on that bus, so a broadcast setBusServoID(0xFE, target_id) cannot accidentally affect any other arm servo. If the dongle is available, always use it for ID work.

Bench pigtail method (fleet standard, no dongle required): keep a dedicated single-servo direct pigtail — same 3-pin JST as the arm bus. At the STM32's bus servo port, unplug the arm's daisy-chain connector, then plug the direct pigtail (with only the target servo on it) into that same port. Program + verify the ID via RPi5 → /dev/ttyUSB0 → STM32 → pigtail → single servo. Then unplug the pigtail and reconnect the arm-bus daisy-chain. One connector swap in, one out — no daisy-chain disassembly, no risk of a broadcast setBusServoID(0xFE, X) accidentally reprogramming any of the arm's other servos. This is the fleet-standard approach in the absence of a dedicated debugger dongle.

Procedure — reprogramming a servo's ID (bench, before install)

Preconditions: target servo held safely (not on the arm), bus power available (dongle self-powers the servo from USB; STM32 pass-through requires the STM32 booted per Electronics_BringUp_Plan.md Phase 0).

  1. Connect the target servo alone to the dongle (or to the STM32 bus servo port with ALL other servos physically unplugged).
  2. Read its current ID to know what you're starting from: 
    from bus_servo_control import BusServoControl  # path varies; use the copy your project already imports
bus = BusServoControl(port='/dev/ttyUSB0')     # dongle path may differ, e.g. /dev/ttyUSB1
current_id = bus.getBusServoID()               # broadcast — safe with one servo
print(f'current servo ID = {current_id}')      # typical fresh-servo default = 1
  3. Write the new ID (e.g. 4 for elbow on Ultra convention): 
    bus.setBusServoID(0xFE, 4)                     # broadcast old-ID → new-ID 4
  4. Verify by reading back: 
    assert bus.getBusServoID() == 4
  5. Torque OFF so the servo doesn't hold pose when you handle it: 
    bus.stopBusServo(4)
  6. Physically label the servo with its new ID (silver Sharpie on the case, or a tape flag) — future-you will thank you when the arm has 6 identical-looking bus servos.

Alternative — Servo Upper Computer GUI: DISPLAY=:0 python3 /home/pi/software/Servo_upper_computer/main.py (or over SSH with -Y X-forward). The GUI has an "ID" panel: read current ID, type new ID, click set, click read to verify. Same underlying protocol; same isolation rule (broadcast ID-write affects every servo on the bus).

Procedure — horn indexing before coupling to mechanical linkage

Applies to ANY new servo whose output shaft couples to a new mechanical assembly — base servo ↔︎ base-rotation plate, shoulder ↔︎ shoulder link, elbow ↔︎ elbow link, gripper servo ↔︎ jaw gear plate, any custom joint. If the horn is engaged to the mechanical linkage at any servo position other than mechanical center (pulse 500), the whole downstream reference frame shifts by that offset: every hard-coded HOME value, every /joint_states readback, every FK/IK computation, every gamepad center-position assumption misaligns by the same amount.

The rule: the servo's output horn MUST be at pulse 500 (mechanical center) when it is physically coupled to whatever it drives. Do this on the bench, before final mechanical assembly onto the arm.

Procedure (bench, after ID reprogramming from previous subsection):

  1. Power the servo from the bench pigtail (STM32 pass-through, or dongle if available).
  2. Command the servo to pulse 500 with a slow move so you can see it settle: bus.setBusServoPulse(<id>, 500, 1000) (1000 ms travel). Let it hold for ~2 s.
  3. Verify it arrived (a load-fought move can stall mid-travel): bus.getBusServoPulse(<id>) — should return ~500.
  4. Torque off so the shaft doesn't spring back or resist your hands during coupling: bus.stopBusServo(<id>).
  5. Physically couple the horn / gear plate / output linkage to the mechanical assembly. The shaft is at its mechanical zero; whatever pose the mechanism ends up in when coupled at this shaft position becomes the ROS "pulse 500 = …" reference. Match that pose to what the codebase / URDF assumes for this joint (arm-forward-center for base, jaws-closed for gripper, per-joint HOME for the arm links).
  6. Power down, install on arm per the next subsection.

Why 500? Hiwonder LX/HTS bus servos range 0–1000 pulse over their ~240° mechanical travel; 500 = physical mid-position. Ultra's conventions anchor here — e.g. base HOME = 500 pulse = arm-forward-center; gripper closed = 500 pulse. If a specific joint's HOME value in MEMORY.md is NOT 500 (e.g. Ultra elbow HOME = 825, shoulder = 600), that's an intentional post-coupling pose choice baked in the codebase, not a coupling error — the horn was still indexed at pulse 500 during coupling; the codebase then commands a non-500 pulse to reach the preferred HOME.

Recovery if a servo was coupled off-index:

  • Cheap bench check first — with the servo powered but torque off, aim the mechanical linkage to its intended reference pose by hand, then read bus.getBusServoPulse(<id>). Delta from 500 tells you how far off the coupling was and which recovery to use.
  • Small offset (roughly ± 22° ≈ ± 125 pulse counts, per typical Hiwonder deviation range): trim in EEPROM with bus.setBusServoDeviation(<id>, <offset>) + bus.saveBusServoDeviation(<id>). Persists across power cycles.
  • Larger offset: torque off, disassemble the shaft ↔︎ linkage coupling, re-command pulse 500 + verify, re-couple at the correct index, re-tighten.

Extra weight for reference-frame joints (base especially): a mis-indexed base means the whole arm's world orientation is offset — the arm will swing when first commanded to HOME. Always verify the base index before the first Phase 1 bring-up, and keep the arm supported by hand on the first base position read. Same principle at a smaller scale for shoulder + elbow (arm reach envelope shifts by the offset).

Procedure — replacing a servo on a live arm

Preconditions: the new servo has already been reprogrammed to its target ID (previous procedure) and labeled. Arm is powered down at the DC rocker OR the STM32's controller switch. Container idle, no bringup stack running (per [[feedback_never_autostart_robot_stack]]).

  1. Torque OFF the joint(s) around the servo you're replacing. If the arm is powered, run bus.stopBusServo(<affected_ids>) first. Then power the arm DOWN at the DC rocker (or STM32 controller switch) — no bus power during physical work.
  2. Support the arm by hand or in a bench cradle so it can't fall / swing when the servo is disconnected.
  3. Disconnect the servo's daisy-chain leads at the connectors on either side of it (bus servos daisy-chain input↔︎servo↔︎output; the servo has 2 identical 3-pin JST connectors so power/data pass through to the next servo).
  4. Unbolt the failed/removed servo from its arm bracket (typically 4× M2/M3 into the servo case).
  5. Bolt the new servo into the same bracket. Note the horn orientation — Hiwonder bus servos have a defined 0°/500-pulse position; the horn must be indexed to that position before you cross-couple it to the mechanical linkage, or the joint's zero will be off. Follow the original arm's assembly reference for the horn orientation at each joint.
  6. Reconnect the daisy-chain leads — either lead into either of the servo's 2 connectors (they're symmetric).
  7. Power up — DC rocker ON, STM32 controller switch ON (per Electronics_BringUp_Plan.md Phase 0 sequencing).
  8. Bench-verify per Electronics_BringUp_Plan.md Phase 1 (CONDITIONAL) — read-only motor_controller_node launch, confirm all 6 arm servos publish in /joint_states, then supported-by-hand single 5° jog on the replaced joint. Compare jog latency + holding torque to a known-good joint. No HOME / no multi-joint pose commands until Phase 8 sign-off per docs/ULTRA_GAMEPAD_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md — project_2026_04_28_arm_slam_incident rule applies.

Safety callouts

  • Never setBusServoID(0xFE, X) with multiple servos on the bus. Broadcast ID-writes hit every connected servo. Isolate first.
  • Torque off before physical work. A powered bus servo holds its commanded position with ~25 kg·cm — physically forcing it while powered can damage the gearset. stopBusServo(id) cuts torque cleanly; power-down at the DC rocker does it more forcefully.
  • Zero the horn on install. If the servo is mounted with the horn off its 0°/500-pulse index, /joint_states readback will be off by the same offset and every downstream computation (IK, kinematics, forward planning) will be off. Save any needed trim with setBusServoDeviation + saveBusServoDeviation.
  • New servo out-of-box default = ID 1. ID-1 is the Ultra gripper. A new servo daisied without reprogramming = ID collision on the gripper.
  • No HOME until Phase 8. project_2026_04_28_arm_slam_incident — a single-shot HOME teleport from unknown pose destroyed the Ultra elbow servo. The whole per-phase discipline in Electronics_BringUp_Plan.md + docs/ULTRA_GAMEPAD_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md exists because of this incident.

Detailed procedure — swap raspberrypidev gripper+servo → BigClaw + HTS-25L

This is a specific instance of the general Hiwonder bus servo replacement procedure above — read that first for the servo-ID isolation + programming rules, then apply the BigClaw-specific mechanical detail below.

A. Bench-assemble the BigClaw + HTS-25L (before touching the arm):

  1. Unbox the BigClaw and the HTS-25L. Confirm the BigClaw screw pack is present (M3 cap-heads for gear-plate ↔︎ disc-horn engagement) and the HTS-25L ships with its included circular disc horn + horn-mount screw.
  2. Program the HTS-25L to ID 1 using the general procedure above. A fresh HTS-25L defaults to ID 1 (which IS the Ultra gripper ID), so if the arm's existing gripper has already been physically removed from the bus, no reprogramming is needed — but verify with getBusServoID() before daisy-chaining. If the ID happens to be anything other than 1, reprogram to 1 now. Label the servo case with a "1" for future reference.
  3. Index the HTS-25L horn at pulse 500 before engaging the disc horn with the BigClaw gear plate — per the general horn-indexing procedure above (setBusServoPulse(1, 500, 1000) → verify → torque off → couple). Gripper-specific note: for the BigClaw the jaws-closed position corresponds to pulse 500 — coupling the gear plate to the disc horn at that shaft index is what makes the codebase's 500 = closed / 200 = open gripper convention work.
  4. Torque OFF so the servo doesn't fight during assembly: stopBusServo(1). Then power the servo down.
  5. Bolt the HTS-25L into the BigClaw body — the HTS-25L case has 4× M2/M3 mount points (per the standard Hiwonder bus-servo case). The BigClaw body has the mirror-image mounting holes on its rear plate. Align, insert screws from the BigClaw's outer face into the servo case, snug. Do not overtighten into the servo case (aluminum threads on the servo case strip under excessive torque).
  6. Mount the disc horn onto the servo output shaft — dry-fit first without the gear plate engaged, so you can confirm the horn indexes at 500 pulse (mid-position). Secure with the HTS-25L's included horn-mount screw (M3 typically). The horn should have zero slop on the output spline.
  7. Engage the BigClaw gear plate onto the disc horn — the BigClaw's driven gear has M3 through-holes that align with the disc horn's threaded holes at the 500-pulse index. Fasten with M3 cap-heads from the BigClaw screw pack. This is the critical rotational coupling — servo pulse → gear plate rotation → gripper jaws.
  8. Hand-cycle the assembled gripper (servo unpowered) to confirm the jaws open and close smoothly through the full mechanical range. Any binding at this stage means the gear engagement is off or the horn wasn't at 500-pulse when coupled — disassemble step 7 and redo.

B. Swap on the raspberrypidev arm:

  1. Torque OFF the current gripper servo: stopBusServo(<current_gripper_id>) (usually 1). Then power the arm DOWN at the DC rocker (or STM32 controller switch) — no bus power during physical work.
  2. Support the arm by hand or in a bench cradle so it can't fall / swing while the gripper is off.
  3. Disconnect the old gripper's daisy-chain lead at the connector between it and its upstream neighbor (typically the wrist-rot servo, ID 2, per Ultra convention where the gripper is the terminal end of the chain).
  4. Unbolt the old gripper assembly from the arm's end-effector bracket.
  5. Bolt the pre-assembled BigClaw + HTS-25L (from A above) into the same end-effector bracket. Match the existing arm's U-bracket geometry — cross-fleet standardization per MEMORY.md shares the U-bracket across Ultra + raspberrypidev + Yahboom.
  6. Reconnect the daisy-chain lead into the HTS-25L's bus servo connector (either of its 2 symmetric 3-pin JST ports — the other one is left unpopulated since the gripper is the chain terminal).
  7. Power up — DC rocker ON, STM32 controller switch ON (per Electronics_BringUp_Plan.md Phase 0 sequencing).

C. Verify per bring-up plan:

  1. Run Electronics_BringUp_Plan.md Phase 1 (CONDITIONAL) — REQUIRED here because raspberrypidev-gen2 is a fresh build (new base servo + user-machined base plate + add-back elbow + new gripper all landing together). Supported-by-hand, per-joint 5° jog on every joint. Compare the new elbow's and gripper's jog latency + holding torque to a known-good joint (e.g. wrist-rot).
  2. Run Electronics_BringUp_Plan.md Phase 2 — gripper open (200 pulse) / close (500 pulse) cycle × 5, no bus contention; close-on-wand-diameter-cylinder object test (jaw holds without slip or thermal cutout).
  3. NO HOME / no multi-joint pose commands until Phase 8 sign-off per ULTRA_GAMEPAD_INTEGRATION_PLAN.md — the project_2026_04_28_arm_slam_incident rule applies unconditionally.

Common pitfalls:

  • Horn not indexed at 500 pulse when engaging the gear plate → every 500=closed / 200=open assumption in the codebase misaligns. Symptom: the gripper won't fully close (or over-closes and stalls) at the commanded pulse. Fix: disassemble the gear-plate ↔︎ disc-horn coupling and redo step 7 with the horn re-indexed at 500 pulse.
  • HTS-25L still at default ID 1 while the old gripper is still on the bus → bus collision (both servos at ID 1). Symptom: motor_controller_node reports intermittent packet loss on the gripper node; jaw motion is erratic. Fix: physically remove the old gripper from the bus first, THEN daisy-chain the new BigClaw.
  • Old gripper had a setBusServoDeviation trim saved → the new HTS-25L's zero isn't trimmed to match. Symptom: the physical jaw resting position is slightly off from the previous gripper. Fix: use setBusServoDeviation(1, offset) + saveBusServoDeviation(1) to trim the new servo to match.
  • Missing safety supported-by-hand during Phase 1 verification → the arm can swing under gravity when torque is applied to a mis-indexed servo. Always physically support the arm during first bench jogs of a replaced joint.

Hiwonder STM32 controller — firmware flashing (replacement boards ship BLANK)

Applies to any Hiwonder ArmPi Ultra STM32 controller obtained as a bare replacement (core board + carrier), on any LumiAI arm host (raspberrypidev/Halo gen2, Ultra, future builds). This is the authoritative recovery procedure — a blank replacement board is the expected state, not a fault.

Why it matters (the month-and-a-half failure mode)

Replacement controller boards ship with an empty STM32 flash — no application firmware. A blank board is indistinguishable from a "dead" one by every normal test:

  • Powers up (core + carrier red LEDs lit), CH340 enumerates as /dev/ttyUSB0 (bcdDevice c233).
  • Totally silent to the app protocol at every baud — no buzzer, no get_battery()/get_imu() data, WonderPi app connects but the arm never moves.
  • Identical behaviour whether tested under our Ubuntu, Hiwonder's vendor OS, or A/B against a known-good Ultra.

A factory-complete robot (e.g. Ultra) works only because its controller was flashed on the assembly line. Verified 2026-09-22: this was the entire Aug→Sep "DOA controller" saga on Halo — the boards were never broken; they were unflashed. Full incident + root-cause in memory/project_raspberrypidev_is_ultra_gen2.md.

Critical hardware fact — the reset strap (why stm32flash CANNOT flash this board)

On this board: DTR is wired to NRST, but BOOT0 is not wired to RTS. Two consequences that break every "normal" flashing method:

  1. No software bootloader entry. BOOT0 can't be driven from the host, so DTR/RTS auto-reset sequences (ATK-XISP style, stm32flash -i ...) all time out. The chip can only be put into ROM bootloader with the physical BOOT + RST buttons.
  2. stm32flash resets the chip out of the bootloader the instant it opens the port. Opening the tty asserts DTR → NRST low → the manually-entered bootloader is wiped before the first sync byte. stm32flash therefore can never flash this board (always "Failed to init device, timeout"), even with a perfect manual BOOT+RST first.

Hiwonder's generic guide 3. Project Compilation and Download.pdf (ATK-XISP over Type-C UART, "Reset@DTR Low / ISP@RTS High", 115200 baud) is what revealed flashing is a UART operation — no SWD/ST-Link needed — but its DTR/RTS auto-reset applies to their M4 (F407) controller, not this board.

The working procedure (repeatable — for spare sets + to recover any "DOA" board)

Tooling — canonical, version-controlled: docs/tools/hiwonder_stm32_flash.py (the pyserial flasher) + docs/tools/hiwonder_bl_listen.py (a no-reset 0x7F bootloader prober for a quick alive/blank check). Firmware ArmPiUltra.hex (Hiwonder-supplied, 114666 B hex / 40752 B binary) is not committed — keep it at ~/ArmPiUltra.hex on the target host + ~/Downloads/ArmPiUltra.hex on the dev machine. Run the flasher as python3 hiwonder_stm32_flash.py [firmware.hex] [/dev/ttyUSB0] (defaults find ./ArmPiUltra.hex or ~/ArmPiUltra.hex).

  1. Confirm the board is enumerating — ls /dev/ttyUSB0, CH340 1a86:7523 bcdDevice c233. Ensure ModemManager is masked (it grabs new CH340s).
  2. Launch the flasher FIRST, then press the buttons. Run python3 ~/hiwonder_stm32_flash.py. It opens the port with dtr=False, rts=False (no reset on open) and prints a prompt, then polls 0x7F for ~90 s. Order matters — opening first, pressing second, avoids the open-transient DTR pulse fighting the button.
  3. Enter ROM bootloader with the physical buttons: HOLD BOOT → tap RST (while holding BOOT) → keep BOOT held ~2 s → release BOOT.
  4. The flasher auto-runs the STM32 ROM bootloader protocol: sync 0x7F @ 115200 8E1 → Get → Get-ID → global erase (0x43) → write-memory (0x31, 256 B blocks) → Go (0x21). Expect: bootloader ver 0x22, CHIP_PID=0x0410 = STM32F103 (medium-density; matches the F103RBT6 datasheet — not the F407 in the generic PDF). Firmware writes in ~2 s.
  5. Verify with the SDK (~/software/Servo_upper_computer/ros_robot_controller_sdk.py, device /dev/ttyUSB0, 1 M 8N1 for the app — note: app baud ≠ bootloader baud): board.enable_reception() → board.set_buzzer(...) should beep, and board.get_battery() should return ~9 V (the DROK rail). Beep + battery = firmware alive. (Reference-good values from Ultra: ~9.1 V.)

Common pitfalls

  • Reaching for stm32flash / an ST-Link. Neither is needed and stm32flash can't work here (resets on open — see above). The pyserial no-reset flasher is the only method that works over the Type-C UART. SWD would work but is unnecessary.
  • Pressing BOOT+RST before the flasher opens the port → the open-transient reset can knock the chip back out of bootloader. Always launch the flasher first, watch for its prompt, then press.
  • Assuming a silent board is dead / RMA-ing it. Blank-from-factory is the default for bare replacement boards. Flash it before concluding anything about the hardware.
  • Wrong baud on verify. The ROM bootloader is 115200 8E1; the application runs at 1 M 8N1. Verifying at 115200 after flashing will read nothing — use the SDK default (1 M).
  • Firmware .hex missing. If ~/ArmPiUltra.hex was wiped (e.g. OS reinstall), restore from the dev machine ~/Downloads/ArmPiUltra.hex before flashing.
