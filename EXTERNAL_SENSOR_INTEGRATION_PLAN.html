Why the change from 2× INA226 → 1× INA226

The original 2-sensor plan added a second INA226 on a secondary rail to separate "battery fault" from "servo stall." Per the canonical power doc (Power/Compact_UPS_Power_System_HiWonder_RPi5.md) the real topology is: PSC-100-12 / PSC-150-12 CH1 → INA226 (in series) → Master Switch → distribution, splitting into a DROK 7.5 V buck → STM32 → Pi 5 + arm bus-servos branch (the DROK replaced the LY-KREE — there is no LY-KREE; the DROK is in the Pi/servo path) and a 12 V MOSFET (IRFZ44N) LED-driver → gripper LEDs branch. A shunt on any single branch could still only report "that branch's current is high" — it could not tell a stalled servo from one legitimately working hard. The real discriminator is the arm controller's commanded-vs-actual joint error, already published on /joint_states. The single INA226 already sits in series on CH1 (the as-installed design), so it sees the aggregate of both branches — a servo stall (DROK 7.5 V → STM32 branch) still shows as a CH1 current transient — and fusing that with the joint-error signal recovers full discrimination in software, freeing a sensor slot, an I²C address, and BOM. See §7.3.

Why presence sensing, and why PIR

The arm is ceiling-/wall-mounted and spends most of its time idle. We want low-cost passive sensing that fires whenever a person moves anywhere in the room volume below the assembly, without relying on the always-on camera or voice pipeline (a wake source that costs ~50 µA, not a CPU core). HC-SR501 is the right tool:

  • Passive — no emission, no licensing concerns.
  • **~2each * *in2 − packs( 8 for the 4-sensor set; redundancy is cheap).
  • 120° × 7 m detection cone per sensor.
  • 3.3 V digital OUT → drops directly onto an RPi5 GPIO header pin — no level shifter, no ADC (unlike the rejected MQ-x; Pi5 has no ADC — and even bay-thermal uses a digital I²C sensor, the TMP102, so there's no ADC anywhere in the design).
  • ~50 µA quiescent — does not move the LiFePO4 battery needle.
  • mmWave drop-in path — the same fence pocket + 3-wire pigtail accepts a 24 GHz mmWave module (LD2410 / RCWL-0516, ~$15–20) if richer range+angle presence is ever needed (see Appendix E.7).

Four sensors on the four slanted fence facets give ~347° coverage at the datasheet's 110° cone (~357° at the 120° bound). The one gap is a 13° wedge straight out from the FRONT wall (~0.8 m wide at 3 m). The coverage geometry, facet normals, and per-pair overlap math are detailed in Appendix E.2.

AC-vs-battery is detectable from the CH1 step (~14.2 V AC → 12 V battery). INA226 board reference (GODIYMODULES clone — verified from vendor schematic + datasheet 2026-05-23)

What is the 2 mΩ shunt? It's the low-value precision resistor (silkscreen R002 = 0.002 Ω = 2 mΩ) built into the board, sitting physically between the ISENSE+ and ISENSE− screw terminals. It's the only thing the INA226 needs to measure current: all load current has to flow through it, and the INA226 chip measures the tiny voltage drop across it (Ohm's law: V = I × R, so I = V / 0.002). At our peak PSU CH1 load of ~11.7 A the drop is 11.7 × 0.002 = 23.4 mV — well within the INA226's ±81.92 mV differential input range. Power dissipated at peak: I² × R = 11.7² × 0.002 = 0.27 W (comfortable for the ceramic shunt's ~1 W rating). Value is chosen small so it doesn't noticeably drop bus voltage downstream, but large enough to give the INA226 a useful signal to digitise.

Direction of current flow: PSU CH1 +V → ISENSE+ → [2 mΩ shunt] → ISENSE− → downstream load. ISENSE+ is UPSTREAM of the shunt (higher voltage), ISENSE− is DOWNSTREAM (lower voltage by 2 mΩ × I). Getting these swapped produces a negative current reading — the INA226 will still work but the sign is flipped, so match the direction above.

Note on "orange pair" / "green pair": the two orange terminals are both source-side signals (V+(VBUS) + ISENSE+ — both at the SOURCE-side node of the shunt), and the two green terminals are both non-source signals (ISENSE− at load-side + GND at system return). The colour grouping is meaningful — orange = same node as PSU CH1 +V, green = the two "other" nodes. Ignore any vendor silkscreen labels calling the orange pair "measured voltage" or the green pair "measured current" — those labels are misleading. Use the individual silk labels (V+/ISENSE+/ISENSE−/GND) as the truth.

Pad-modification recipe — INA226 + TMP102 (committed 2026-05-23)

The exact pads you have to touch on each board to (a) set the I²C address and (b) reconcile the bus pull-ups. The closeup photo below is the visual source of truth — silk text was confirmed against this image, not just the schematic.

Silk reading — confirmed 2026-05-23. Two facts the operator verified visually on this exact board:

  1. Row order: TOP = A0, BOTTOM = A1. (Earlier draft of this doc had them inverted — fixed in this pass. Different INA226 clones may have the rows flipped — always verify the row silk-letter before soldering, or use the continuity check below.)
  2. Pad letters per row: G U L A (left → right). Vendor uses "U" for VCC on this silk — probably to avoid clashing with the "V+" screw-terminal label. Silk-letter → function: G = GND, U = VCC, L = SCL, A = SDA.

The Step-1 continuity check below stays in the recipe — it's the verification step, and the only safe path on a different INA226 clone where the silk may differ.

⚠️ SAFETY: ONE bridge per row, ever. Each row's 4 pads (G/U/L/A) all tie the same address pin to different rails. If you bridge two pads in the same row simultaneously — e.g. A0:G and A0:U — you tie GND directly to VCC through the row's internal traces → dead short on the 3.3 V I²C rail. Risk: pop the Pi's 3V3 LDO or the INA226's VCC pin. Before adding a new bridge to any row, first desolder any existing bridge in that row. End state: exactly one bridge per row, two bridges total on the board (one in A0 row, one in A1 row).

Factory state → target state (0x44) — the actual work you're doing:

The GODIYMODULES INA226 ships with both G pads bridged (A0:G in the top row + A1:G in the bottom row → factory 0x40). Our target 0x44 is A1=VS, A0=GND, so:

Row Factory bridge Target bridge Action
A0 (top row) G (GND) G (GND) KEEP as-is — no work. Target address happens to want A0=GND, which is factory.
A1 (bottom row) G (GND) U (VCC) DESOLDER A1:G first, then SOLDER-BRIDGE A1:U (2nd pad from the left in the A1 row per this vendor). Never both bridged at once (would short VCC→GND).

Net: one bridge removed (A1:G), one new bridge added (A1:U), one factory bridge preserved (A0:G). Two bridges total on the board when done.

Step 1 — identify which pad is which (continuity check, the only trustworthy way):

Power the board OFF and disconnected. Set the multimeter to continuity (beep) mode. The four reference rails are exposed on the bottom 4-pin header (silk: GND / SCL / SDA / VCC, left→right) — those are your known nets.

For each of the 8 address-bridge pads (4 in the A1 row, 4 in the A0 row), touch one probe to the pad and the other to each of the 4 header pins in turn. The pad will beep against exactly one of {GND, SCL, SDA, VCC} — that tells you what function that pad selects when bridged. Mark each pad with a pencil dot (or just note position: leftmost / 2nd / 3rd / rightmost) labeled with G/V/L/A. (The schematic confirms each row is laid out left→right in some consistent order across G/V/L/A — likely "G V L A" or its mirror, but you don't need to guess once you've verified.)

Step 3 — bench check: power up, run docker exec ArmPiUltra i2cdetect -y 1 → must show 0x44 (not 0x40, not 0x41, not absent). If 0x41 → you mirrored the rows (A0 ended at VS, A1 at GND); redo Step 1's continuity check and rework. If 0x4X for any other X → wrong pad somewhere — re-run Step 1 and rework. If i2cdetect shows nothing and the 3.3 V rail dropped → you bridged two pads in the same row and shorted VCC→GND (the safety callout above); power down, lift one of the bridges, retry.

Why bonded parallel cable is the fleet standard (do NOT switch to fully-jacketed multi-conductor without re-visiting these reasons — locked at bench 2026-08-03):

  1. Bends easier. Fully-jacketed multi-conductor cable (single silicone/PVC tube around all N conductors) is significantly stiffer than N bonded-parallel conductors of the same gauge count — the outer jacket adds bulk without adding flexibility. In tight bay layouts (G469 terminals sit ~30 mm apart) the stiffness of jacketed cable fights the routing.
  2. Splice-able mid-run. If a cable needs a mid-run tap or repair (e.g. adding a 5th PIR later, or field-repairing a broken conductor), bonded parallel lets you separate one conductor from the bundle at that point without cutting through an outer jacket. Fully-jacketed cable forces you to slit the jacket back over a long distance to reach a single conductor, and then you can't re-jacket cleanly.
  3. Peel-to-terminate at the ends. Bonded parallel = grab two conductors, pull sideways, they separate. Fully-jacketed = strip 10 mm of outer jacket back, then cut individual conductor insulation. One-motion vs three-motion.
  4. Pre-assigned colors. LED-strip bonded cable uses standard RGB(W)-family colors (black / red / green / blue / white) — assemblers can read pin identity from color instead of tracing back to a legend.

Individual single-conductor wires + bundle-yourself was rejected earlier for the opposite reason — creates a rat's-nest of loose wires with no cohesive routing. Bonded parallel is the "one physical cable" answer without the rigidity penalty of jacketed.

Rationale for the single-6-cond-SKU signal roll:

  • Wire gauge (22 AWG per the 2026-09-30 palette; v1 as-built 24 AWG) comfortably handles the highest signal-side current (PIR 5 V bus at ~260 mA on the full 6-cond trunk — 24 AWG chassis rating ~3.5 A, so 7 % loaded). All other signal cables run at µA to tens of mA.
  • The 6-conductor cable is EASILY reduced to 5, 4, or 3 by peeling off and snipping the extra conductors flush at each end — no tools needed beyond wire cutters. That means ONE roll on the bench covers all six fleet signal cable variants.
  • Tinned copper strands vs bare copper — resist oxidation over shelf life + easier soldering (strand tips are pre-tinned, so crimps grip cleanly without a separate tin-and-strip step).
  • PVC vs silicone jacket — PVC is fine for the static cables (INA226 pigtail, TMP102-bay pigtail, ZE15-CO tail) because they don't move. Marginal concern for the wand tether signal leg — the wand tether is a MOVING cable (arm articulates), and PVC has lower flex-life than silicone. Current spec keeps the wand signal leg on the same PVC roll for SKU consolidation; if long-term bench service ever shows PVC fatigue cracking on the wand signal leg after N thousand arm cycles, upgrade THAT ONE cable to silicone-jacketed 24 AWG 5-conductor (Fermerry / BNTECHGO stock). Static cables stay on PVC. (PIR harness — no longer on the fleet PVC roll; uses repurposed RC servo cable per the PIR row in the cable-inventory table above.)
  • 26 AWG IS a fleet gauge — the signal gauge (I²C, 3V3, pull-ups), and the only gauge with a G469-proven ferrule pair besides 24 (E0208). The 24 AWG roll is bench stock.
  • 22 AWG is the fleet mid gauge (tether V+/GND, fan branches, taps, signal extensions). ⚠ 22 AWG at a G469 cage goes in the AIRIC violet ferrule (proven to 0.516 mm² there — four 26 AWG, bench + as-built 2026-09-23); the XHF E0508 stays banned at the G469.

How to keep the wand's ~8 A switching power off the FFC/signals (priority order — do 1–2 always; escalate as soak shows):

  1. Segregate at the aperture and along the run. Do not bundle the wand power pair with the FFC or the sensor tails. Pass them through different zones of the large servo hole (opposite sides / a separate grommet), keep max practical air-gap along the arm, and where they must cross, cross at ~90° — never long parallel adjacent runs.
  2. Tightly twist the wand +10.6 V/GND pair. The FFC is flat ribbon and can't be twisted, so twist the aggressor: a tight twisted pair cancels the differential-mode magnetic field of the ~8 A switching ripple — highest-value, zero-cost fix.
  3. Decouple at the wand head (source fix — owned by the LumiAI wand-PCB design, noted here for completeness): bulk low-ESR + ceramic HF caps at the LED-driver-array input (all three driver variants are buck-switching topology, same dI/dt class) keep the high-dI/dt switching loops local on the wand rail, so the tether carries smoother current and radiates less. The cleaner this is, the less 4–5 are needed.
  4. Common-mode choke / ferrite on the wand power pair at the aperture (clip-on toroid) — kills HF common-mode noise, no effect on the DC.
  5. Shield (if 1–4 insufficient): shielded twisted pair for the wand power, or a grounded foil/braid barrier between the power pair and the signal bundle at the aperture — ground the shield at one end only (power-bay/PSU ground) to avoid a ground loop.
  6. Dedicated wand GND return: the ~8 A return goes back on its own conductor to the wand-DROK star point — never shared mid-run with signal grounds (prevents ground-bounce coupling). Consistent with the power-doc star-ground.
  7. Signal-side hardening (only if soak shows residual coupling): I²C/UART tails are already short (power-bay-local); levers are a series ferrite/RC on I²C or a stiffer pull-up pair — but honor the "don't double-up pull-ups" rule (§2).
  8. Validate as part of the shared-FFC soak-test watch item: run the wand at full ~33 W / ~2.4 A (read "~84 W" until 2026-09-01 — (Integration §5.3, measured 2026-09-01)) (worst-case switching) while logging I²C errors / UART CRC / PIR false-triggers; escalate 4→5 if degraded.

Operator-confirmed 2026-05-17: the base servo bay sits at ambient room temperature (it is not an engineered Noctua bay-airflow zone — power or electronics — and it does not self-heat meaningfully). This clears the two thermal risks:

  • SPS30: operating −10…+60 °C, best-performance 10–40 °C (datasheet §1.1) — base-servo-bay ambient sits squarely in the best-performance band.
  • Electrochemical CO cell (datasheet −10…+55 °C): comfortably in range at room temp; its 3–5 yr life (in air) is calendar-driven, not thermally accelerated here. Still a documented consumable (BOM + replacement interval).
  • Bonus: ambient room air at the base is better representative sampling for actual room smoke/CO than the sealed electronics bay would be.

Presence array (4× HC-SR501) — power bay (functional) / base-plate fence slants (physical)

Per the two-bay topology above, the PIR array belongs to the power bay functionally (battery + PSC-100-12 / PSC-150-12 PSU/UPS + DROK + PIR + INA226) — its cabling and the GPIO harness are routed in that bay. Physically the 4 sensors mount on the 4 slanted corner facets of the base-plate fence, integrated into the parametric fence (printed-in-place retention, no aftermarket brackets) — full cutout/baffle/macro spec is in Appendix F and lumi_baseplate_fence.py.

The PIRs go on the slants, deliberately NOT the louvered front/back walls. PIRs detect IR temperature differentials, and a fan-driven plume of warm air sweeping past louvers (or out the back-wall fan cutouts) reads as a moving thermal target → false wake. The 4 slanted facets carry no louvers — they are smooth wall between the louvered front face and the fan-equipped back wall, the quietest thermal real-estate on the fence.

Current/correct thermal-false-trigger mitigations (the ones that are accurate today — see §12 for a stale-source note):

  1. Slant placement (no louvers there) — primary mitigation; addressed purely by mounting choice.
  2. Raised thermal baffle ring around each lens — 5 mm tall on the front slants (FL, FR), 8 mm tall on the back slants (BL, BR) (back slants sit next to the back-wall fan exhaust jets; the extra height shields the Fresnel dome from the plume's lateral edge). Ring OD/ID and macro constants in Appendix F.2.
  3. Downward-tilted back-wall fan cutouts — fan cutout normals tilted 5–10° below horizontal so the exhaust jets angle down, away from the nominally-horizontal PIR FOV.
  4. Continuous-PWM bay-fan control — both back-wall Noctua NF-A4x20 PWM fans run always-spinning, duty slewed on temperature (25 kHz PWM from the Pi5), so there is no on/off air-mass transient to trigger the PIRs. This is why the old Phase-9 fan-status software interlock is NOT needed by default — it is kept only as a scoped fallback if a specific deployment shows residual PIR sensitivity to fan-duty changes.

(The fence/baffle macro detail and the slant-facet geometry are in Appendices E.3 (thermal mitigations) + F (CAD spec) and lumi_baseplate_fence.py.)

7. Alert / Detection Logic

7.1 Battery state (INA226-MAIN) — LiFePO4 4S1P, unchanged

Voltage State Action
> 13.0 V on_ac_power Normal, AC mains connected (or fully charged)
12.8 – 13.0 V ok (battery, full) Normal
12.0 – 12.8 V ok (battery, discharging) Normal
11.6 – 12.0 V low_battery Telegram warn / 5 min; Lumi Wand amber pulse; "connect AC soon"
11.2 – 11.6 V critical Stop autonomous motion; Lumi Wand red flash; manual commands only
< 11.2 V emergency Publish shutdown request; capability node refuses motion; ALERT-pin GPIO hook

Cutoff: pack BMS cuts at ~10 V; thresholds keep us well above. Hysteresis 0.2 V. AC vs battery detected via the ~1.7 V CH1 step (13.7–13.8 V on AC under load, operator load test 2026-11-28 (14.2 V at no load), 12.0 V on battery); the 13.0 V on_ac_power threshold keeps 0.7 V margin → on_ac_power boolean.

7.2 Air-quality / hazard hint (SPS30 + CO)

  • SPS30 PM2.5/PM10 spike → smoke-aerosol hint.
  • CO module ppm over a configurable advisory threshold → CO hint.
  • Both feed /lumiai/hazard_hint and /lumiai/air_quality. Per §6, these corroborate a Matter alarm event; they do not trip ALERT alone. Bench-tune advisory thresholds to avoid nuisance hints from cooking aerosol / transient CO.

7.3 Servo-stall vs battery-fault discrimination — software fusion (replaces 2nd shunt)

Single-shunt CH1 current (the aggregate of the DROK 7.5 V→STM32→Pi/servo branch and the 12 V MOSFET LED branch) + arm state, fused:

  • Servo stall: sharp current step (high di/dt) on CH1 — the arm bus-servos sit on the DROK 7.5 V → STM32 branch, so a stall there spikes the aggregate CH1 current the INA226 sees — + transient bus-voltage dip + a commanded arm motion where /joint_states is not tracking (position/velocity error growing, effort saturating). The time-correlation of the current spike with a non-tracking commanded motion is the unambiguous signature. → set stall_detected.
  • Battery fault: bus voltage below threshold, current moderate, slow/monotonic timescale, no correlation with any commanded arm motion. → battery state machine (§7.1).

This is better than the removed 2nd shunt: a dedicated secondary-rail shunt only says "that branch's current is high" and could not separate stalled from working-hard; only the joint-error cross-check can. Bench-tune the di/dt threshold and the joint-error/time window to avoid false triggers from normal high-acceleration moves.

7.4 Presence / wake-from-idle (4× PIR)

  • Any of the 4 PIR OUT lines high → /presence/pir = true; the slant bitmask publishes on /presence/pir/quadrant.
  • A /presence/pir rising edge is a wake trigger for lumiai_state_machine_node (IDLE → SEARCHING), peer to the /voice/wake_word trigger. Presence is not a hazard/ALERT input — it is orthogonal to §6 (no UL boundary involved).
  • The active-quadrant centroid is a directional prior only, not a target lock — the camera/face pipeline still does the lock. It just pre-aims the spotlight, saving ~1 s of blind search.
  • Stuck-high (>5 min) on a single slant → /diagnostics (likely thermal-noise on that slant or a failed module), not a wake.
  • 60 s post-boot warm-up blackout (HC-SR501 spec) — during it, presence is unknown; the state machine should not treat "no presence" as authoritative until warm-up clears.

9. Wiring & Install

INA226-MAIN

Module sits on top of the PSC-100-12 / PSC-150-12 PSU (power bay) — CH1 sense leads are short and local; its I²C is a short tail to the power-bay GPIO screw-terminal breakout (the Pi header is extended into the power bay via the FFC chain, §2 — the I²C does not cross bays; the FFC does). Screw-terminal names below match the silkscreen on the board — see §2 "INA226 board reference" for the full KF1 pinout table and colour coding.

Power side (4× KF1 screw terminals, top edge — cut the PSC-100-12 / PSC-150-12 CH1 +V trunk and land the two ends across the on-board 2 mΩ shunt):

  • V+(VBUS) (pin 1, orange) → SOURCE side of the cut (same node as PSC-100-12 / PSC-150-12 CH1 +V output). Easiest install: short jumper wire from V+(VBUS) to ISENSE+ so both terminals share the source-side node. 16 AWG.
  • ISENSE+ (pin 2, orange) → SOURCE side of the cut (PSC-100-12 / PSC-150-12 CH1 +V output). Current flows IN here. 16 AWG.
  • ISENSE− (pin 3, green) → LOAD side of the cut (goes to whatever consumes CH1 +V — Master Power Switch INPUT positive / distribution-block input IN-A iN). Current flows OUT here. 16 AWG.
  • GND (pin 4, green) → PSC-100-12 / PSC-150-12 CH1 GND / bay star ground. 16 AWG.

Logic side (4-pin header, bottom edge, silk GND SCL SDA VCC left→right):

  • VCC → 3.3 V (Pi pin 1) — landed at the power-bay GPIO screw-terminal breakout (header extended via the FFC chain, §2)
  • SDA → Pi I²C1 SDA (GPIO2, Pi pin 3) — landed at the power-bay GPIO screw-terminal breakout
  • SCL → Pi I²C1 SCL (GPIO3, Pi pin 5) — landed at the power-bay GPIO screw-terminal breakout
  • GND → common ground (Pi pin 6/9/14/…, any GND on the G469)
  • I²C pull-ups: HiWonder strip is removed; multiple breakouts now carry their own pull-ups (TMP102 4.7 kΩ, INA226, maybe PCA9685) — leave ONE pair on the bus, cut the rest (SparkFun Qwiic has a cuttable I²C jumper; see §2 bus note). Keep tails short, twist SDA+GND / SCL+GND, don't route alongside servo power.

SPS30 + CO module (UART)

  • SPS30 power: supply 4.5–5.5 V (5 V nominal — NOT 3.3 V, unlike the INA226). Measurement-Mode avg 60 mA; max 80 mA for the first ~200 ms of Measurement-Mode (fan spin-up); Idle-Mode <8 mA (datasheet §2.1). Duty-cycling (Idle between measurements) drops the average toward Idle. From the regulated 5 V rail (separate from the diode-dropped 12.4 V fan rail); clean tap away from PSU/fan noise. ±16 mA max per I/O pin; abs-max VDD 5.5 V.
  • SPS30 connector (datasheet §3, confirmed): JST ZHR-5 (1.5 mm pitch), on the side opposite the air inlet/outlet. Pinout: 1 = VDD (5 V ±10 %), 2 = RX/SDA, 3 = TX/SCL, 4 = SEL, 5 = GND. RX/TX are TTL-5 V and LVTTL-3.3 V compatible → wire directly to the Pi5 3.3 V UART, no level shifter (SPS30 VOH > 2.9 V, VIH > 2.31 V). For UART: leave SEL (pin 4) NC/floating; pull SEL→GND selects I²C (datasheet-confirmed polarity — my earlier hedge resolved). Caution: SEL abs-max is 4.0 V — never pull it to 5 V.
  • CO module (ZE15-CO): supply 5–12 V DC (5 V rail is fine — bottom of range), ~tens of mA continuous. UART is 3 V logic (0/3 V) — directly compatible with the Pi5 3.3 V UART, no level shifter needed. (The sibling ZE16B is 5 V UART and would have needed shifting on a 3.3 V Pi — another reason ZE15 is the right pick.) TX/RX/GND to its own port.
  • UART TX/RX/GND per module to the assigned Pi5 native UART (SPS30 → uart0 GPIO14/15; ZE15-CO → 2nd PL011 GPIO8/9), reached at the power-bay G469 screw terminals (Pi header extended via the shared 500 mm FFC, §2). Each sensor is a short tail (base servo bay → large servo hole → G469) — keep tails short, clear of fan/servo power. TTL-on-shared-FFC robustness = the shared-FFC soak-test watch item (§2). Total sensor draw trivial vs the ~84 W wand / servo loads — confirm 5 V rail headroom.

PIR array (4× HC-SR501, GPIO)

4 sensors on the base-plate fence slants. The as-built harness is repurposed RC servo leads — zero solder: per-PIR F-F extension leads into 2 modified servo Y-cables (Y1 = FL+FR, Y2 = BL+BR), each Y ending in a 2-pin power connector + a 2-pin OUT connector, landing on the G469. Fence cutout + cable-slot geometry: Appendix F.1. Bench procedure: Power_Bay_Design.md §13.3.

Why a stock Y-cable will not work as bought. A servo Y-splitter has two 3-pin female inputs and one combined 3-pin output with all three conductor positions bussed straight across. Two PIRs on it would parallel their push-pull OUT drivers — when one is HIGH and the other LOW they fight, which risks damage and destroys per-quadrant identification. The Y must be modified.

🔴 CUT THE CENTRE (RED) CONDUCTORS — not the "signal" wire (corrected 2026-09-28)

The mapping is positional, and it inverts the servo colour convention. The SR501's 3-pin header is VCC / OUT / GND with OUT on the CENTRE pin (master map, and the board silk — verify per board, clones vary). A servo lead's centre conductor is always the red one. Plug the housing straight onto the sensor and:

Servo conductor Servo role What it actually carries here
red (centre) V+ OUT — the PIR signal
white / orange / yellow (outer) signal VCC 5 V (with the orientation rule below)
black / brown (outer) GND GND

Cut by POSITION, not by servo colour role: by colour, the cut lands on a WHITE conductor — which here is a POWER rail — and leaves the two OUTs bussed on the reds, the exact driver-fight the modification exists to prevent, plus a dead 5 V feed.

Orientation rule (per arm, per extension lead): a 3-pin servo housing is unkeyed, so fix the flip once and everywhere — black/brown aligns with the GND pin on the SR501 silk. Then white = VCC on every lead in the harness. Tape-label the Y outputs, because every colour now lies about its role.

The modification, per Y (×2):

  1. On the Y's shared/output side, cut both red (centre) conductors where the arms merge — the two OUTs must never join. Heat-shrink the stub of the combined output's red.
  2. Re-terminate the two individual red conductors (one per arm) into a JST-XH 2-pin — that Y's two independent OUTs. Label which position is which sensor.
  3. The bussed outer pair (white = VCC, black = GND) re-terminates into a separate 2-pin power connector.
  4. Plug a per-PIR F-F extension lead into each arm (short for the front pair, longer for the back pair), observing the same black-on-GND orientation at the sensor.
  5. Verify before landing anything: at the Y's connectors, red-A ↔︎ red-B must read OPEN (a short = the wrong conductor was cut or the merge survived), and white ↔︎ black must read OPEN too. Then power up one Y on the bench: white–black = 5 V in, each red swings 0 → ~3.3 V on a hand-wave.

From G469 screw Notes

Y2 red — BL GPIO 20 (pin 38)

  • Combined draw: ~200 µA quiescent, ~480 mA peak if all 4 trigger — within the Pi5 5 V budget.

Thermal

  • INA226: 2 mΩ shunt → 0.8 W at 20 A peak, ~0.05 W at typical 5 A — negligible, dissipated locally at the PSU-top mount (no longer base-plate-heatsinked; a non-issue at these levels).

12. Risks & Open Questions

  1. A0/A1 solder bridges — RESOLVED 2026-05-23. Verified from vendor schematic against TI table: 0x44 = A1=VS, A0=GND → on this board's silk = bridge top-row "G" pad (A0=GND, silk "G") + bottom-row "U" pad (A1=VCC, silk "U" — vendor's letter for VCC). See §2 "Pad-modification recipe" for the row-label confirmation (top=A0, bottom=A1 on this clone — varies by vendor), the continuity check, and the one-bridge-per-row safety rule. Earlier doc combo "A1=GND, A0=VS" mapped to 0x41 — corrected in §2. Bench check still: i2cdetect -y 1 must show 0x44 before code commits.
  2. Runtime estimation — LiFePO4 mid-curve is flat (12.5–13.0 V); voltage-only SoC is rough. Coulomb counting more accurate but needs persistent state.
  3. Stall-fusion tuning — di/dt + joint-error window must reject normal high-acceleration moves while catching real stalls. Bench-tune; this is the main new validation item replacing the 2nd shunt.
  4. CO cell life — calendar consumable, 3–5 yr in air (ZE15-CO datasheet) regardless of the (benign) ambient temp; needs a replacement-interval policy + factory date stamp.

E.1 Why PIR + why on the fence

LumiAI is ceiling-mounted on dual MGN15H rails (per Ceiling_Rail_Mount_Guide.md) — the assembly hangs DOWN from the ceiling looking at the floor. The arm spends most of its time idle. We want low-cost passive sensing that fires whenever a person moves anywhere in the room volume below the assembly, without relying on the always-on camera or voice pipeline.

Why HC-SR501 PIR:

  • Passive (no emission, no licensing concerns)
  • ~$2 each in 2-packs
  • 120° × 7 m detection cone
  • 3.3 V output → drops directly into RPi5 GPIO
  • Quiescent draw ~50 µA (does not move the battery needle)
  • Future drop-in path: same wire pattern accepts a $15-20 24 GHz mmWave radar module if richer presence data is ever needed (see E.7)

Why integrated INTO the fence (not bolted onto it):

  • The LumiAI base-plate fence already integrates the power plug, 2× rocker switches, vent louvers, and the LumiAI logo cutout. PIRs follow the same design language — cut into the fence wall, retention features printed in place. Cleaner than aftermarket brackets; reads as intentional design.
  • Fence is built parametrically by cad/freecad/lumi_baseplate_fence.py — the PIR features are baked into the macro. Re-running the macro emits an updated STL with all 4 PIR cutouts pre-cut.

E.2 Coverage geometry — 4 PIRs on the 4 slanted corner facets

The base plate (and therefore the fence) has four slanted corner facets — two long ones at the front (~55.7 mm wide each, angled ~47° to the front edge) and two shorter ones at the back (~29.4 mm wide each, angled ~28° to the back edge). Each slant points its outward normal into a different quadrant of the room. Mounting one PIR per slant gives near-360° coverage with no significant blind zones at human-walking height.

Note on naming: the macro and this doc use the USER's installed-orientation convention — FRONT is the wall facing the user (with the LCD viewport and fan vent), BACK is the wall opposite (with the AC inlet). In the base-plate's native CAD frame those map to Y > 0 = user FRONT and Y < 0 = user BACK. Slant labels FL / FR / BL / BR follow user convention.

E.3 Thermal noise concern + design mitigations

Bay thermal architecture (current): the bay is cooled by two 40 mm Noctua NF-A4x20 PWM fans on the back wall running as exhaust, with passive intake louvers on the front wall and (where present) the battery-tray slope walls. There is no per-component PSU or DROK fan — cooling is bay-level only. PIR sensors detect IR temperature differentials — a fan-driven plume of warm air sweeping past louvers (or directly out the back-wall fan cutouts) shows up as a moving thermal target and triggers a false positive. This will randomly wake the arm whenever the fans modulate, which is unacceptable without mitigation.

The 4 slanted corner facets carry NO louvers — they are smooth wall sections between the louvered front face and the now-fan-equipped back wall. That's why the PIRs go on the slants and not on the front/back walls. The slants are the quietest thermal real estate on the fence.

Additional mitigations baked into the design:

  1. Slant placement (no louvers). Already addressed by the slant-mounting choice above.
  2. Vertical placement near fence centerline. The macro centers each PIR at Z = 30 mm (the fence is 90 mm and PIR_Z_CENTER is pinned at the absolute 30; the upper 30 mm band is reserved for the power-bay carrier). Pulling the PIRs slightly LOWER (Z = 20-25 mm) would put them in slightly cooler ambient air; tweak PIR_Z_CENTER in the macro if false-trigger rate ends up high in soak testing.
  3. Raised thermal baffle ring on the outside of the wall, around each lens hole.
    • Front slants (FL, FR): Ø28 OD × Ø24 ID × 5 mm tall hollow ring. Front slants are far from the back-wall fan exhaust plume.
    • Back slants (BL, BR): Ø28 OD × Ø24 ID × 8 mm tall hollow ring (bumped from 5 mm). Back slants are immediately adjacent to the 2× back-wall fan exhaust jets; the extra 3 mm of ring height shields the PIR Fresnel dome from the lateral edge of those plumes.
  4. Downward-tilted back-wall fan cutouts. Tilt the fan cutout normals 5–10° below horizontal so the exhaust jets angle downward away from the PIR FOV (which is nominally horizontal). Easy in the macro; cuts the fan-to-PIR thermal coupling further.
  5. Continuous-modulation PWM fan control (replaces older Phase 9 fan-status interlock). Bang-bang fan on/off control creates the air-mass transients that trigger PIRs. With both back-wall fans on continuous 25 kHz PWM modulation from the RPi 5 (always spinning, duty cycle slewed on temperature), there is no on/off transient — the Phase 9 software interlock is not needed by default. Keep it scoped as a fallback if a specific deployment shows residual PIR sensitivity.

Why baffle OD = 28 mm and not 30 mm: the back slants are only 29.36 mm long. A Ø30 baffle ring crosses the slant/back-wall corner and (in initial macro testing) fragmented the wall into two solids. Shrinking to Ø28 leaves ~0.68 mm margin on each side of the back slants. Same baffle OD used on all four slants for symmetry; only the height differs (5 mm front, 8 mm back).

E.4 Aesthetic placement

The 4-PIR-on-4-slants layout is automatically symmetric. The lens domes and baffle rings repeat at four corners, reading as "intentional design pair × 2" rather than as ad-hoc add-ons. No tradeoff against the LumiAI logo, rocker switches, or power plug — those features stay on the FRONT and BACK walls (between the slants), so they don't compete with the PIRs visually.

Suggested visual sanity checks once the fence is printed:

  • All 4 baffle rings should sit at the same Z height (the macro guarantees this — PIR_Z_CENTER is one constant).
  • Front slants are wider (55.7 mm) than back slants (29.4 mm). The PIR lens + baffle look proportionally smaller-relative-to-facet on the front, larger-relative-to-facet on the back. That asymmetry is a property of the base plate geometry, not a fix-able design issue.

E.6 ROS2 node spec — pir_sensor_node

Single Layer-4 rclpy node, reads all 4 GPIOs, publishes /presence/pir. Peer to hazard_health_node (§5).

Topic Type Direction Rate Behavior
/presence/pir std_msgs/Bool publisher 10 Hz true while ANY of the 4 OUT lines is high; false when all low
/presence/pir/raw_fl std_msgs/Bool publisher (debug) on change Front-left raw state
/presence/pir/raw_fr std_msgs/Bool publisher (debug) on change Front-right raw state
/presence/pir/raw_bl std_msgs/Bool publisher (debug) on change Back-left raw state
/presence/pir/raw_br std_msgs/Bool publisher (debug) on change Back-right raw state
/presence/pir/quadrant std_msgs/UInt8 publisher on change Bitmask: bit0=FL, bit1=FR, bit2=BL, bit3=BR

The /quadrant topic is the cheap-but-useful direction estimator: which slant fired tells the spotlight tracker which quadrant of the room to pre-aim toward, BEFORE the camera/face pipeline has a target lock.

Behavior:

  • Warm-up window: discard readings during the first 60 s after node startup (HC-SR501 spec: 30-60 s warm-up, take the conservative bound). All 4 PIRs warm up in parallel.
  • OR-logic: /presence/pir = OUT_FL OR OUT_FR OR OUT_BL OR OUT_BR.
  • Debounce: none in the node — PIR module already has the 3 s delay pot. State-machine consumer can add software debounce if needed.
  • Diagnostic publishing: if any OUT pin stays HIGH for > 5 minutes uninterrupted, publish a warning to /diagnostics with the offending sensor name (likely a stuck sensor or thermal-noise issue on that slant).

State-machine consumer (Phase 9): lumiai_state_machine_node subscribes to:

  • /presence/pir (this node) — binary "anyone in the room?"
  • /presence/pir/quadrant (this node) — direction prior for the tracker
  • /voice/wake_word (WonderEcho)
  • /tracker/active_target (Phase 8 spotlight tracker)

State transitions:

  • IDLE → SEARCHING on /presence/pir rising edge OR /voice/wake_word. On entry to SEARCHING: pre-aim spotlight toward centroid of active quadrants (cheap directional prior — saves ~1 s of search time).
  • SEARCHING → TRACKING on /tracker/active_target (target locked)
  • TRACKING → IDLE after configurable timeout with no target + no presence

Docker GPIO access (deployment flag, not block): GPIO from inside the container needs ONE of:

Option Description Trade-off
A Privileged container with --device=/dev/gpiomem Easy: just add the device mapping Looser security model
B pigpiod daemon on Pi host + pigpio Python in container Cleaner — daemon owns GPIO, container talks via socket Extra service to manage
C Host-side ROS node (runs as pi user, publishes over --network host DDS) Cleanest if Pi has ROS natively installed Requires Pi-native ROS install

Default plan: option C if Pi has ROS natively installed already, option B otherwise.

E.7 Future expansion

  • Drop-in mmWave radar replacement — same 32.5 × 24.5 PCB pocket and 3-wire pigtail accept a 24 GHz mmWave module (e.g., LD2410 or RCWL-0516, $15-20). The macro doesn't need to change; the radar PCB drops into the same pocket. Radar gives RANGE + ANGLE instead of just binary presence — useful for Phase 10 fine-direction tracker priors.
  • Replace ring with hood — if soak testing shows the 5 mm × Ø28 ring isn't enough to block ceiling-radiated IR, replace make_pir_baffle_ring() in the macro with a half-hemisphere or a forward-tilted hood. The boolean structure stays the same; just the cut/fuse shape changes.

E.8 Open items (PIR-specific)

  • Z-position tuning: if false-trigger rate is high during fan operation, lower PIR_Z_CENTER from 30 to 20-25 mm (puts PIRs in slightly cooler ambient air near the bay floor).
  • Snap retention lip: not in v1 of the macro. If the press-fit pocket alone doesn't hold the PCB reliably, add 4× 1 × 0.5 mm inward lips at pocket corners — same as the Plasticity-era spec.

F.3 Per-slant boolean operations (what the macro does, in order)

For each of the 4 slants:

  1. Subtract lens opening: Ø24 ROUND through the 1.0 mm skin — only the round dome pierces. (A through-SQUARE was tried first: its corners punched 4 visible holes past the baffle OD.)
  2. Subtract dome-base relief: 25 × 25 × 3.0 box from the inside — the FULL 3 mm square base is housed here (this is what fixes the original seating failure: the square base never has to pass the round hole).
  3. Subtract 2× Ø2.0 M2 screw through-holes at ±14 mm from the lens centre (28 c/c, width centreline).
  4. Fuse baffle ring: Ø28 OD × Ø24 ID × 2.5 mm (front) / 4.0 mm (back). (Cable slot and PCB pocket REMOVED 2026-07-31.)

F.4 PCB orientation in the pocket

HC-SR501 PCB is 32 × 24 mm. The 3-pin header (VCC / OUT / GND) is centred on a LONG (32 mm) edge, with its pins pointing out of the component side (the side facing the bay). The two trim pots sit along the opposite long edge, and the L/H jumper is at one corner on the pots side. The two M2 mounting holes are mid-way along the two SHORT edges, 28 mm apart. (Operator, 2026-10-23, from a vendor product photo; matches the Handson datasheet figure in Power_Bay §13.1.)

Component-side parts that stand tall: four electrolytic capacitors. One sits just inboard of the jumper corner. The other three sit at the three remaining board corners, and the vendor photo shows them leaning past the board outline on bent leads. Their exact positions and heights vary by lot, so measure one board from the 10-24 AliExpress order when it arrives (not Halo's: the operator is not disassembling Halo for this). Until then CAD assumes the photo's leaning corner caps.

Mounted orientation (per slant local frame):

  • PCB long axis (32 mm) → vertical in the fence (along Z); the M2 holes are at the top and bottom
  • PCB short axis (24 mm) → along the slant tangent
  • The header is therefore on a vertical SIDE edge, at mid-height (fence Z ≈ the PCB centre), and the Dupont plug sticks straight back into the bay. The pots face the other side. Rotating the board 180° in-plane only swaps which side the header is on; the dome and the hole pair are symmetric.

F.5 Mounting hardware

2× M2 screws + nuts per sensor (2026-07-31 — press-fit FAILED in the field). The PCB sits FLUSH against the fence's inner wall; M2 screws pass through the fence's Ø2.0 holes and the PCB's Ø2.0 holes (28 mm c/c on the width centreline, operator-measured); nuts on the PCB side. The dome's square base seats in the 25 × 25 × 2.5 relief with the round dome emerging through the baffle (apex ≈ 11 mm above the outer surface).
